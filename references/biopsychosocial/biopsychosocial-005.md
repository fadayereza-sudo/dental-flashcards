## The principle

In complex health conditions with multiple interacting causes, the clinician's task is not to find *the* cause but to identify *modifiable* causes — promising targets for intervention. Most common health conditions have no single cause of large enough effect for complete prevention or cure; a multimodal approach to multiple factors is usually required.

## What the book says

The book argues that in biopsychosocial systems, causal pathways criss-cross biological, psychological, and social domains. Rather than seeking a single root cause, clinicians should identify modifiable causes — factors that can actually be changed to improve outcomes. "Dysfunction attribution is in part — and somewhat paradoxically — shorthand for belief about promising possibilities for change. While 'dysfunction' and its cognates connote deficit, promising possibilities for change are opportunities." For chronic conditions, optimal outcomes typically come from a combination of high-quality healthcare, self-management, and social support (Ch 4, Sect. 4.3).

## How this applies in dental practice

A GDP rarely deals in single causes. Consider a patient with progressive periodontal disease:

- **Non-modifiable**: genetic susceptibility, age, past attachment loss (can't be undone)
- **Modifiable by the patient**: oral hygiene technique, smoking, diet, stress management
- **Modifiable by the dentist**: professional debridement, treatment of local factors (overhangs, ill-fitting margins), referral for specialist treatment
- **Modifiable by others**: GP managing diabetes (a bidirectional risk factor), social support for smoking cessation, employer allowing time off for dental appointments

The question is not "what caused this?" in the abstract, but "what can we change to improve the trajectory?" This reframes the conversation with the patient from blame ("you're not brushing properly") to opportunity ("here are the things we can work on together to slow this down").

The same principle applies to:
- **Recurrent caries**: modifiable factors include diet, fluoride exposure, dry mouth management, recall interval — not just "drill and fill again"
- **Tooth wear**: modifiable factors include reflux management, dietary acid, bruxism splint, stress reduction — not just "monitor and restore"
- **Failed restorations**: modifiable factors include occlusion, material choice, moisture control technique, patient habits — not just "redo it the same way"
