## Acute periapical abscess

Inflammation of periapical tissues, commonly caused by caries leading to loss of vitality (can happen after trauma).

### Signs and symptoms

- Pain (usually localised; triggered when biting or/and by touch; varying severity; likely to disturb sleep)
- Swelling (gingiva, face and/or neck may be swollen)
- Fever

### Management

**Is the tooth restorable?**
- Access the tooth to initiate abscess drainage through it. If drainage through the tooth is achieved, irrigate the canal with either sodium hypochlorite solution or chlorhexidine, then dry and dress with non-setting Calcium Hydroxide, place a cotton pellet over the canal entrance and seal with a temporary filling material such as GIC (ChemFil Rock contrast white)
- Carry out incision and drainage if there is an associated intra oral fluctuant soft tissue swelling as soon as possible
- Check and alleviate occlusion on affected tooth, if appropriate
- Prescribe analgesia (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) if attempts to drain the infection were not effective and/or to control post-operative pain

**Is the tooth unrestorable?**
- Extract the tooth and allow abscess drainage if possible
- Prescribe analgesia (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) to control post-operative pain

**For all cases**
- Do not prescribe antibiotics unless there are signs of spreading infection (e.g. facial/neck swelling), systemic infection (e.g. fever, aches, chills, weakness), or for an immunocompromised patient
