## Analgesic prescribing

### Odontogenic pain

Optimal analgesia in mild to moderate or post-operative pain:

**Paracetamol tablets, 500 mg**
- 1 g 4 times daily for 5 days or until pain subsides
- 40 tablets
- Do not to exceed the maximum dose of 4 g in 24 h

**Ibuprofen tablets, 200 mg**
- 400 mg 3 to 4 times daily for 5 days or until pain subsides
- 30 tablets
- Take with food
- Do not to exceed the maximum dose of 2.4 g in 24 h

In cases where paracetamol or ibuprofen alone is not effective, both drugs can be taken. If this is still not effective, consider codeine phosphate (refer to BNF).

Ibuprofen: avoid in those with hypersensitivity to any other NSAID and asthma. Avoid in pts with previous or active peptic ulcer disease.

### Oral ulceration

Recommend a topical analgesic (as below) and, if required, optimal analgesia (as above):

**Benzydamine mouthwash, 0.15%**
- Rinse or gargle using 15 ml every 1½ to 3 hours as required for 7 days or less
- One 300 ml bottle
- Can be diluted with an equal volume of water if stinging occurs

**Benzydamine oromucosal spray, 0.15%**
- 4 to 8 sprays onto affected area every 1½ to 3 hours
- One 30 ml bottle

**Lidocaine ointment, 5%**
- Rub sparingly and gently on affected areas before meals
- One 15 g tube
- Take care with the application to avoid producing anaesthesia of the pharynx before meals as this might lead to choking

Benzydamine: avoid in those with hypersensitivity to any other NSAID.
