## Post-extraction haemorrhage

Bleeding following tooth extraction.

### Signs and symptoms

- Bleeding can be immediate (failed haemostasis), within a few hours (reactionary) or within a week of an extraction (indicative of possible infection)

### Management

- Reconfirm if the patient is taking blood thinning medication (e.g. warfarin, rivaroxaban, aspirin, clopidogrel)
- If the patient has returned with prolonged bleeding it is sensible to check patient's pulse rate and blood pressure (they may need IV fluids), especially if bleeding is substantial or very prolonged
- Place a sterile gauze moistened with saline over the socket and ask pt to bite on it with pressure for 20 min
- If application of pressure does not work, administer local anaesthetic and find the source of the bleeding
- Apply a haemostatic dressing to the socket (e.g. Surgicel/Curacel) if appropriate
- Suture the soft tissue quite tightly to compress it
- Monitor until haemostasis is achieved. If it is not achieved, consider the need for other measures to control bleeding or the need to admit the patient to hospital for management
- Once bleeding stops, advise the patient to avoid drinking alcohol, smoking or exercising for 24 hours and to avoid disturbing the blood clot. Clear instructions and emergency contact details must be given
- Do not prescribe antibiotics unless there are signs of spreading infection, systemic infection, or for an immunocompromised patient. Also, bear in mind the interaction between some antibiotics and warfarin
