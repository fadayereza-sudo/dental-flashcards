## Pulpitis

Inflamed dental pulp, could be reversible or irreversible pulpitis.

### Signs and symptoms

**Reversible**
- Sharp pain of short duration (seconds) triggered by stimuli
- Analgesics relief pain
- No tenderness to percussion

**Irreversible**
- Pain of long duration (may last hours), may be dull and throbbing, spontaneous and may disturb sleep
- Analgesics do not relief pain effectively
- May be worsened by heat

### Management

**Reversible**
- Administer local anaesthetic
- Provide a temporary dressing (GIC, RMGIC, Kalzinol) and if appropriate arrange for future definitive restoration

**Irreversible**
- Administer local anaesthetic
- Provide endodontic access and pulp extirpation. If patient still experiences pain after good anaesthesia, access the tooth and dress it with a corticosteroid-antibiotic paste (e.g. Odontopaste) to reduce inflammation for extirpation at a later date. Seal the tooth with a cotton pellet and GIC or RMGIC
- or Extract the tooth
- If appropriate, recommend optimal analgesia
- Do not prescribe antibiotics
