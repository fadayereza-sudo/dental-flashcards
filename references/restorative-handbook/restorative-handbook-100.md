A wide range of emergencies exist, they can range from infections (bacterial, fungal or viral) to fractures (teeth and/or restorations), each requiring an individual response and treatment that is unique to the situation.

It is worth reminding that knowing the patient's medical history is of utmost importance when delivering safe dental care, therefore always check medical history! In particular for blood thinning agents (e.g. warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel, rivaroxaban) and bisphosphonates (e.g. alendronic acid (Fosamax, Fosavance), risedronate sodium (Actonel)) as these could have serious impact in extraction cases for example.

## Initial and general management

For every case where infection and/or swelling is present:

- Determine if the airway is compromised (patient unable to swallow, unable to push their tongue out of their mouth and/or difficulty breathing)
- If the airway is compromised, the patient needs immediate emergency care (if you are at the LDI call the tutor immediately otherwise call 999)
- If the airway is not compromised, consider the treatment suggested for each condition below
