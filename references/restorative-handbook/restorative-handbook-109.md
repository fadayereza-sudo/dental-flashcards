## Cracks, Fractures, mobile or lost Tooth Fragments and Restorations

Problems with teeth (fracture, chip, crack, split tooth) or restorations (fracture, chip, mobile/debonded or lost indirect restorations).

### Signs and symptoms

- Pain; Caries (primary or secondary); Part of tooth missing (missing restoration or fracture); Sharp tooth edges (may present with trauma to soft tissues); Mobile fragment or whole tooth; Gingival inflammation

### Management

- If it is known or suspected that the patient has inhaled a piece of tooth, filling or restoration, send the patient immediately to A&E for chest x-ray and further assessment/treatment if required
- If the patient has an open cavity or fractured tooth provide a temporary dressing and recommend optimal analgesia

**If tooth or filling fracture (tooth is considered restorable)**
- Smooth rough edges, remove any loose or displaced tooth fragments or defective fillings
- Carry out pulp therapy (direct pulp cap, pulpotomy or pulpectomy) if fracture involves the pulp
- Provide a temporary soothing dressing (Kalzinol) or RMGIC/GIC temporary

**If the tooth is unrestorable**
- Extract the tooth

**If pt has the indirect restoration (crowns, bridges and veneers)**
- Recement the restoration with a temporary cement, depending on the tooth beneath and the integrity of the restoration

**If patient lost or fractured the indirect restoration**
- Provide new temporary coverage (Directa temporary/permanent crowns for anterior teeth and premolars and or preformed aluminium temporary crowns for posterior teeth, all temporary crowns will require addition of temporary crown material (Integrity) to improve the fit prior to cementation), depending on whether a new restoration is needed
