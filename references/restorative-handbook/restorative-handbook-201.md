The major restrictions on single visit treatment relate to the time it takes, especially in inexperienced hands. Whilst it may be convenient to stop after the preparation phase, there are few real contra-indications to single-visit obturation, particularly for single-rooted teeth. It is wise not to obturate a canal which is persistently wet or one which demonstrates a symptomless periapical area as the facultative anaerobes which lurk there may put on an ATP burst, resulting in spectacular abscess formation. The ideal situation would be the vital or partially vital extirpation. The presence of a sinus is not always a contra-indication to single visit treatment if the canal can be sufficiently dried. Calcium hydroxide has also been shown to weaken the dentine if left for too long.
