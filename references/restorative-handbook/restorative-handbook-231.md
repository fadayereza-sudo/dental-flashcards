The endocrown is single-piece ceramic restoration characterized by a supragingival butt joint prep, retaining maximum enamel to improve adhesion. The prep also involves the pulpal chamber, but not the root canals, this ensures retention and stability to the final restoration which is produced by means of milled computer-aided techniques or by moulding ceramic materials under pressure.

This technique is particularly useful in cases where preparations for conventional crowns are likely to weaken further the remaining dental tissue. This approach allows restoration of posterior teeth without the use of posts and core reconstruction prior to crown preparation.

**Criteria for adequate preparation:**

Occlusal and marginal preparation:

- Adequate occlusal reduction of at least 2mm clearance in the axial direction
- Well defined recordable margins, ideally supragingival
- Butt joint margins, a flat surface of at least 2mm wide for most of its circumference with a band of peripheral enamel for bond optimization

Axial preparation (pulp chamber preparation):

- Use a cylindrical-conical diamond bur to make the coronal pulp chamber and endodontic access cavity continuous (total occlusal convergence of 7° taper)
- Minimal preparation in order to remove undercuts
- Do not touch the bur on the pulpal floor
- Pulpal chamber at least 3mm deep is required
