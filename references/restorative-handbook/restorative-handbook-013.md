## Special tests

### Pulp Sensitivity Tests

Electric pulp test (EPT), cold (ethyl chloride) and application of heat provide an indication of the pulpal health of the tooth, as a reaction to different stimuli.

### Electric pulp test (EPT)

Response may be influenced by the thickness of the enamel and dentine overlying the pulp. Interpretation of results should be carried out with care and the corresponding contra-lateral tooth should always be tested for reference. Overall, teeth with normal enamel and normal pulp tissue tend to give definite results, more or less painful, according to the individual. Teeth with inflamed pulp reacted to a much smaller amount of current than teeth with normal pulps. And devitalized teeth do not respond.

The actual number recorded does not tell us about the state of the tooth it either responds or it doesn't.

1. Apply lubricant to the tip of the EPT.
2. Apply to dry tooth on cervical area. Do not touch the soft tissues. Place interproximal plastic strip to isolate tooth if metal fillings are present to adjacent teeth.
3. Ask patient to hold on to the end of the EPT handle and instruct patient to let this go once stimuli is felt. Write down the tooth and number displayed on the EPT screen in the patient's notes.

### Ethyl chloride

1. Spray on a small cotton pellet.
2. Apply to dry tooth on cervical area and wait a few seconds for the patient's response. Write down if positive or negative response in the patient's notes.
