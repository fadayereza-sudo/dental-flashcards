## Composite placement - Posterior teeth

Irrespective of cavity design, composite increments should be of less than 2mm and light cured for at least 20s.

- For posterior teeth, initially transform the cavity into a class I, for example, if the cavity involves the approximal area(s), build up this wall and the marginal ridge first.
- Note that the height of the marginal ridge is usually similar to the adjacent tooth, so this is not over/under built.
- If the cavity is deep, consider using a bulk fill material (step-by-step use of SDR bulk fill is described next in this chapter).
- Reconstruct each cusp at a time (appreciate the fissure pattern and occlusal morphology below).
- Aim to achieve the right anatomy, this will save time in the finishing stage.
- Once finished adding the composite, remove the matrix band and wedge and inspect the marginal adaptation with BPE probe for voids and overhangs on free surfaces and with floss interproximally.

## Finishing (to get the right shape) and Polishing (for a glossy and smooth surface)

**Marginal and occlusal finishing:**
- Should be performed using fine diamond burs and/or white/green stones.
- Ensure overhangs are removed as these will act as plaque retentive factors.

**Accessible surfaces:**
- Use graded soflex discs, coarser discs for finishing and finer for polishing.

**Interproximal surfaces:**
- Interproximal strips as the name suggests should be used in these areas (grey/white for finishing, dark/light blue for polishing).

**Final polish:**
- Enhance kit (single use aluminum oxide impregnated resin discs, cups and points) is also available as well as diamond impregnated polishing paste.

These can easily cut through mucosa, good finger rest and good soft tissue retraction are essential to avoid lacerations.
