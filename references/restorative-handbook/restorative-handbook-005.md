## Medical history (MH)

Following history taking, the student should approach the supervising member of staff to discuss the main/relevant findings and receive permission to carry on with the examination as in some cases more details are required before proceeding.

Findings from a medical history are likely to influence the treatment plan, basically we are interested in the patient's:

- Ability to accept treatment safely
- If they had local anaesthetic in the past? Any problems?
- Any possible drug interactions
- Any limit on treatment sessions? Diabetics?

The medical history section of Salud should be completed and checked before commencing any treatment.

If you find yourself in a situation without a medical history form, a good way to ensure coverage of most important aspects of MH is to follow the ABCDEF... approach as described:

- **A – Allergy:** Any food, substances, medications; If yes, what happens when in contact with this?
- **B – Bleeding:** Do you bleed excessively if you are injured or wounded?
- **B – Breathing:** Do you suffer from any difficulty in breathing? Asthma? COPD?
- **C – Cardio-Vascular:** Any heart problems? High/low blood pressure? Is it controlled?
- **C – Cards:** Do you carry any warning cards?
- **D – Diabetes:** Type? Is it controlled?
- **D – Drugs:** Taking any medication? Including prescribed or over the counter? Taking steroids in last 2 years?
- **E – Epilepsy:** How frequent? / severe, what is normal for them
- **F – Faints?**
- **G – GIT:** Any stomach problems? Reflux / ulcers
- **H – Hospitalisation:** Recent hospitalization? Any reactions to sedation or GA?
- **I – Infections:** Any serious infectious disease like Hepatitis or HIV?
- **J – Jaundice:** Any liver problems?
- **K – Kidneys:** Any Kidney problems?
- **L – Likelihood of pregnancy:** Are you pregnant or are you on contraception?
