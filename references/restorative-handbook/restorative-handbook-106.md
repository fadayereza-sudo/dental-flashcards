## Acute periodontal conditions

The main acute periodontal conditions are:

**Necrotising gingivitis and Necrotising periodontitis** — severe gingival/periodontal inflammatory disease caused by Fusiform bacteria and Spirochetes, affecting more commonly immunocompromised patients.

**Periodontal abscess** — such abscesses develop in deep periodontal pockets where drainage is obstructed commonly due to pocket marginal closure leading to abscess formation.

**Perio-endo lesions** — combination of endodontic and periodontal lesions affecting a tooth.

### Signs and symptoms

**Necrotising gingivitis / periodontitis**
- Pain; Swelling; Bleeding; Halitosis; Ulcerated gingival tissue; Loss of attachment; Malaise; Fever

**Periodontal abscess**
- Pain; Presence of swelling on gingiva; Suppuration from the gingiva; Increased tooth mobility; Fever

**Perio-endo lesions**
- Pain; Deep pocketing to root apex; Swelling with or without suppuration

### Management for all acute periodontal conditions

- Scale teeth as effectively as possible under local anaesthesia
- Prescribe chemical plaque control (hydrogen peroxide and 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash)
- If patient does not have a GDP, arrange required periodontal treatment with a hygienist or dentist
- Recommend optimal analgesia
- Do not prescribe antibiotics unless there are signs of spreading infection, systemic infection, or for an immunocompromised patient if there are signs of necrotising disease

### Additional condition-specific management

**Necrotising gingivitis / periodontitis**
- Give OHI and if the patient is a smoker, smoking cessation advice
- Prescribe metronidazole (Metronidazole 200mg tablets, 1 tablet 3 times daily (every 8 hours) for 3-5 days (9 tablets))

**Periodontal abscess**
- Scaling and irrigating the periodontal pocket
- Extraction

**Perio-endo lesions**
- Root canal treatment / retreatment + NSPT (RSD)
- Extraction
