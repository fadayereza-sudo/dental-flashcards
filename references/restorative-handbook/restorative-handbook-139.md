## Composite placement - Anterior teeth

Irrespective of cavity design, composite increments should be of less than 2mm and light cured for at least 20s.

A diagnostic wax up is useful when reconstructing multiple teeth, when is planned to change OVD and/or occlusal guidance. If this technique is used, produce a putty index and use it to reproduce the palatal surface first, otherwise a free hand technique is described below.

The technique described is for Class IV restorations, for Class III and V refer to 'Pickard's Manual of Operative Dentistry'.

### Class IV technique

- Apply finger pressure over the matrix strip on the palatal/lingual surface and place a thin layer of composite to create a palatal shelf, it is common practice to over-build the incisal edge and then finish/polish down as necessary later.
- Ensure the composite is packed well around the cavity margins to prevent voids in this area.
- Before curing, visually check the incisal aspect to ensure the palatal shelf is at a good level (in a palatal-buccal direction), allowing enough space to reconstruct the tooth to the right anatomy. If too much finger pressure is applied, the resulting palatal shelf will slope buccaly, leading to a thinner and more fragile final restoration towards the incisal.
- Double check the palatal aspect was recreated to the desired anatomical shape, if deficient, add more composite until this is.
- Construct the approximal wall, pack the composite well to prevent voids and support the matrix strip palatally with your finger again to prevent excesses to be pushed in this direction.
- Construct the labial surface in increments until reaching the desired anatomy, you may also bring the matrix strip over the final layer and light cure as this will result in a very polished surface.
- With a coarse/medium-coarse disk reduce the incisal edge to the desired height.
- You may carry out initial finishing (to get the right shape) before removing rubber dam. Inspect the marginal adaptation for voids and overhangs with BPE probe on free surfaces and floss interproximally.
