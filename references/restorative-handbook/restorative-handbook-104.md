## Acute pericoronitis

Infection under the gingival tissue covering a partially erupted tooth (operculum).

### Signs and symptoms

- Pain (usually localised)
- Swelling (usually localised but can present facial swelling)
- Discomfort with swallowing
- Trismus
- Bad taste and/or halitosis
- Fever

### Management

**First episode**
- Administer local anaesthetic
- Try to flush/remove lodged debris around the partially erupted tooth and underneath the operculum, use USS and/or hand scaling instruments
- Irrigate under the operculum with chlorhexidine 0.2%*
- Advise the patient to rinse their mouth with 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash*
- Recommend analgesia

**Repeated episodes with the same tooth**
- Extract the tooth (or refer for removal)
- If the opposing tooth is causing trauma to the inflamed operculum, extract or adjust it, unless this is an important tooth for function

**For all cases**
- Do not prescribe antibiotics unless there are signs of spreading infection (e.g. limited mouth opening, facial swelling), systemic infection, or for an immunocompromised patient
- *NB: Be cautious with chlorhexidine in atopic patients
