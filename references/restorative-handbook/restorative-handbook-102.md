## Alveolar osteitis (dry socket)

Socket inflammation after tooth extraction, most commonly affecting molar extractions. Smokers are at higher risk of developing alveolar osteitis.

### Signs and symptoms

- Usually described as severe pain, tends to develop 1 to 2 days after extraction
- Bad taste and/or smell
- Loss or breakdown of clot. The socket usually appears empty or contains debris

### Management

- Exclude other potential causes such as a retained root (occasionally a radiograph is indicated)
- Most cases can be treated without the need for local anaesthetic - in severe cases LA may be needed
- Irrigate socket with saline (very rare but serious allergic reactions have been reported when using chlorhexidine for alveolar osteitis irrigation)
- Dress the socket with Alvogyl, warn patient that re-dressing may be required - to return if required
- Advise patient to carry out good oral hygiene and to not smoke
- Recommend optimal analgesia
- Do not prescribe antibiotics unless there are signs of spreading infection, systemic infection or for immunocompromised patients (e.g. patient undergoing chemotherapy treatment, who received organ transplant, HIV +)
