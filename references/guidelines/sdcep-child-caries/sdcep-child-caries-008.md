## The recommendation

Use the Hall Technique — sealing an advanced carious lesion in a primary molar under a preformed metal crown with no local anaesthesia, tooth preparation or caries removal — when a complete peripheral seal can be achieved and radiographs show unaffected dentine between the lesion and the pulp.

## Why

A carious lesion needs a sustained supply of fermentable carbohydrate and oxygen from saliva to stay active. A well-sealed PMC cuts that supply; the biofilm under the crown shifts to a less cariogenic composition and the lesion slows or arrests, even with soft dentine still in situ. That lets the tooth serve out its remaining time to exfoliation without pulp exposure, LA, or rotary instruments — so children, parents, and dentists all prefer it in primary-care trials. The technique only works if the seal holds, which is why radiographic confirmation of dentine between lesion and pulp matters: without that buffer, you seal in a lesion that will ride straight to the pulp.

## How this applies in practice

- Primary indication: advanced occlusal or proximal lesion in a primary molar, dentine visible between lesion and pulp on bitewing.
- Also useful when there is "no clear separation" radiographically but no signs or symptoms of pulpal pathology — seal and discuss the uncertain prognosis with the parent/carer.
- If contact is tight, place a separator 3–5 days before fitting so the PMC will seat cleanly.
- Protect the airway — use a sticking-plaster "handle" on the crown or gauze pack during fitting.
- Seat the crown filled with glass ionomer luting cement, let the child bite down to seat, remove excess cement, floss the contacts. Avoid cement reaching the tongue (bitter taste).
- Don't use Hall when you can instead achieve a good adhesive restoration with selective caries removal — that's still first choice when cooperation allows.
