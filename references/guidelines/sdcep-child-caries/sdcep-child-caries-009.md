## The recommendation

For advanced dentinal lesions in primary and permanent teeth, carry out selective caries removal — clear cavity walls to hard ("scratchy") dentine for bonding, but pulpally remove only enough carious tissue to give adequate restoration depth, leaving soft dentine over the pulp if needed to avoid exposure.

## Why

Complete caries removal down to hard dentine over the pulp is the traditional approach but carries a high rate of iatrogenic pulp exposure in deep lesions, which in a child typically means pulpotomy, extraction, or both. A well-sealed restoration deprives residual bacteria of substrate so the lesion arrests even under soft dentine — the marginal seal on hard wall dentine is what does the work, not elimination of every carious bacterium. Less tissue removed, less chance of pulp exposure, less need for LA in primary teeth, shorter appointment, and better tolerated by anxious children.

## How this applies in practice

- Access with a high-speed if needed; remove superficial caries with slow-speed or excavators.
- Walls: clear to hard, scratchy dentine (stained but hard dentine can stay unless aesthetic).
- Pulpal floor: shallow/moderate lesion — take down to leathery/firm dentine. Deep lesion — leave soft, deformable carious dentine over the pulp rather than expose.
- Restore with adhesive material (composite, resin-modified GI, compomer). Don't use conventional glass ionomer for multi-surface cavities in either dentition — failure rate is too high.
- Primary teeth: LA often not needed if sound dentine isn't being cut.
- Permanent teeth: LA usually needed; marginal seal is even more important because the restoration must last a lifetime.
- For primary multi-surface lesions where a complete peripheral seal is hard to achieve, Hall Technique is preferred over selective caries removal.
