## The recommendation

Give local anaesthesia for any cavity preparation that cuts sound dentine, for any endodontic treatment and for any extraction; infiltration is usually adequate for primary teeth, but mandibular primary molars may need an IDB with lignocaine. Avoid "sleight of hand" and use slow injection with topical and distraction.

## Why

Dentine in primary teeth is as sensitive as in permanent teeth — "they're just milk teeth" is a myth that produces avoidable pain and the "mistrust phobia" that follows. Children as young as 4 can accept LA if it is introduced transparently; hiding the syringe and promising "nothing" may get you through one appointment but seeds distrust that persists through adult life. Slow injection, taut tissue, suction, distraction, and a small-needle "cat's whisker" explanation work in trials to reduce acceptance problems. Primary molar pulp is close to the surface and the mandibular block is occasionally needed when infiltration won't reach through dense bone.

## How this applies in practice

- Rule of thumb for LA: any sound dentine being cut, any endodontics, any extraction, any pulpotomy.
- Primary incisors and first primary molars: buccal infiltration usually enough.
- Lower second primary molar: consider IDB with lignocaine if infiltration doesn't achieve anaesthesia.
- Techniques to reduce LA discomfort: topical, distraction (physical — pulling the lip taut — and verbal), suction, slow injection over at least 60 seconds for infiltration, intra-papillary before palatal.
- Offer to show the child the syringe and explain its role ("makes the tooth, not you, go to sleep") — transparency beats concealment long-term.
- Selective caries removal of primary teeth may not need LA (no sound dentine removed), but don't assume — check.
- Atraumatic restorative technique (ART) with sharp hand instruments may avoid LA in cooperative primary teeth and is particularly useful in anxious children.
