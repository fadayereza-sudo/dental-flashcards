## The recommendation

Silver diamine fluoride can be applied to cleansable cavitated lesions in primary teeth as part of non-restorative cavity control; its use for caries arrest is off-label in the UK, requires explicit consent about permanent dark staining of the lesion, and is contraindicated in specific medical and oral conditions.

## Why

Silver is bactericidal and disrupts the cariogenic biofilm; fluoride promotes remineralisation. Together they arrest lesions in primary teeth in low-certainty evidence, without the need for handpieces, local anaesthetic, or cooperation beyond sitting still for a minute. The cost is that SDF permanently stains carious tooth tissue dark brown/black — a visible, permanent mark on the front teeth is the side effect a parent/carer can resent years later if they weren't warned. SDF is licensed in the UK only for dentine hypersensitivity, so using it for caries is off-label — the clinician must be able to justify and feel competent in its use, and document that off-label status was discussed.

## How this applies in practice

- Contraindications to screen for: irreversible pulpitis, abscess/sinus/radiographic periradicular pathology, infection/pain from pulpal origin, allergy to silver or heavy metals, active ulceration/mucositis/stomatitis, pregnancy or breastfeeding, thyroid disease or on thyroid medication (if using potassium iodide).
- Consent must cover: off-label use, permanent discolouration of the carious tissue, temporary soft-tissue staining (1–3 weeks), possible chemical burn of gingiva.
- Protect soft tissues with petroleum jelly, isolate, dry the lesion, apply with a single-use applicator, wait at least 1 minute, keep isolated up to 3 minutes, blot dry.
- Potassium iodide can be offered to reduce staining but may persist anyway and may reduce SDF efficacy — worth discussing but not a guarantee.
- Review at 2–4 weeks: a hard darkened lesion means arrest; reapply 6-monthly if ongoing arrest is the plan.
- If SDF is being used on advanced lesions in a child, it's acceptable to also apply to initial lesions in that child with consent.
