## The recommendation

Consider a pulpotomy for a primary molar with pulpitis with irreversible symptoms but a vital pulp, or with an advanced lesion where there is no clear band of dentine between lesion and pulp radiographically — remove coronal pulp tissue, achieve haemostasis with ferric sulphate, place calcium silicate or zinc oxide-eugenol, and fit a PMC at the same appointment.

## Why

Conventional root canal endodontics is contraindicated in primary teeth because the roots physiologically resorb, so removing irreversibly inflamed pulp is limited to the coronal chamber; the radicular pulp is left, treated with a biomaterial, and coronally sealed. The immediate PMC is not cosmetic — evidence shows that placing the crown at the same visit improves prognosis compared with a temporary-then-crown approach, because the seal has to be complete from day one or the tooth reinfects. Ferric sulphate is used for haemostasis; formocresol is no longer recommended due to safety concerns.

## How this applies in practice

- Indication: primary molar with irreversible pulpitis (vital pulp) or advanced lesion with no clear dentine band to the pulp radiographically.
- Contraindications: tooth close to exfoliation, unrestorable, pre-cooperative child, immunocompromised child, or multiple teeth needing pulp therapy (extract instead).
- Technique: LA, rubber dam, large access cavity, deroof entire pulp chamber, remove contents, irrigate with NaOCl or sterile saline (not compressed air — surgical emphysema risk).
- Haemostasis: cotton wool pellet dampened in ferric sulphate, child bites on cotton roll for ~2 minutes. If bleeding won't stop or any canal is necrotic — pulpectomy or extract.
- Place calcium silicate cement (MTA) or ZOE on pulp stumps; fill cavity with ZOE; fit PMC same visit.
- Be aware of primary molar anatomy: pulp horns very high, pulpal floor very thin — perforation is easy.
- Annual radiographic review.
