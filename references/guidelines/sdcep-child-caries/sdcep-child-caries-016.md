## The recommendation

Assign recall intervals to children based on caries risk: 3-monthly for children receiving Enhanced Prevention, 6-monthly for Standard Prevention, and only exceptionally up to 12 months for an older, low-risk child where this can be justified and documented.

## Why

NICE permits recall intervals of 3 to 12 months for children, but in much of the UK where childhood caries incidence is high, 12 months is wrong for most children — the interval lets lesions develop, progress, and cavitate before the next review. Three-monthly recall for Enhanced Prevention exists precisely so the four-per-year varnish schedule fits into routine visits, and so hands-on brushing instruction, diet review, and plaque monitoring actually happen rather than being deferred. The recall interval is the scaffolding that makes the preventive protocol deliverable.

## How this applies in practice

- Increased-risk child: 3-monthly recall, each visit delivering an Enhanced Prevention package (varnish, hands-on brushing, diet review, plaque score, fissure sealant check).
- Standard-risk child: 6-monthly recall with Standard Prevention.
- Exceptional 12-monthly intervals: older child, clearly low risk, orally healthy, reliable attendance, family compliant — document why.
- Reassess caries risk at every recall; a child can move between categories as circumstances change (diet, hygiene, family, new caries).
- If caries is not being controlled despite Enhanced Prevention, consider dental neglect and the need for additional support.
- Consider occlusal development, pending care, and fissure sealant status as additional inputs alongside caries risk.
