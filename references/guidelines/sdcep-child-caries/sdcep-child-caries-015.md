## The recommendation

Take bitewing radiographs at 6–12 month intervals for children at increased caries risk, and every 2 years for all other children, starting from age 4 when cooperation allows; record the justification in the notes.

## Why

Proximal caries in primary molars is hidden behind broad contact points and is routinely missed on visual exam; by the time the marginal ridge breaks down, the lesion is usually into dentine. Risk-based bitewing intervals are tuned to that reality: shorter intervals in higher-risk children let you catch lesions while they are still sealable or selectively removable, before they reach the pulp and force pulpotomy or extraction. The 6–12 month lower bound is driven by caries-progression rates in children at risk; the 2-year upper bound reflects low population radiation dose balanced against low baseline risk. The radiation risk is very low — about a few days of background — and this is worth explaining to worried parents.

## How this applies in practice

- Increased-risk child: bitewings every 6–12 months; adjust toward 6 if new lesions are appearing.
- Standard-risk child: bitewings every 2 years.
- Use size 0 films and film holders with adhesive tabs for small mouths; most young children tolerate this well.
- If a proximal enamel-only lesion is found, consider orthodontic separators at a return visit to check for cavitation before committing to restoration.
- Document the reason if you do not take bitewings at the expected interval (e.g. well-spaced dentition, pre-cooperative child).
- Be aware of mimics: the Cusp of Carabelli can produce a triangular radiolucency on mesial upper Es and upper 6s that looks like proximal caries but is more occlusally situated with no enamel radiolucency.
- Dental panoramic radiography only for a clear clinical indication (e.g. pre-GA assessment) — higher skin dose.
