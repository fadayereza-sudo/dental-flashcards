## The recommendation

For children at increased caries risk, consider recommending 1,350–1,500 ppm fluoride toothpaste up to age 10, and prescribing 2,800 ppm fluoride toothpaste for children aged 10 to 16 for a limited period with regular review.

## Why

Standard DBOH guidance lets under-7s at average risk use a minimum 1,000 ppm paste. SDCEP sharpens that for the higher-risk group — at the same ages where standard-risk children get 1,000 ppm, higher-risk children move up to the 1,350–1,500 ppm band (still over-the-counter) and get earlier access to the caries-preventive effect of the higher topical dose. From age 10 a prescription-strength 2,800 ppm option opens up; this is a step change in topical fluoride and is reserved for patients who need it and will be reviewed, not used long-term by default.

## How this applies in practice

- Increased-risk child under 10: 1,350–1,500 ppm paste at the age-appropriate amount (smear under 3, pea 3–6).
- Increased-risk child 10–16: prescribe 2,800 ppm fluoride toothpaste, specify "for a limited period" on the script, and review at each recall.
- 2,800 ppm is prescription-only in the UK — check the SDCEP Drug Prescribing for Dentistry guidance for dose and duration.
- Don't slip into 2,800 ppm under 10 — the evidence base and licensing are for 10 and over.
- Combine the paste change with hands-on brushing instruction at every recall, not a one-off demonstration.
