## The recommendation

When preparing multi-surface cavities in primary or permanent teeth, protect the adjacent proximal surface — place a matrix band around the adjacent tooth, use wedges or separators, and prepare enamel margins with hand instruments where possible.

## Why

Iatrogenic damage to the adjacent proximal surface has been reported in up to 60% of multi-surface preparations, and that damaged enamel is significantly more likely to develop caries later. The damage is usually invisible at the time and becomes a problem two recalls later when the radiograph shows a new lesion on the unrestored neighbour. For primary multi-surface caries involving the distal of an E, the next tooth is the mesial of the first permanent molar — a 60-year asset that a careless bur in a 5-year-old's mouth can compromise permanently. Hand instruments (gingival margin trimmers, sharp spoon excavators) on proximal margins, or a matrix band on the adjacent tooth before rotary instrumentation, are specific mechanical protections against this.

## How this applies in practice

- Access the cavity with a high-speed leaving the proximal wall of enamel intact to protect the neighbour.
- Remove caries with a slow-speed.
- Finish proximal margins with gingival margin trimmers or a sharp spoon excavator — not the high-speed.
- Place a matrix band around the adjacent tooth before any rotary approach to the proximal wall when that's the safest option.
- For advanced distal caries on an E with a first permanent molar neighbour, prefer the Hall Technique — no rotary, no risk.
- Wedges during restoration placement also protect the adjacent surface and produce a cleaner margin.
- Document the technique used; if you do damage the neighbour, note it and monitor at the next radiograph rather than hoping it heals.
