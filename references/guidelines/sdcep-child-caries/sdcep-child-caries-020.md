## The recommendation

Suspect dental neglect when a child has obvious untreated dental disease, evidence that the disease has significantly impacted wellbeing, and a pattern of failure to obtain care (irregular attendance, repeated missed appointments, incomplete treatment, returning in pain, or repeated GA for extractions) after the family has been made aware and offered treatment.

## Why

All three elements matter: caries alone isn't neglect (many children have caries), and missed appointments alone aren't neglect (life happens). Neglect is the persistent combination — disease the family has been told about, evidence of impact on the child's life, and a pattern of not acting on it. The GDC and national child protection guidance make dental teams responsible for recognising this pattern, not just treating teeth. The British Society of Paediatric Dentistry recommends a tiered response: preventive single-agency, preventive multi-agency, then formal child protection referral. Missed appointments when a child is in pain or has active infection are especially significant — the "Was Not Brought" toolkit reframes these from the child's perspective.

## How this applies in practice

- Three features to document together: obvious disease, significant impact (pain, eating, sleeping, school attendance), repeated failure to obtain care.
- Screen with four questions: delay seeking advice without explanation? history changes or doesn't match the findings? unexplained injuries? concerning parent-child interaction?
- Any "yes" — discuss with a colleague and follow local child protection procedure.
- Tiered response: start with preventive dental team intervention; escalate to multi-agency (health visitor, school nurse, social work) if concerns continue; formal referral if at child protection threshold.
- Information sharing with consent where possible; without consent where there is a child protection concern (this is always legally permissible).
- Keep local safeguarding contacts up to date in the practice — this is the contact most likely to be out of date when you urgently need it.
- "Was Not Brought" framing: a child doesn't fail to attend; adults fail to bring them. Track non-attendance as a safeguarding signal, not just an admin problem.
