## The recommendation

For initial (enamel-only) carious lesions, carry out site-specific prevention: show the lesion to the child and parent/carer, demonstrate effective brushing of the site, give dietary advice, apply fluoride varnish to the lesion four times per year, and review the lesion in three months.

## Why

Initial lesions are in the window where remineralisation can still outrun demineralisation. Success depends entirely on the parent/carer and child changing their behaviour — brushing that specific site, reducing sugar frequency, absorbing the varnish dose — so showing them the lesion and making them explicit partners in the plan is load-bearing, not a nicety. The three-month review is the checkpoint: if the lesion isn't arresting, you move to an alternative (sealing, SDF, or selective removal) rather than letting an unaddressed active lesion progress quietly through a 12-month recall.

## How this applies in practice

- Use this approach for primary and permanent teeth with initial occlusal, proximal or anterior white-spot lesions, and for arrested lesions that need maintenance.
- Four varnish applications a year on the lesion specifically — this is identical to the Enhanced Prevention schedule even if the child is otherwise Standard.
- Record the site photographically or by radiograph at baseline so you can actually judge progression at review.
- Assess plaque directly on the lesion at each visit; if the family can't keep it plaque-free, switch strategy.
- If the three-month review shows the lesion is still active, don't repeat the same prevention indefinitely — escalate to sealing/restoration.
