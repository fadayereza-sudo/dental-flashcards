## The recommendation

Avoid dental extractions with local anaesthesia on a child's first visit whenever possible; for a symptomatic tooth, dress with corticosteroid-antibiotic paste and a temporary dressing to buy time, and build the child's cooperation before attempting extraction.

## Why

A child's first experience of dentistry shapes every future interaction. An emergency extraction with LA on day one — especially in a distressed, unrehearsed child — is a reliable way to produce lasting dental anxiety and avoidance, which then becomes the real clinical problem for decades. The corticosteroid-antibiotic dressing controls the symptoms quickly enough to let you plan the extraction properly on a later visit, once the child has met the team, tolerated examination, and worked up to LA through behaviour management. There is also the risk that once out of pain, the family doesn't return — so the plan has to be robust to that possibility too.

## How this applies in practice

- Acute pulpitis with irreversible symptoms on first visit: gross debris out of cavity (no LA if possible), corticosteroid-antibiotic paste under temporary dressing, analgesia, bring back after cooperation built.
- Infected tooth that must come out: local measures first (drainage via cavity, antibiotics only if spreading or systemic involvement), then plan the extraction.
- Build cooperation across appointments using tell-show-do, enhancing control, distraction — the techniques from Helping the family.
- If the child truly can't cope and the tooth must come out, refer for sedation or GA rather than forcing it in the chair.
- Document the plan and the reason for deferring — "extracted on first visit" without that reasoning is hard to defend if anxiety is the long-term outcome.
- Never force dental treatment on an unwilling child however strong the imperative felt by you or the parent.
