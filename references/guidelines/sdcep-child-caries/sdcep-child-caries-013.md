## The recommendation

For a first permanent molar of poor prognosis (advanced caries, MIH, or pulpal/apical pathology) and an unerupted second permanent molar, consider interceptive extraction between 8 and 10 years old so the second molar can erupt into the space; obtain a specialist paediatric or orthodontic opinion first where possible.

## Why

Extracting a first permanent molar at 8–10 years old — when the second molar is still unerupted and its bifurcation is just forming — allows the second molar to drift mesially into an acceptable contact with the premolar, sometimes producing a tidy, caries-free dentition without orthodontic appliances. Extract earlier and the second molar may over-erupt or migrate unpredictably; extract later and it has already settled in its normal position so a space remains. The 8–10 window is most reliable for the lower arch; the upper is more forgiving. Getting the timing wrong has lifetime occlusal consequences, so the decision is worth a specialist opinion.

## How this applies in practice

- Radiographic prerequisites for a good interceptive outcome: second premolars and third molars visibly present, distal angulation of the second molar, bifurcation forming, class I relationship, little or no buccal-segment crowding.
- Get a specialist opinion before extraction if: any permanent teeth are missing, the child has a malocclusion (especially class II div 2 or class III), or generalised developmental defects.
- For an emergency extraction of a symptomatic first molar, extract only the affected tooth first, then refer — don't do bilateral/balancing extractions on the fly.
- Balancing extractions (contralateral) are not routinely recommended; compensating extractions (opposing arch) only if there's a clear occlusal reason.
- To bridge the wait until optimal age, temporise with a PMC (no prep, no caries removal) after separators — adhesive materials fail on MIH/carious enamel.
- For MIH molars that are sensitive, use glass ionomer as the sealant; if enamel breakdown is severe, plan toward extraction at 8–10.
