## The recommendation

Before referring a child for treatment under sedation or general anaesthesia, first relieve pain, provide prevention and attempt caries treatment with behavioural management and local anaesthesia; sedation or GA is not a management option for acute dental pain.

## Why

Relieving acute pain is the referring dentist's responsibility — waiting lists for sedation and GA are measured in weeks to months, so punting an acute pulpitis to GA leaves the child in pain. General anaesthesia also carries mortality risk (very low at <1:100,000 for healthy elective procedures but not zero) and is generally recommended only after inhalation sedation has at least been considered. Treating under GA also means no chance to build cooperation for future appointments, so the treatment-induced anxiety cycle continues unless behaviour work is done first. The decision to accept a sedation or GA referral lies with the specialist centre, so don't promise the family either will happen.

## How this applies in practice

- First, relieve pain: drainage, dressing, corticosteroid-antibiotic paste, analgesia, antibiotics only if systemic involvement or spreading infection.
- Second, establish prevention — the referral centre will not start treatment until caries risk is under control.
- Third, attempt treatment with behaviour management + LA. Most children can complete planned care this way.
- Inhalation sedation: useful from age 5+; limited benefit under school age. IV sedation for teenagers.
- GA indications: very young (under 5 where comprehension limits cooperation), young school-age children needing extensive treatment, interceptive extraction of all four first permanent molars around age 9–10, or where less invasive options have failed or are unsuitable.
- Include radiographs, anxiety assessment, treatment attempted, LA use, previous sedation/GA, antibiotic history in the referral letter.
- Warn the family the first visit to the referral centre is usually assessment only; do not promise sedation/GA will be provided.
- Continue routine preventive care for the child during the wait, and resume it immediately after treatment.
