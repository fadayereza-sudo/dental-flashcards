## The recommendation

For all children, decide whether to place fissure sealants on permanent molars through shared decision-making with the child and parent/carer, guided by individual caries risk, clinical factors and cooperation; resin-based sealants are first choice.

## Why

The blanket DBOH rule "seal every child" has been softened by SDCEP into a conditional recommendation because the certainty-of-evidence for added benefit on top of optimally applied varnish is moderate-to-very-low. For a standard-risk child whose varnish is applied well and whose molars have shallow, accessible fissures, varnish alone may be enough. For a higher-risk child, a child with deep or oddly angled fissures, or a child already showing caries in other molars, the balance tips decisively toward sealing. Resin sealants give the best retention when moisture control is achievable; glass ionomer is a temporary fallback when it isn't.

## How this applies in practice

- Seal permanent molars as soon as possible after eruption if sealant is decided upon — don't wait for full emergence if moisture can be controlled.
- Always seal the buccal pits of lower first permanent molars and the palatal fissures of upper first permanent molars when sealing.
- For increased-risk children, also consider sealing palatal pits on upper lateral permanent incisors and occlusal/palatal surfaces of Ds and Es.
- Check every sealant at every recall visit — visually for wear, probe for integrity — and top up worn sealants.
- A leaky sealant (that the probe lifts) must be removed and replaced, not topped over.
- If sealant placement isn't feasible today (pre-cooperative child, partial eruption), apply a glass ionomer sealant as a temporary measure and ensure varnish is optimal.
