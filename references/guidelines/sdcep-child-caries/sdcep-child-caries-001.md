## The recommendation

Regard any child who lives in an area of relative disadvantage, or who has any decayed, missing (due to caries) or filled teeth, as at increased risk of developing caries. Reassess risk at every visit.

## Why

Every child is at some risk, so every child gets Standard Prevention. The point of risk assessment is to identify the subset who need Enhanced Prevention — more varnish applications, shorter recall, tighter radiograph intervals, hands-on brushing instruction at every visit. The two most reliable predictors in the evidence base are previous caries experience and area-based deprivation (postcode in a SIMD Quintile 1–3 in Scotland). Other factors — diet, plaque, fluoride exposure, saliva, medical history — contribute to clinical judgement but none of the published risk-assessment tools has been shown clearly superior. Risk is dynamic, so a child who starts Standard can move to Enhanced at any recall.

## How this applies in practice

- Two automatic flags for increased risk: decayed/missing/filled teeth, or a home postcode in a disadvantaged area. Either one triggers Enhanced Prevention.
- On top of those, use clinical judgement on diet (snacking frequency, sugary drinks, sugary medication), fluoride exposure, plaque, saliva, and medical history.
- The risk assessment drives three things: radiograph interval (6–12 months vs 2 years), varnish frequency (4x vs 2x per year), and recall interval (3 months vs 6 months).
- Document the risk category at every recall — it changes.
- There is an association between caries and obesity but no causal link, so obesity alone is not a formal risk trigger.
