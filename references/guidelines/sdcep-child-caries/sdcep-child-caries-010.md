## The recommendation

For a primary tooth with advanced caries where operative treatment is not feasible, or for unrestorable/arrested primary teeth with no signs of infection, use non-restorative cavity control: make the lesion cleansable, demonstrate brushing, apply fluoride varnish four times a year, consider silver diamine fluoride, and review in three months.

## Why

Some children cannot tolerate any operative intervention and some lesions are beyond restoration but not yet causing symptoms. NRCC accepts the tooth will carry open carious tissue but aims to render that tissue brushable and remineralisable rather than a reservoir for bacteria. The enamel overhangs get removed so the toothbrush can reach the base of the lesion; varnish and SDF tip the chemistry toward arrest; behaviour change from the family does the daily work. Success is entirely behaviour-dependent, so the child and parent/carer need to be explicit partners — if plaque keeps building on the lesion, the approach has failed and you must escalate.

## How this applies in practice

- Use for: arrested caries, teeth close to exfoliation, unrestorable teeth without infection, advanced lesions when alternatives aren't feasible.
- Make the lesion cleansable — remove undermined enamel adjacent to the cavity with a slow-speed or hand instruments so the brush bristles can reach the dentine.
- Four varnish applications per year to the specific lesion, plus consider SDF, plus continue Enhanced Prevention background.
- Review at three months — active lesion not arresting means escalate (restoration if restorable, extraction if not).
- Take extreme care with rotary instruments near the mesial of the first permanent molar when making the distal of an E cleansable — this is a common iatrogenic trap.
- If at any stage the lesion becomes infected, NRCC is no longer on the table — extract.
