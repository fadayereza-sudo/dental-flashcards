## The recommendation

Use glass ionomer as a temporary sealant material when the child is pre-cooperative, when moisture control is inadequate for resin, or when sealing a partially erupted tooth — applying it using the "press finger" technique.

## Why

Resin sealant retention depends on a dry, etched enamel surface; without moisture control the resin peels in weeks, leaving caries to progress under a false sense of security. Glass ionomer bonds chemically to enamel even in a damp field, making it usable on partially erupted molars or in a struggling 3-year-old. The trade-off is much poorer long-term retention, so glass ionomer buys time until the tooth fully erupts or the child can tolerate a resin procedure — it isn't a permanent sealant in a low-risk mouth.

## How this applies in practice

- Press finger technique: glass ionomer on one finger tip, petroleum jelly on the adjacent finger. Wipe tooth with a cotton roll, press GI finger firmly onto occlusal for two minutes, then rapidly swap fingers so the petroleum jelly covers the GI before saliva contacts it.
- Common scenario: the partially erupted lower 6 with a deep distal fissure in a 7-year-old — seal with GI now, re-do in resin when fully erupted.
- Document the GI sealant as temporary so it's reviewed — not forgotten for two years.
- If the molars are hypomineralised (MIH) and sensitive, GI sealant is also preferred over resin because it doesn't require the same etch/bond and handles the atypical enamel better.
