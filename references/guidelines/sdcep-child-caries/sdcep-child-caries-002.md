## The recommendation

Apply sodium fluoride varnish (5%, 22,600 ppm) twice a year to all children aged 2 years and over as Standard Prevention; increase to four times a year for children at increased caries risk.

## Why

Twice-yearly varnish is the baseline because that is where the Cochrane evidence of a 37–43% caries-increment reduction sits. Doubling the frequency to quarterly in higher-risk children delivers proportionally more topical fluoride to teeth that are actively demineralising faster than they remineralise. This is the single biggest SDCEP-specific addition to the general DBOH baseline, and it is where the assessment pays off — marking a child as "increased risk" has to lead to the 4x schedule, otherwise the label is cosmetic.

## How this applies in practice

- Increased-risk child: 4 applications per year. Two of those may come from Childsmile at school/nursery; the dentist tops up the rest to reach 4 total, not 4 on top of Childsmile.
- If residual varnish is visible or the child has had varnish applied in the last 24 hours (e.g. a Childsmile session that morning), defer to the next visit.
- Varnish can be given up to 4 times per year without concern even in Standard Prevention children if the schedules happen to align that way.
- Bundle varnish with another appointment — making a child attend just for varnish increases travel/environmental burden and is discouraged.
- If fissure sealants can't be placed (pre-cooperative child, partial eruption), keeping varnish at the optimal frequency matters even more.
