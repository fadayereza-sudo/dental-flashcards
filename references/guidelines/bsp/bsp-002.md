## The recommendation

Identify and document diabetic status (and HbA1c where available) for every periodontitis patient; the target HbA1c is 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) or lower. If a patient presents with multiple lateral periodontal abscesses, suspect undiagnosed diabetes and liaise with their GP for a screen.

## Why

Poorly controlled diabetes amplifies periodontal destruction and impairs wound healing — both directly via hyperglycaemia-driven inflammation and indirectly via slower tissue repair. Because HbA1c reflects average glucose over the previous 2–3 months, it is a more useful index of risk than a single fingerprick reading, but control still varies over time so a "good" HbA1c does not guarantee a stable response to therapy. Multiple lateral periodontal abscesses are a recognised oral marker of undiagnosed or poorly controlled diabetes — a presentation a GDP can spot before the patient ever sees a doctor.

## How this applies in practice

- Ask about diabetes type, current HbA1c and recent control at periodontal assessment; request the most recent HbA1c result from the managing physician if appropriate.
- Warn poorly controlled diabetic patients that their healing response to scaling will be slower and the result less predictable, and consider deferring elective complex perio work until control improves.
- Multiple lateral periodontal abscesses in an apparently healthy patient = referral to GP for diabetes screen, not just antibiotics.
- For severe periodontitis patients reporting good general health, a recommendation to ask their GP for a diabetes screen can be appropriate — perio breakdown can be the first sign.
