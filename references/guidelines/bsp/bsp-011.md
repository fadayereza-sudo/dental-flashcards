## The recommendation

Treat probing depth, clinical attachment loss (CAL = pocket depth + recession) and bleeding on probing as separate measurements that each answer a different question. Pockets ≥4 mm are beyond what brushing and interdental cleaning can clean and should be considered for active therapy. Bleeding on probing from the base of the pocket reflects active periodontitis; bleeding from the gingival margin reflects gingivitis; absence of BOP from the pocket base predicts stability — except in smokers where the absence of bleeding is misleading.

## Why

Probing depth tells you how deep below the gingival margin the pocket goes — but the gingival margin can be sitting above the CEJ (recession) or below it (false pocket from overgrowth), so depth alone misrepresents how much support is lost. CAL combines pocket depth and recession to give the total destruction relative to the CEJ — a stable long-term measure. BOP is a measure of inflammation, not active destruction in the moment. In smokers, reduced gingival blood flow suppresses BOP, so a smoker's apparently dry chart is not reassurance — the periodontium can be inflamed but not visibly bleeding.

## How this applies in practice

- Measure six sites per tooth on a detailed chart but record only depths ≥4 mm; always record BOP alongside depths.
- Add recession to pocket depth to get true CAL when comparing visits — especially when monitoring response to treatment over months.
- 4 mm pocket = active treatment threshold (the patient cannot keep that site clean themselves).
- BOP at the gingival margin = OHI focus. BOP from the pocket base = needs subgingival debridement.
- A non-bleeding chart in a smoker is not the same as a non-bleeding chart in a non-smoker — interpret with caution and rely more on probing depth and radiographs.
- 2 mm change between visits sits within measurement error for most clinicians — flag a real change only when it is consistent and at a strategic site.
