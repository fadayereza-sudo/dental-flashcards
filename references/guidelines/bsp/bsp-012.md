## The recommendation

Treat behaviour change as the active ingredient of periodontal therapy, not as adjunctive advice. Use open questions, deliver instructions in small doses across visits, use reflective listening to surface barriers, guide rather than direct, and follow the SDCEP TIPPS framework — Talk, Instruct, Practise (in the surgery), Plan, Support — at every appointment. Position the patient upright at eye level for these conversations.

## Why

Lifestyle change comes from within the patient; you cannot install motivation by lecturing. Most plaque control failure is not lack of knowledge but lack of confidence and lack of a workable daily plan, so the highest-yield intervention is rehearsing the technique with the patient and helping them slot it into their day. Repeating instructions multiple times does not increase motivation — it can offend and reduce it, because the natural response to force is resistance. Learning a skill takes minutes; changing a habit takes weeks to months, which is why support across multiple visits is what actually shifts behaviour.

## How this applies in practice

- Open questions: "Tell me how you got on with the interdental brushes" beats "Did you use them every day?".
- Confidence is the strongest predictor of plaque-control success — get the patient to brush in the surgery and check they can do it before sending them home.
- Hand over only one or two new techniques per visit; bolt on more once the basics are working.
- Replace "you must use floss" with "what's been your experience with floss in the past?" — same point, different power dynamic.
- Tailor the *when*: a patient too tired at night may do better focusing on a different time of day than failing the recommended routine.
- Plaque charts at every visit make progress (or its absence) visible to both clinician and patient — they convert "try harder" into a measurable, jointly owned goal.
- Periodontal care is an ongoing conversation, not a procedure delivered at one appointment.
