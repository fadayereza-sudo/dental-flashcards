## The recommendation

Consider periodontal surgery only after a thorough course of non-surgical therapy in a highly motivated patient with optimal plaque control and risk-factor control, where residual increased pockets and bleeding on probing remain. Most patients requiring periodontal surgery should be referred to a specialist unless you have the relevant expertise.

## Why

Surgery delivers two main gains: physical access for debridement of root surfaces hidden in deep pockets or complex furcations, and (in selected cases) regeneration of lost tissue using membranes, enamel matrix proteins like Emdogain, or graft materials. But surgical healing depends on a stable, low-inflammation field — if the patient cannot maintain plaque control, the surgical site is just another site that will reinflame and break down. Smokers heal poorly, so surgery in active smokers is generally avoided. Selecting only motivated, low-risk patients with proven engagement protects both the patient and the procedure from predictable failure.

## How this applies in practice

- Reassess at around 12 weeks after non-surgical therapy. Residual pockets ≥6 mm with BOP in a compliant non-smoker = consider surgical referral. Same finding in a smoker or non-engaged patient = re-instrument and reinforce behaviour change first.
- Open-flap debridement: for visualisation and cleaning of complex root anatomy.
- Regenerative procedures (GTR membranes, Emdogain, bone grafts): indicated mainly for deep narrow vertical infrabony defects.
- Crown lengthening: for restorability (subgingival caries margins, fracture, severe wear) or aesthetics (delayed passive eruption); requires bone removal to achieve a stable result if hard tissue contributes to the problem.
- Mucogingival surgery for recession: aesthetic indication primarily; reliable only for Miller Class I and II defects (no interdental bone loss).
- Refer rather than attempt unfamiliar procedures — surgical recurrence on a botched flap is harder to retreat than the original disease.
