## The recommendation

Build the diagnosis in three steps. First decide the type — health, gingivitis (no attachment loss with inflammation), chronic periodontitis (plaque-consistent attachment loss), or aggressive periodontitis (plaque-inconsistent attachment loss). Second decide the pattern — generalised if more than 30% of sites are involved, localised if less than 30%. Third decide the severity by the worst attachment loss present — mild (1–2 mm), moderate (3–4 mm) or severe (≥5 mm).

## Why

A diagnosis like "moderate generalised chronic periodontitis (smoking-associated)" carries every piece of information needed to set prognosis, choose treatment intensity, and decide referral. "Periodontal disease" alone tells you nothing about how to treat it. Splitting type from pattern from severity also makes change measurable: a patient who improves from moderate generalised to mild localised after therapy has demonstrably responded; the language captures the gain.

## How this applies in practice

- Always state all three components plus any modifying factor (smoking, diabetes, drug-induced overgrowth) when writing the diagnosis in the notes.
- Use the periodontal chart to count sites — >30% involved = generalised.
- Use the worst CAL site to grade severity, not the average.
- Without a diagnosis, prognosis and treatment plan are guesses — only formulate the plan once the three-part diagnosis is on the record.
- A patient with attachment loss but minimal plaque should ring bells for aggressive periodontitis, not "chronic perio with poor brushing".
