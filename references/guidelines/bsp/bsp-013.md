## The recommendation

Manage periodontitis in a stepwise sequence: first behaviour change with patient-performed plaque control, oral hygiene instruction and risk-factor control (notably smoking and diabetes); then thorough subgingival root surface debridement to remove plaque biofilms, calculus and overhangs; then reassessment at around 12 weeks where decisions about further non-surgical, surgical or maintenance care are made. Surgical intervention is only considered after a thorough course of non-surgical therapy in a patient with optimal plaque control.

## Why

Non-surgical therapy is highly effective for early-to-moderate disease — probing depths reduce through resolution of inflammation, often accompanied by recession, over the six months following treatment. The reason patient-performed plaque control comes first is that even perfect subgingival debridement only buys a few weeks: pathogenic flora can re-establish at the base of a 5 mm + pocket within three months. Without daily home plaque control there is no platform on which any subsequent therapy can hold. Risk-factor control — smoking, diabetes — is part of the same step because both blunt healing and re-seed disease. Surgery is reserved for residual disease in a motivated, low-risk patient because operating into uncontrolled inflammation produces poor healing and predictable recurrence.

## How this applies in practice

- Step 1 — behaviour change: OHI, interdental cleaning skills, smoking cessation, diabetes control liaison, removal of plaque-retentive factors. Do not skip this even if pockets are dramatic.
- Step 2 — subgingival instrumentation: full-mouth subgingival debridement of all sites ≥4 mm under LA as needed; can be delivered in 1–2 long appointments or spread over 4 with no loss of effect (factor in operator and patient fatigue).
- Step 3 — reassessment at around 12 weeks (after the gingiva has remodelled): repeat full periodontal chart. Residual deep pockets with BOP in a compliant patient = consider re-instrumentation, surgery or referral. Resolved pockets = move to maintenance.
- Step 4 — supportive periodontal therapy: tailored recall (initially 2–4 months), repeat charting annually, ongoing OHI coaching.
- Hand vs powered instruments: roughly equivalent in skilled hands. Several minutes per tooth in deep pockets is normal.
- Inform the patient up front about expected side effects: increased recession, longer-looking teeth, black triangles, sensitivity, food packing — these are signs of healing, not failure.
