## The recommendation

Record a BPE on every new patient and use it as a screening tool only — never as a diagnosis. Divide the dentition into six sextants (UR 17–14, UA 13–23, UL 24–27, LR 47–44, LA 43–33, LL 34–37), examine all teeth in each sextant except third molars (unless first or second molars are missing), require at least two teeth per sextant for it to qualify, and walk a WHO 621 probe (0.5 mm ball end, black band 3.5–5.5 mm) around the sulcus using a light force of 20–25 g — equivalent to the pressure that just blanches a fingernail. Record the highest score in each sextant.

## Why

The BPE was designed by the BSP in 1986 as a fast, reproducible screening tool to flag who needs more detailed assessment — it does not measure attachment loss and so cannot diagnose periodontitis or monitor treatment response. The sextant system gives the highest score that triggers further investigation without requiring a full chart on every patient. Six sites per tooth are not all coded — only the highest finding in the sextant is. A standardised probe and a controlled force (~25 g) are essential because heavier force pushes through inflamed tissue and inflates the score; lighter force misses true pockets. Excluding third molars avoids skewing the screen with teeth that are commonly malpositioned, partially erupted, or about to be extracted.

## How this applies in practice

- One new patient = one BPE, child or adult.
- Use a WHO 621 (BPE) probe — its 3.5–5.5 mm black band is what defines codes 3 versus 4.
- Calibrate your probing force: press the probe tip into your fingernail until the nail just blanches; that is roughly 25 g.
- "Walk" the probe around each tooth in the sextant, checking all sites — only when you have found the highest score is it safe to move on.
- If you find a code 4 in a sextant, keep examining the rest of that sextant before moving on, so you don't miss a furcation.
- Two teeth minimum to score the sextant; if only one tooth remains, it is included in the adjacent sextant.
- Add an asterisk (*) to any sextant where a furcation is detected — code and asterisk are independent.
