## The recommendation

For periodontitis patients, schedule supportive periodontal therapy (SPT) at intervals informed by clinical history, risk and stability — typically 2–4 months initially, with a baseline of three months because pathogenic flora can re-establish at the base of a 5 mm + pocket by then. Repeat full periodontal charting at least annually. Patients without periodontitis (BPE 0–2) can usually remain stable with hygienist visits every 1–2 years, but around 50% will develop periodontitis if causative factors are not controlled.

## Why

The three-month default recall comes from the bacteriology — even with good home care, putative pathogens have re-colonised deep pockets within twelve weeks of debridement. Three months is also short enough to keep the patient's behaviour-change momentum intact, since motivation drifts and instructions need re-reinforcing. Intervals can be extended once the patient demonstrates stable plaque control, no progressing sites and good compliance over several visits — but lengthening too early loses the periodontal control that the maintenance was designed to defend. Annual full charting catches site-specific drift before it becomes another round of attachment loss.

## How this applies in practice

- Set the first SPT recall at 2–3 months after the active phase, then adjust based on observed stability.
- Each SPT visit: oral exam, plaque and gingival inflammation record, probing depths, BOP from the pocket base, supra- and subgingival instrumentation, OHI re-motivation, deeper debridement under LA at residual sites ≥4 mm with BOP.
- Persistently bleeding sites or 2 mm increases at strategic sites (furcations, advanced sites) trigger re-treatment, not just observation.
- Furcations, infrabony defects, root grooves, enamel projections and chronic food-packing sites are the maintenance hotspots — focus extra time there.
- Tell the patient at the start of treatment that periodontitis is a lifelong susceptibility — maintenance is not optional, and stopping it forfeits the gains from active therapy.
- Even after extension to longer intervals, do not exceed what your monitoring data justify; tighten back if any site shows recurrent BOP or depth increase.
