## The recommendation

Antimicrobials have very little place in routine periodontal therapy. There is no indication for systemic antibiotics in chronic periodontitis. In aggressive periodontitis, consider amoxicillin 500 mg t.d.s. with metronidazole 200 mg t.d.s. for 7 days, started on the day of the final debridement (doxycycline 100 mg OD for 21 days with a 200 mg loading dose if penicillin-allergic) — but only after a thorough course of subgingival debridement delivered in 7–10 days, and only as an adjunct to that mechanical therapy, never alone. Necrotising periodontal disease: metronidazole 200 mg t.d.s. for 3 days alongside debridement and risk-factor control. Periodontal abscess: drainage and debridement first; antibiotics only if there is fever, malaise or facial swelling.

## Why

Antibiotic resistance is rising and anaphylaxis remains a small but real risk, so antibiotic use must be tied to a clear evidence base. Periodontitis is fundamentally a biofilm disease — antibiotics cannot penetrate established biofilms effectively, so without mechanical disruption of the biofilm by debridement first, the drug just exposes the patient to risk for negligible gain. The amoxicillin + metronidazole combination is chosen for aggressive periodontitis because it widens the spectrum across the unusual flora associated with that condition. Metronidazole alone covers the fuso-spirochaetal anaerobes of necrotising disease. A periodontal abscess is an infection with a closed cavity — drain it and the antibiotic question often becomes moot.

## How this applies in practice

- Chronic periodontitis: never prescribe systemic antibiotics for the perio itself.
- Aggressive periodontitis: refer for specialist care if available. If antibiotics are used, debride first, prescribe at the end of the debridement course, and start on the day of final debridement.
- NUG/NUP: metronidazole 200 mg t.d.s. for 3 days plus debridement, smoking cessation, OHI and stress/nutrition advice.
- Single periodontal abscess: drain via the pocket or by incision and debride; only add antibiotics if systemic involvement (fever, malaise, swelling).
- Multiple lateral periodontal abscesses: think undiagnosed diabetes or other systemic disease — antibiotics alone miss the cause.
- Local-delivery antimicrobials: only after non-surgical treatment has failed at isolated sites with no calculus and good plaque control. Modest benefit at best — referral is usually the better answer.
