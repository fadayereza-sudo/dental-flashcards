## The recommendation

Take radiographs that show crestal bone levels for any sextant scoring BPE 3, 4 or *. Periapicals taken with a long-cone paralleling technique (and a beam-aiming device) are the gold standard for code 4 sextants because they let you measure bone loss as a percentage of root length and visualise the periapical tissues. Bitewings opportunistically show early crestal loss; panoramic films are not justified as a routine perio screen.

## Why

Radiographs are retrospective — they show damage already done — but they are the only way to convert pocket depth into a meaningful estimate of remaining support. Knowing total root length matters because a 6 mm pocket on a long-rooted canine is a different prognosis from a 6 mm pocket on a short-rooted lower incisor. Periapicals also reveal angular defects, furcation involvements and perio-endo lesions that change the treatment plan: angular defects deeper than 3 mm will not resolve with non-surgical therapy alone, and multiple angular defects with furcations point to specialist referral. Panoramic radiation dose is too high relative to the diagnostic yield for a screening role, and panoramic geometry distorts the anterior region.

## How this applies in practice

- BPE 3 or 4 sextant: take a radiograph where bone crests are clearly visible — bitewings often suffice for posterior sextants, periapicals for anterior or where total root length matters.
- Code 4 with deep pockets, complex anatomy or anterior involvement: paralleling-technique periapical with a beam-aiming device.
- Report degree of bone loss as a percentage of root length when the apex is visible, plus the pattern (horizontal vs angular), furcation involvement, subgingival calculus, and any incidental pathology.
- Angular defects >3 mm or multiple furcations on the radiograph = warn the patient that non-surgical therapy alone won't close those pockets and consider referral.
- DPT is reserved for complex multi-issue cases, not for perio screening alone.
- Use rectangular collimation for periapicals and field-size collimation for DPTs to keep the patient dose to a minimum.
