## The recommendation

Refer using the BSP three-level complexity framework. Level 1 — uncomplicated cases stay in general practice. Level 2 — residual chronic moderate periodontitis (30–50% horizontal bone loss, residual true pockets ≥6 mm) after primary care therapy, certain non-plaque-induced diseases, aggressive periodontitis as determined by a specialist, strategic furcation defects, drug-induced overgrowth — manage in general practice if you have the skills, otherwise refer. Level 3 — severe periodontitis (>50% horizontal bone loss, true pockets ≥6 mm), aggressive periodontitis, perio surgery, complex furcations, peri-implantitis — refer the majority. A relevant modifying factor (e.g. complex medical history, head/neck radiotherapy, IV bisphosphonate, immunosuppression, bleeding disorder) bumps the case up by one level, but is not cumulative.

## Why

A complexity-based system protects patients in two directions: GDPs avoid attempting cases beyond their training, and specialist services avoid being overwhelmed by cases that should have been managed in primary care. The 50% bone loss / 6 mm pocket cut-off captures the point where non-surgical therapy alone is unlikely to succeed and where consequences of misdiagnosis are most severe. Aggressive periodontitis is escalated at all complexity levels because its rapid course and uncertain response to therapy give specialist input the highest payoff. Modifying factors increase complexity because they change either the safety of treatment (bleeding risk, MRONJ risk) or the predictability of healing (immunosuppression).

## How this applies in practice

- Even when referring, start non-surgical therapy in general practice yourself — it is part of your duty of care, and any other primary pathology (caries, endo) should be addressed before specialist hand-over.
- Address modifiable risk factors (especially smoking) before or alongside referral; a referral to a specialist for an active heavy smoker often returns with a request to stabilise lifestyle first.
- Aggressive periodontitis = always offer referral after initial preventive advice and OHI.
- Patient declines referral: document the discussion, the reasons for decline, the consequences explained, and continue to monitor — re-offer at later visits.
- Referral letter: patient details, reason, medical history including smoking, all medications, treatment already done, recent radiographs and periodontal charts.
- Inadvertent criticism — avoid saying "this must have been there for years" about previous care; frame the conversation around current findings and the plan ahead.
