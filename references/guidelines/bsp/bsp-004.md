## The recommendation

Score each sextant as the highest finding from this scale: 0 — pockets <3.5 mm, no calculus or overhangs, no bleeding (entire black band visible); 1 — as 0 but with bleeding on probing; 2 — pockets <3.5 mm with calculus, plaque-retentive factor or overhang (entire black band visible); 3 — pocket 4–5 mm (black band partially visible); 4 — pocket 6 mm or more (black band entirely within the pocket); * — furcation detected, recorded alongside the numerical code.

## Why

The codes are cumulative — code 2 implies bleeding plus a retentive factor, code 3 implies a pocket beyond the reach of patient brushing (4–5 mm), and code 4 indicates pockets that almost certainly require surgical or specialist input. The black band on the WHO 621 probe (3.5–5.5 mm) is the visual cut-off: if you can still see the whole band the pocket is <3.5 mm, if part is hidden the pocket is in the 4–5 mm range, and if the band disappears entirely the pocket is ≥6 mm. The asterisk is recorded *in addition* to the numerical code because furcation involvement changes the prognosis and management even at lower probing depths.

## How this applies in practice

- 0 — repeat at next routine exam, no treatment.
- 1 — oral hygiene instruction.
- 2 — OHI plus removal of plaque-retentive factors and supra/subgingival calculus.
- 3 — OHI, root surface debridement, then a 6-point pocket chart of that sextant after initial therapy; consider radiographs for attachment loss.
- 4 — OHI, RSD, full periodontal charting (6 sites per tooth) for the whole dentition before and after treatment, radiographs taken, consider specialist referral.
- * — treat according to the numerical code (0–4) but flag for specialist consideration; the furcation often dictates prognosis more than the pocket depth.
- Recession does not change the BPE code on its own (a code 1 sextant with recession remains code 1) — recession is recorded separately on the detailed chart.
