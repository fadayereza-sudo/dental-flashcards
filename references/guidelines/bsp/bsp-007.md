## The recommendation

Do not use the BPE around implants. Instead, record detailed probing depths at four or six points per implant, with the presence of any bleeding or suppuration noted, at least annually.

## Why

Peri-implant tissues are not connected to the implant by a periodontal ligament — there is no Sharpey-fibre attachment, just a hemidesmosomal cuff — so they are less resistant to probing and the probe travels further into healthy tissue than around a tooth. The implant's position in the bone (often deeper than a CEJ) also produces baseline "deep" readings with no disease. A BPE applied to implants would therefore over-call code 3 and 4 and fail to monitor change at specific sites. Probing pressure does not damage the implant or peri-implant soft tissues.

## How this applies in practice

- Four- or six-point probing record at each implant baseline (placement and superstructure delivery), then at least annually.
- Record bleeding on probing and suppuration alongside the depths — these distinguish peri-implant mucositis (no bone loss) from peri-implantitis (with bone loss).
- Do not be reassured by absence of pain — peri-implantitis can be silent and progress faster than periodontitis.
- Take baseline periapicals at placement and superstructure delivery so future bone loss can be measured against a real reference, not estimated.
