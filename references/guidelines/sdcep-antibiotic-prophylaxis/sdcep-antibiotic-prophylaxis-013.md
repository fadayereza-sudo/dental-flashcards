## The recommendation

Advise all high and moderate risk patients to contact their GP as soon as possible if they develop any of: fever ≥38°C; sweats or chills, especially at night; breathlessness on normal exertion; poor appetite or unexplained weight loss; fatigue; or muscle, joint or back pain unrelated to recent activity — particularly if these occur together as a flu-like illness. Tell the patient to mention any recent dental treatment to whichever clinician they see.

## Why

IE is hard to diagnose because the early picture mimics flu, and the case-fatality rate is around 30% — every week of delay matters. The classic textbook signs (Janeway lesions, Osler's nodes, splinter haemorrhages, new murmur) appear late or inconsistently. The flu-like cluster is the earliest reliable trigger, and the dental-treatment context is the missing detail that often delays referral when the patient sees their GP.

## How this applies in practice

- Give the symptom list verbally and on paper (the SDCEP patient information leaflet) — patients forget half of what is said in a dental chair.
- Frame it honestly: most of the time these symptoms will be a viral illness, but a heart-valve patient should always be checked.
- "Tell the doctor or A&E about any recent dental work" is the line that gets the patient onto the right diagnostic pathway.
- Patients prescribed prophylactic antibiotics need an additional warning: severe diarrhoea may be antibiotic-associated colitis (Clostridioides difficile) and needs urgent medical review — this can be fatal.
- Document that the IE-symptom discussion took place.
