## The recommendation

For every at-risk patient facing an at-risk dental procedure, make a shared decision with the patient that includes the IE risk of the procedure, the benefits and harms of antibiotic prophylaxis, the patient's oral health status, their values and preferences, and any information from their cardiac team. Record the discussion contemporaneously in the clinical notes.

## Why

Montgomery v Lanarkshire (2015) and McCulloch v Forth Valley (2023) reset the law on consent in the UK: the clinician must inform the patient of "material risks" — meaning what this particular patient would want to know, not what the clinician thinks is important. Antibiotic prophylaxis carries real harms (anaphylaxis, colitis, resistance) and uncertain benefits, so the decision sits squarely in the territory of values and preferences. GDC Standards 3.1–3.3 and NICE NG197 require shared decision-making to be documented. A note that simply says "prescribed amoxicillin 3g" is not consent — the discussion is.

## How this applies in practice

- Cover the same headings every time: risk of IE from the procedure, why prophylaxis is offered or considered, possible side effects, the patient's own preference, the cardiac team's input.
- Use the SDCEP Patient discussion aid and patient information leaflet — they save time and standardise content.
- Document specifically: the options discussed, benefits and harms covered, the patient's response, and their final decision.
- If the patient declines prophylaxis for an extraction, record that they were told this may expose them to a higher IE risk, and continue with the procedure.
