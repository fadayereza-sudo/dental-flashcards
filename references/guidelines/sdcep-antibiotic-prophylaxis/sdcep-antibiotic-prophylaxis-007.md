## The recommendation

For patients at high risk of infective endocarditis, OFFER antibiotic prophylaxis prior to extractions and oral surgery procedures. These include dental extractions, incision and drainage of an abscess, all oral surgical procedures, periodontal and endodontic surgery, placement of dental implants (including temporary anchorage devices and mini-implants), and uncovering submucosal implants or implant components.

## Why

In a high-risk patient without prophylaxis, the estimated risk of IE after extraction is about 1 in 100, and after oral surgery about 1 in 40 — orders of magnitude higher than the 1 in 1,000 background risk in the same patient for any at-risk procedure. These procedures cut bone or deep soft tissue, releasing a large bolus of oral bacteria into the circulation. The size of the bacteraemia and the consistency of the evidence is what justifies the stronger "offer" wording, rather than the softer "consider" used for less invasive procedures.

## How this applies in practice

- The "offer" language means: actively recommend prophylaxis as the default for these procedures, then complete the shared decision.
- For high-risk patients, plan ahead — write the prescription at the appointment before the extraction (or check practice stock).
- If a patient needs multiple invasive procedures, group them where possible to reduce repeated antibiotic doses (AHA suggests at least 10 days between separate dosed episodes).
- Implant placement counts — including TADs and mini-implants — even though some textbooks list it as "minor".
- A patient who declines prophylaxis for an extraction must be told they may be exposing themselves to a higher IE risk; record the conversation.
