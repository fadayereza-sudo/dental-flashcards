## The recommendation

For an adult without penicillin allergy, prescribe a single oral dose of amoxicillin 2 g (4 × 500 mg capsules) or 3 g oral sachet. For a child, amoxicillin 50 mg/kg (maximum 2 g). The dose must be taken 30–60 minutes before the procedure begins.

## Why

Amoxicillin reaches peak plasma concentration about 1–2 hours after an oral dose, so the 30–60-minute pre-procedure window puts circulating drug at its peak when the bacteraemia is generated. A bigger dose (2 g or 3 g) gives a longer window of cover than the standard treatment dose; a single dose is enough because the bacteraemia from the procedure is short-lived and the goal is to kill the small inoculum before it seeds the endocardium. The 2023 ESC default is 2 g (cheaper, more reliably available); the historic UK 3 g sachet is still acceptable. Both work — choose by patient and clinician preference, supply and cost.

## How this applies in practice

- Confirm "no penicillin allergy" properly, not just "any allergies?" — capture rashes, swelling, anaphylaxis history.
- Time it: take the dose at the practice 30–60 minutes before the appointment, or take at home and confirm the procedure is going ahead before swallowing.
- If the patient takes it at the surgery, keep them in the practice between dose and procedure — never let them go for a coffee.
- For sequential at-risk procedures over a short window, leave at least 10 days between dosed appointments (AHA suggestion) and prescribe the same drug each time — no need to rotate antibiotic class.
- A recent course of amoxicillin for an unrelated infection does not bar amoxicillin for prophylaxis; class change is not required.
