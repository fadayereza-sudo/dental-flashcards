## The recommendation

Antibiotic prophylaxis against infective endocarditis is recommended in patients with: a previous episode of IE; surgically implanted prosthetic valves or any material used for surgical valve repair; transcatheter implanted aortic and pulmonary valvular prostheses; untreated cyanotic congenital heart disease (CHD); cyanotic CHD treated with surgery or transcatheter procedures with post-operative palliative shunts, conduits or other prostheses; or ventricular assist devices.

## Why

These categories share two features. They have a much higher absolute incidence of IE — about 497 cases per 100,000 per year, around 36 times the general-population rate. And when they do develop IE, they have a markedly worse prognosis: higher mortality, more complications, more need for cardiac surgery. The combination of higher incidence and worse outcome is what pushes the risk–benefit balance in favour of accepting the harms of a single antibiotic dose. After surgical CHD repair with no residual defect or valve prosthesis, the 6-month healing window is the only period of high risk — after that the patient drops out of the high-risk group.

## How this applies in practice

- Build a habit of asking specifically: "Have you ever had infective endocarditis?" and "Have you had heart-valve surgery — repair or replacement?" — these two questions catch most of the high-risk group.
- Cyanotic CHD is rare in adult primary care; if a patient mentions a "blue baby" history or central cyanosis, treat as high risk and contact the cardiac team.
- After repair of cyanotic CHD with no residue, prophylaxis is recommended only for the first 6 months — note the date.
- Patients with these conditions should have a cardiologist letter or alert card; if not, contact the cardiac team or GP before invasive treatment.
