## The recommendation

Antibiotic prophylaxis "should be considered" in patients with transcatheter mitral and tricuspid valve repair, and within the first 6 months after implantation of septal defect closure devices, left atrial appendage closure devices, vascular grafts, vena cava filters, or central venous system ventriculo-atrial shunts. It "may be considered" in heart transplant recipients.

## Why

These conditions carry a real but less well-evidenced IE risk. The ESC ranks the recommendations Class IIa (transcatheter mitral/tricuspid repair, plus the 6-month post-implantation devices) and Class IIb (heart transplant), reflecting weaker evidence than the Class I categories in reference 002. The 6-month rule for closure devices, grafts, filters and shunts reflects the time it takes for the implant surface to endothelialise — once it is covered with native tissue, bacteraemia is much less likely to seed it. After that window, the patient drops back to low risk.

## How this applies in practice

- "Recommended" vs "should be considered" wording matters — these patients still get a shared-decision conversation but the conclusion is genuinely two-sided.
- Always ask about timing: "When was the device implanted?" Within 6 months = treat as high risk. Beyond 6 months for these specific devices = no prophylaxis needed.
- A heart transplant recipient on tacrolimus or ciclosporin must NOT receive clarithromycin (calcineurin-inhibitor interaction) — pick amoxicillin or a different macrolide.
- If unsure whether a device falls into this group or the truly Class I high-risk group, contact the cardiac team rather than guess.
