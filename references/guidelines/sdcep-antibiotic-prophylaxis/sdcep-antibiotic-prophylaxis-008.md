## The recommendation

For high-risk patients, CONSIDER antibiotic prophylaxis for procedures that involve manipulation of the gingival or periapical region of the teeth: PMPR (supra- and subgingival scaling), full periodontal examinations including pocket charting, basic periodontal examination (BPE), plaque and bleeding indices, subgingival restorations and fixed prosthodontics, preformed metal crowns, subgingival rubber dam clamps and matrix bands, placement and removal of orthodontic separators and bands, and endodontic treatment before the apical stop is established.

## Why

The IE risk attached to these procedures is real but uncertain — far less clear than for extractions or oral surgery. Bacteraemia happens, but at lower volume, and the evidence linking it to clinical IE is weak. So the recommendation is conditional rather than absolute, and the patient's own oral health status becomes a key driver. A patient with healthy gums bleeds less during a BPE than one with unstable periodontitis — meaning the bacteraemia, and therefore the prophylaxis case, depends on the gum the GDP is actually looking at.

## How this applies in practice

- "Consider" means a genuine two-way discussion: the IE risk is uncertain, the antibiotic harms are real, and the patient's preference and oral health status both swing the decision.
- Healthy gingiva → lean toward no prophylaxis. Unstable periodontitis → lean toward yes, and address the underlying disease.
- Plan PMPR + restorations + indices in the same visit if the patient has chosen prophylaxis — one antibiotic dose covers the lot.
- Re-visit the decision at every new course of treatment; oral health, experience and preferences change.
- Endodontic treatment counts only before the apical stop is established; once it is, the canal is no longer open to the periapical tissues.
