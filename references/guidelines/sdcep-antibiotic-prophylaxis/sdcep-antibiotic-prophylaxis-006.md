## The recommendation

All patients at high or moderate risk of IE should be given clear and consistent information about prevention, covering: the importance of maintaining good oral health and individualised oral hygiene advice; regular dental check-ups based on individual risk assessment; symptoms that may indicate IE and when to seek expert advice; and the risks of invasive procedures including non-medical ones such as body piercing or tattooing.

## Why

Cumulative low-grade bacteraemias from everyday tooth-brushing, flossing and chewing are now thought to contribute more to IE risk than sporadic high-grade bacteraemias from dental procedures. The healthier the gingiva, the less bacteraemia per brush stroke. So consistent oral hygiene over the years lowers IE risk in a way that no single antibiotic dose can. Patients also need to know IE symptoms because the disease is hard to diagnose and case-fatality is around 30% — early presentation matters. Tattoos and piercings are flagged because they breach a non-sterile surface and are easy for patients to overlook.

## How this applies in practice

- Treat the prevention conversation as the main IE intervention, not a bolt-on to the antibiotic discussion.
- Personalised oral hygiene instruction beats generic advice — show the patient where they are missing.
- Tell the patient and their GP/cardiac team to act on flu-like symptoms (fever ≥38°C, night sweats, breathlessness, weight loss, fatigue, joint or back pain) — and to mention any recent dental treatment.
- Warn before they ask: if they are planning a tongue piercing or a tattoo, this matters for their heart, not just for infection control.
- Document that the prevention discussion happened.
