## The recommendation

Antibiotic prophylaxis is not recommended in patients with rheumatic heart disease, non-rheumatic degenerative valve disease, congenital valve abnormalities including bicuspid aortic valve disease, cardiovascular implanted electronic devices (CIEDs), or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy — unless their cardiac team has specifically requested it (e.g. for added comorbidity such as diabetes or immunosuppression).

## Why

Moderate-risk patients have an IE incidence around 280 per 100,000 per year — about 20 times the general-population rate but still much lower than the high-risk group (497 per 100,000). The evidence does not show that a single prophylactic antibiotic dose changes their outcome, while the harms (anaphylaxis, colitis, resistance) apply equally. ESC therefore reserves prophylaxis in this group for individual cases where the cardiac team identifies extra risk on top of the cardiac diagnosis itself.

## How this applies in practice

- A patient with a bicuspid aortic valve, mitral valve prolapse, or a pacemaker who asks for "the antibiotic before the dentist" needs the conversation, not the prescription.
- If a moderate-risk patient still requests prophylaxis after the discussion, make a shared decision and document it.
- The exception is a written instruction from the cardiac team — if the cardiologist has written or said yes (often because of comorbidity), follow that and treat them as high-risk for prescribing purposes.
- A repaired valve with prosthetic material is no longer "moderate risk" — that moves the patient into the high-risk group (see reference 002).
