## The recommendation

NICE CG64 states: "Antibiotic prophylaxis against infective endocarditis is not recommended routinely for people undergoing dental procedures." Antibiotic prophylaxis is therefore not the default option for most patients at risk of IE.

## Why

Three things shifted the position. First, the link between an isolated dental procedure and IE is weak — fewer than 10% of new IE cases had an invasive dental procedure in the preceding 30 days, and 30–40% have no known predisposing cardiac disease at all. Second, the bigger source of bacteraemia is the cumulative low-grade exposure from everyday tooth-brushing, flossing and chewing — which makes day-to-day oral hygiene more important than a one-off antibiotic dose. Third, the evidence that a single prophylactic dose actually prevents IE is weak, while the harms (anaphylaxis, antibiotic-associated colitis, resistance) are real. NICE judged the balance against routine prophylaxis.

## How this applies in practice

- Default position for any patient asking "do I need antibiotics before my dental work?": no.
- Prophylaxis is now reserved for a small high-risk subgroup (see references 002 and 003) and only for specific 'at-risk' procedures.
- Spending the appointment teaching oral hygiene does more for IE risk than reaching for the prescription pad.
- NICE's 2024 update added a direct link to the SDCEP implementation advice — that is the document the GDP is expected to apply.
