## The recommendation

Antibiotic prophylaxis is NOT recommended for: infiltration or block local anaesthetic injections in non-infected soft tissues; supragingival restorations; removal of sutures; radiographs; placement or adjustment of removable orthodontic or prosthodontic appliances; adjustment of fixed orthodontic appliances which does not involve placement or removal of separators and bands; following exfoliation of primary teeth; and following trauma to the lips or oral mucosa.

## Why

These procedures either do not breach the mucosa, do not produce a clinically significant bacteraemia, or involve tissue that has already been compromised in a way prophylaxis cannot help. A subgingival restoration is at-risk; a supragingival one is not, because the bur stays above the bacterial reservoir in the sulcus. LA injections in healthy tissue cause negligible bacteraemia. Suture removal opens nothing. Radiographs are external. Exfoliation and trauma have already happened — antibiotics started later cannot prevent the bacteraemia that followed the bleed.

## How this applies in practice

- A high-risk patient turning up for a new denture fitting, a repeat bitewing or removal of post-extraction sutures does NOT need prophylaxis.
- Knocked-out tooth, lip laceration after a fall: no prophylaxis — the bacteraemia is already in the blood by the time the patient arrives.
- A child with a wobbly primary tooth that exfoliates at home does not need to come in for antibiotics.
- The line between supragingival (no prophylaxis) and subgingival (consider prophylaxis) restorations is the one to watch — if the margin breaches the gingiva, it counts as at-risk.
