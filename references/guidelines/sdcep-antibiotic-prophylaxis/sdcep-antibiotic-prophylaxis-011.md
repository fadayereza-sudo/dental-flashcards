## The recommendation

For oral prophylaxis in penicillin allergy, use clarithromycin 500 mg or azithromycin 500 mg (children: 15 mg/kg, maximum 500 mg). Clindamycin is no longer recommended. Do not give amoxicillin to anyone with a history of anaphylaxis, urticaria or rash immediately after penicillin; gastrointestinal-only side effects do not count as true allergy and amoxicillin can still be used.

## Why

The ESC dropped clindamycin from the 2023 guideline because it carries a disproportionate risk of Clostridioides difficile-associated colitis — the harm exceeded any IE-prevention benefit. Clarithromycin and azithromycin cover the same oral-strep flora at much lower colitis risk. The amoxicillin allergy filter matters because immediate IgE-mediated reactions can kill on first re-exposure; rash-and-anaphylaxis history must rule the drug out, while a vague "made me feel sick" usually does not. Clarithromycin in turn has its own list of no-go situations (warfarin, statins, calcineurin inhibitors, pregnancy, QT prolongation) — many of which a cardiac patient already meets.

## How this applies in practice

- "Allergic to penicillin" is not enough — ask: "What happened? Rash, breathing trouble, swelling, just an upset stomach?" Document.
- Genuine penicillin allergy: prescribe clarithromycin 500 mg or azithromycin 500 mg, single dose 30–60 minutes before procedure.
- Clarithromycin is contraindicated in pregnancy, in patients on warfarin or statins, and in heart-transplant recipients on tacrolimus or ciclosporin — pick azithromycin or contact the cardiac team for an alternative.
- Use both clarithromycin and azithromycin with caution if the patient has long QT, hepatic or renal impairment.
- If the listed regimens are unsuitable, contact a consultant microbiologist, community pharmacist or the UK Dental Medicines Advisory Service for an alternative.
