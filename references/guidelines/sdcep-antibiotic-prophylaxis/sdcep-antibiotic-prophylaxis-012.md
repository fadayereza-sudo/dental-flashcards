## The recommendation

For an emergency patient with a cardiac condition whose IE risk you cannot determine and whose cardiac team you cannot reach, assume high-risk status and make a shared decision on prophylaxis. Treat any episode of dental infection in an at-risk patient promptly — do not delay over the prophylaxis question. Do not give prophylaxis after trauma to the lips or oral mucosa.

## Why

Untreated dental infection in a high-risk patient is itself a major IE driver, because it produces continuous low-grade bacteraemia. Delaying treatment to chase paperwork is more dangerous than the procedure itself. The default-to-high-risk rule errs on the side of giving an antibiotic that probably is not needed rather than skipping one that probably is. The trauma exclusion exists because the bacteraemia from a knocked-out tooth or torn lip has already happened by the time the patient arrives — a single dose started after the event cannot prevent what is already in the blood.

## How this applies in practice

- Build the habit of recording IE risk status at registration and after any change in cardiac history, so the answer is in the notes when the emergency arrives.
- If a patient turns up with a swollen face and known cardiac history but no documentation, phone the cardiac team or GP first if you have time; if you do not, treat as high risk and proceed with shared decision.
- Beyond your competency? Refer urgently rather than improvise — but do not delay treating the infection itself.
- Trauma case (avulsion, mucosal laceration, lip cut): no prophylaxis indicated; manage the injury and review.
- Document why you assumed high-risk status and what the shared decision was.
