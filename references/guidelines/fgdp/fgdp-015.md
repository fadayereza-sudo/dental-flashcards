## The recommendation

Every UK dental practice must audit patient dose against locally-set diagnostic reference levels (DRLs) — by default, the national DRLs (1.7 mGy for an adult intraoral molar, 93 mGy·cm² panoramic adult, 67 mGy·cm² panoramic child). Equipment delivering doses above the local DRL must trigger investigation and reduction.

## Why

Surveys have shown the dose for the same intraoral examination varying by a factor of 600 between practices — meaning some patients were getting 600 times the necessary dose for an identical clinical question. DRLs are set at the third-quartile value (75% of equipment falls below) so they represent achievable, not idealised, performance. Audit is the mechanism by which a practice notices its own equipment has drifted. Without it, an old machine with deteriorating filtration, an exposure setting that someone increased "for clearer images", or a sensor losing sensitivity goes unchallenged for years.

## How this applies in practice

- Local DRLs default to the national values unless a medical physics expert measures lower achievable doses and supports a stricter local figure.
- Audit is a legal requirement under IRR 2017 — not optional, and not "we'll do it next year".
- If your equipment exceeds the local DRL, investigate before the next regulatory inspection: check exposure settings, filtration, receptor sensitivity, processing, and operator technique.
- A medical physics expert (MPE) is the named person in the practice's radiation protection arrangements who supports DRL setting and review.
- CBCT does not yet have a national DRL but local reference levels should be set with MPE input; users should consult the HPA/PHE achievable dose guidance.
