## The recommendation

Every radiograph must be accompanied by a contemporaneous record of three things in the notes: the justification, the quality grade, and the clinical report (findings, including absence of pathology). Existing radiographs must be sought before exposing new ones, and PACS-stored hospital images should be requested where relevant.

## Why

The trio — justify, grade, report — is what IRMER 2017 expects every operator to evidence. Without justification recorded, you cannot show the exposure was clinically necessary; without a grade, you cannot show the dose produced a diagnostic image; without a report, you cannot show you actually used the information. Failing any one means the patient received dose without demonstrable benefit. Seeking prior images cuts duplicate exposures and is also cheaper and faster than re-taking; PACS now lets every NHS trust transmit radiographs electronically, so "I didn't have access to the hospital film" no longer holds.

## How this applies in practice

- Notes template for every radiograph: clinical question (justification) → film(s) taken → quality grade → findings → action.
- A finding of "no pathology" is a finding — write it down. An empty notes line invites a later regulator or claimant to assume the image was never reviewed.
- New patient: ask whether they have had radiographs elsewhere recently and request copies before exposing your own.
- When referring a patient, send relevant existing radiographs with the referral. The accepting clinician should not need to repeat them.
- Storage: dental radiographs must be kept for 11 years or until the patient is 25, whichever is longer.
