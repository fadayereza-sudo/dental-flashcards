## The recommendation

Posterior bitewing radiographs are an essential adjunct to clinical examination for caries diagnosis in children and adults. Bitewings detect both approximal and occlusal caries missed by visual examination, and no other radiographic technique offers equivalent diagnostic yield at comparable dose.

## Why

Approximal surfaces are hidden from direct inspection by adjacent teeth; an early enamel lesion is invisible clinically but shows on a bitewing. For occlusal surfaces, even meticulous ICDAS visual scoring misses dentine caries hidden under an apparently intact fissure. Studies report bitewings add 150–800% to the caries yield of clinical examination alone — that is, for every lesion you see, the bitewing shows one to eight more. Panoramic radiography is specifically inferior because its geometry distorts the contact areas and its resolution is lower. The bitewing projection's perpendicular beam and short object-to-receptor distance are what give it its diagnostic edge.

## How this applies in practice

- Clinical examination comes first: clean, dry teeth under good light, with transillumination and flossing where needed, and ICDAS scoring rather than sharp-probing.
- Use holders and beam-aiming devices every time — freehand bitewings give parallax distortion that fakes or hides interproximal lesions.
- When approximal contacts are open (e.g. after extractions, in the mixed dentition), bitewings still have a role in detecting occlusal dentine caries.
- Do not substitute a panoramic for bitewings "to save a trip" — the guideline explicitly says panoramic is inferior for common dental pathosis.
- Once you have the bitewing, report it in the notes with a caries status for each interproximal and occlusal surface.
