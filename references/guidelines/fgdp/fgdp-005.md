## The recommendation

Posterior bitewings should be prescribed by caries risk category: high risk six-monthly, moderate risk annually, low risk every two years (adults) or every 12–24 months (children, with longer in permanent dentition). In every category, bitewings continue at that interval until no new or active lesions are apparent and the patient has moved into a lower risk category.

## Why

The frequency reflects the rate at which a new or progressing approximal lesion could reach a point where intervention timing matters. In high-risk individuals, approximal enamel lesions can still take three to four years to penetrate enamel, but once they cross the enamel-dentine junction the progression rate becomes unpredictable — six-monthly is short enough to catch cavitation before the pulp is involved. In low-risk adults, approximal enamel lesions now take six to eight years or more on average to reach dentine; two-year intervals comfortably catch them. Fixing the interval to risk rather than to the recall calendar is what brings the collective dose down without losing diagnostic yield.

## How this applies in practice

- Reassess caries risk at every recall — individuals move in and out of risk categories with changes in diet, saliva flow, medications, and dexterity.
- A patient who has just completed orthodontic treatment, started a dry-mouth medication, had a bereavement, or retired may need to be reclassified upwards even if their clinical exam looks unchanged.
- Do not take bitewings more often than the band allows — "six-monthly until stable" is a ceiling, not a minimum.
- Document risk status, the reasoning, and the planned next bitewing date in the notes. This is what a regulator looks for when auditing frequency.
- An alternative paediatric schedule (EAPD) uses age-based baselines (5, 8–9, 12–16 years) with longer intervals for low-risk children; valid as grade C expert opinion.
