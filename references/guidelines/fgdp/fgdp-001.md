## The recommendation

Every dental x-ray exposure must be individually justified on the basis of history and clinical examination. Routine or "screening" radiographs — taken regardless of signs and symptoms — are unacceptable, and the decision (including any decision not to expose) must be recorded in the notes.

## Why

Justification is both an ethical and a legal requirement under IRMER 2017: the practitioner must show a net benefit to the patient by weighing diagnostic yield against radiation detriment and alternatives. A generalised "take bitewings at every six-month check" or "panoramic on all new patients" approach bypasses this weighing — it exposes patients who would not benefit. Routine screening in medicine has been abandoned (except for evidence-based population programmes like breast screening) for the same reason: the low prevalence of asymptomatic pathology means yield cannot justify the collective dose.

## How this applies in practice

- The two required elements before any exposure: history and clinical examination. A panoramic request of "please take a panoramic for diagnosis" is specifically called out as inadequate clinical information.
- If you and the patient decide against a radiograph that might ultimately be contentious (e.g. declining canine assessment, radiography in pregnancy), document the discussion and decision in the notes. This is what defends you later.
- Consideration of "carers and comforters" is now in the regulations — document that a parent supporting a child in the beam has given informed consent.
- A "routine bitewings every 6 months" recall without a fresh risk assessment is not justification; it is habit.
