## The recommendation

In patients with generalised pocketing of 4–5 mm (BPE Code 3 maximum) and little or no recession, use horizontal bitewings. In patients with pocketing of 6 mm or more (BPE Code 4), use vertical bitewings supplemented by paralleling periapicals at sites where alveolar bone image is not fully included. A periapical taken with the paralleling technique is indicated if a perio-endo lesion is suspected.

## Why

The bitewing projection's perpendicular geometry gives optimal measurement of alveolar bone levels; bitewings that are already being taken for caries can double up for periodontal assessment at no extra dose. For shallower pockets (Code 3), horizontal bitewings image the coronal few millimetres of bone loss — enough to stage and plan. For deeper pockets (Code 4), horizontal bitewings cut off the apical extent of the defect, so a vertical orientation captures more bone height; where the defect still extends beyond the bitewing, a periapical with paralleling technique images the full root and surrounding bone. A full-mouth series of periapicals or a panoramic with supplementary periapicals are alternatives, chosen by balancing detail against dose.

## How this applies in practice

- BPE Code 0, 1, 2: periodontal radiography is not routinely indicated; the clinical examination and any bitewings taken for caries are enough.
- BPE Code 3 in one sextant only: targeted horizontal bitewing of that sextant, not a full mouth survey.
- CBCT is not indicated as a routine method of imaging periodontal bone support; its role is limited to specific research or very complex cases where 2D imaging is inadequate.
- Existing radiographs should be reviewed first — the rate of bone loss over time is often more useful than a single snapshot, and prior films may already answer the question.
- The decision to repeat periodontal radiographs is case-by-case, based on whether the new information would change patient management. No fixed interval.
