## The recommendation

In higher-risk patients, aim to avoid extractions and other procedures that impact on bone by considering other treatment options. If extraction remains the most appropriate treatment, discuss the risks, proceed as clinically indicated without routine antibiotic/antiseptic prophylaxis, and review healing.

## Why

Cancer patients on high-dose intravenous anti-resorptives have an estimated 2.9% MRONJ incidence after extraction — roughly twenty times the osteoporosis figure. That risk is high enough that any conservative alternative deserves serious consideration before defaulting to extraction. Endodontic treatment of an unrestorable tooth (decoronation and root canal) or retaining roots in the absence of infection sometimes substitutes for extraction in this group.

## How this applies in practice

- Consider alternatives first: root canal treatment, decoronation with root retention, conservative restoration of a tooth that would normally be extracted.
- For higher-risk cancer patients in particular, consult an oral surgery or special care dentistry specialist before extracting — joint planning is the safer default for this group.
- If extraction is the only option, get explicit consent covering the MRONJ risk and document the conversation.
- Review at 8 weeks: a non-healed socket triggers referral.
