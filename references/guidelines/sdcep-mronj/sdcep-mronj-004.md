## The recommendation

Allocate patients to one of two MRONJ risk groups. **Low risk:** treated for osteoporosis or other non-malignant bone disease with bisphosphonates for less than five years, or with denosumab, and not on concurrent systemic glucocorticoids. **Higher risk:** any cancer patient on anti-resorptives or anti-angiogenics; any non-malignant patient on bisphosphonates more than five years; or any patient on concurrent systemic glucocorticoids.

## Why

The absolute risk of MRONJ in osteoporosis patients is roughly 0.1% (1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000), versus approximately 1% in cancer patients (1 in 100), with prostate cancer and multiple myeloma at the higher end. Cancer patients receive much larger and more frequent intravenous doses, which is why their cumulative exposure produces a tenfold-or-greater increase in risk. Long-duration osteoporosis patients (>5 years bisphosphonate) and those on concurrent systemic steroids cluster into a higher-risk group because both factors independently amplify the underlying risk.

## How this applies in practice

- The risk category drives the management plan: low-risk patients have routine extractions; higher-risk patients have alternative options considered first.
- Cancer indication alone puts a patient at higher risk regardless of how short the treatment has been.
- Concurrent oral prednisolone or any systemic glucocorticoid bumps an osteoporosis patient out of the low-risk category.
- The two higher-risk subgroups (cancer vs long-duration osteoporosis with steroids) do not have the same absolute risk — the category just signals "more considered planning needed."
