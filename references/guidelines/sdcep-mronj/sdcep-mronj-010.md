## The recommendation

Review healing after any extraction in an at-risk patient. If the extraction socket is not healed at 8 weeks and you suspect MRONJ, refer to an oral surgery or special care dentistry specialist as per local protocols. Refer any patient with evidence of spontaneous MRONJ. Consider reporting suspected cases to the MHRA via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Why

MRONJ is defined by exposed or probeable necrotic bone in the maxillofacial region that has persisted for more than 8 weeks in a patient with current or previous anti-resorptive or anti-angiogenic exposure and no history of head and neck radiotherapy. The 8-week threshold separates a normally slow-healing socket from genuine osteonecrosis. Treatment of MRONJ is specialist-led — primary care management is recognition and referral.

## How this applies in practice

- At the post-extraction review, look for: exposed bone visible in the socket, persistent pain disproportionate to expected healing, intraoral or extraoral fistula, swelling, altered sensation, loose teeth adjacent to the site.
- Spontaneous MRONJ — exposed bone without a triggering procedure — also warrants urgent referral. It can present in patients on high-dose anti-resorptives even without dental intervention.
- Refer locally; the specialist will write to you and the GMP if MRONJ is confirmed.
- File a Yellow Card report to the MHRA and encourage the patient to do the same — incidence data depend on these reports.
