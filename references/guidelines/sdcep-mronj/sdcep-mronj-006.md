## The recommendation

Advise the patient (or carer) that, due to their medication, there may be a risk of developing MRONJ but ensure they understand the risk is small. It is very important they are not discouraged from taking their anti-resorptive or anti-angiogenic medication or from undergoing dental treatment. Record that this advice has been given.

## Why

Patients who hear "necrosis of the jaw" without the absolute numbers often stop taking a drug that is preventing fracture or controlling cancer — the consequences of stopping are far worse than the small MRONJ risk. The dentist's job is to give a calibrated picture: 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000 for osteoporosis patients, 1 in 100 for cancer patients, and stress that MRONJ is an adverse effect of the medication, not something dental treatment causes.

## How this applies in practice

- Use plain numbers, not percentages: "about 1 person in every 1,000 to 10,000" lands better than "0.01 to 0.1 per cent."
- Mention that stopping the drug does not eliminate the risk for bisphosphonates, so withdrawing the medication is not a free fix.
- Make clear the decision to start, stop, or change the drug belongs to the prescribing doctor — the dentist's role is to manage oral health alongside it.
- Document the conversation in the notes; Appendix 4 of the guidance lists the points to cover.
