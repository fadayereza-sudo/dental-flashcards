## The recommendation

Implant placement should be avoided in patients being treated with high-dose anti-resorptive or anti-angiogenic drugs for the management of cancer. The MRONJ risk after implant placement in lower-risk patient groups is currently unknown.

## Why

Implant placement is a procedure that impacts on bone, with the same drilling and surgical trauma as any oral surgical procedure, plus the long-term presence of a foreign body at the bone-mucosa interface. In high-dose cancer patients the background extraction-related MRONJ risk is already around 3%, so adding an elective bone-impacting procedure for a non-essential indication is hard to justify. For lower-risk osteoporosis patients the evidence is mixed and the decision becomes individualised.

## How this applies in practice

- High-dose IV bisphosphonates or monthly Xgeva for cancer is a clear reason to avoid implants — discuss alternatives such as conventional dentures or adhesive bridges.
- For osteoporosis patients on oral bisphosphonates, the risk-benefit is less clear; the patient must understand the uncertainty and consent specifically to the unknown MRONJ risk.
- If the patient already has implants placed before starting anti-resorptive therapy, this is not a reason to remove them — it is a reason for very careful periodontal maintenance.
