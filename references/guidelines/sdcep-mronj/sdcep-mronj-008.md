## The recommendation

In low-risk patients who need an extraction or other procedure that impacts on bone: discuss risks and benefits, proceed with the treatment as clinically indicated, do not prescribe antibiotic or antiseptic prophylaxis unless required for other reasons, and review healing.

## Why

The absolute MRONJ risk after extraction in osteoporosis patients on anti-resorptives is around 0.15% — small enough that withholding necessary extractions causes more harm (pain, infection, eventual urgent extraction in worse circumstances) than proceeding. There is currently insufficient evidence that antibiotic or antiseptic prophylaxis reduces MRONJ risk, so prescribing them adds antimicrobial-stewardship harm without proven benefit.

## How this applies in practice

- Treat the extraction as you would for any patient — atraumatic technique, appropriate post-op instructions.
- Warn the patient to call the practice if they develop unexpected pain, tingling, numbness, altered sensation, or swelling at the site.
- Schedule a review to check healing.
- Skip antibiotic prophylaxis unless there is a separate clinical reason (e.g. spreading infection at the time of extraction).
