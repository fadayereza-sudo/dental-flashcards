## The recommendation

Before commencement of anti-resorptive or anti-angiogenic drug therapy, or as soon as possible thereafter, get the patient as dentally fit as feasible, prioritising preventive care. Higher-risk cancer patients should preferably undergo a thorough dental assessment with remedial treatment before drug therapy starts.

## Why

Once the drug is on board, every extraction and bone-impacting procedure carries an added risk of MRONJ that does not exist before therapy begins. The pre-treatment window is the only one where the risk-benefit of removing a hopeless tooth or treating active periodontal disease is clearly in favour of intervention. After cancer therapy starts, even routine dental clearance becomes a much higher-stakes decision.

## How this applies in practice

- Take a panoramic radiograph; look for unrestorable teeth, retained roots in symptomatic areas, periapical pathology, deep periodontal pockets.
- Extract anything with poor prognosis now, not later. Refit ill-fitting dentures so they don't traumatise mucosa.
- Treat active periodontal disease and deliver oral hygiene instruction.
- Time-pressure is real for cancer patients: oncology often will not delay chemotherapy for dental work, so flag urgency to the medical team and, where the patient is medically complex, consult an oral surgery or special care specialist for joint planning.
- Document that the patient was advised before treatment started.
