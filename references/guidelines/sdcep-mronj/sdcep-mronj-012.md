## The recommendation

Give personalised preventive advice to at-risk patients, emphasising healthy diet with reduced sugary snacks and drinks, excellent oral hygiene, fluoride toothpaste and mouthwash, smoking cessation, and limited alcohol. Advise oral bisphosphonate patients not to hold the tablet in the mouth.

## Why

Prevention is the only intervention that reduces the future need for the procedures (extractions, periodontal surgery) that actually cause MRONJ. Smoking and heavy alcohol independently impair mucosal and bony healing. Holding an oral bisphosphonate tablet against the mucosa allows direct chemical irritation and can cause ulceration — itself a risk factor for triggering MRONJ in already at-risk bone.

## How this applies in practice

- Specifically check that the patient takes oral bisphosphonates with a full glass of water, sitting or standing upright, and does not lie down for at least 30 minutes after — this is the manufacturer's advice and protects both upper GI tract and oral mucosa.
- High-fluoride toothpaste (2,800 or 5,000 ppm) is worth prescribing for those with raised caries risk to head off future extraction needs.
- Smoking cessation referral is part of the dental consultation, not an optional extra.
- Recall and reinforce — these patients benefit from shorter recall intervals than the population average.
