## The recommendation

Use fluconazole 50 mg daily (200 mg loading dose on day 1) for 7 days for severe or extensive oral candidosis, or when topical treatment is unsuitable; do not prescribe to patients on warfarin or statins.

## Why

Fluconazole is a systemic azole with the same CYP inhibition as miconazole — except that, being given orally for systemic effect, the interaction is even more predictable. It is reserved for severe candidosis because systemic absorption brings hepatic, GI, and interaction risks that topical agents avoid. The standard course is 7 days, with a maximum of 14, after which lack of response means the diagnosis or the patient's underlying immune status need rethinking.

## How this applies in practice

- Use only when miconazole and nystatin have failed or are unsuitable (extensive lesions, immunocompromised patient).
- Loading dose 200 mg, then 50 mg daily for 7 days. Maximum 14 days; refer if no response.
- Same absolute contraindication as miconazole: not with warfarin, not with statins.
- Always look for the underlying cause of recurrent candidosis: poorly controlled diabetes, inhaled steroids without spacer/rinse, ill-fitting dentures, iron/B12/folate deficiency, immunosuppression.
- DPF restricts dental prescribing to 50 mg capsules and 50 mg/5 ml suspension only — other formulations need GMP prescription.
- In children, dose by weight: 6 mg/kg loading, then 3 mg/kg daily.
