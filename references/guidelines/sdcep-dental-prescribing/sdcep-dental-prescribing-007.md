## The recommendation

Routine short courses of dental antibiotics no longer require additional contraceptive precautions for women on combined oral contraceptives, patches, or rings, unless diarrhoea or vomiting occurs.

## Why

The old advice was based on theoretical disruption of the gut flora that recycle oestrogen via enterohepatic circulation. The Faculty of Sexual and Reproductive Healthcare reviewed the evidence and found that non-enzyme-inducing antibiotics — which includes every antibiotic recommended for dental use — do not reduce contraceptive efficacy in practice. The exception is when diarrhoea or vomiting prevents absorption of the pill itself, in which case the standard "missed pill" rules apply.

## How this applies in practice

- Tell the patient she does not need extra precautions just because she is on a dental antibiotic.
- Do warn her that if the antibiotic causes diarrhoea or vomiting, she should follow the missed-pill instructions in the contraceptive leaflet.
- This rule applies to phenoxymethylpenicillin, amoxicillin, metronidazole, clarithromycin, clindamycin, doxycycline — none induce liver enzymes.
- Liver-enzyme inducers (rifampicin, rifabutin) are different and DO reduce pill efficacy, but they are not in dental prescribing.
- Document the conversation — patients still hear the old advice from family and online sources.
