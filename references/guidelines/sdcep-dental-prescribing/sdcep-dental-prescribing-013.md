## The recommendation

Diclofenac is contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, peripheral arterial disease, and mild-to-severe heart failure; avoid in hypertension and in patients on low-dose aspirin.

## Why

Diclofenac has a stronger COX-2 selectivity than ibuprofen, which means it inhibits prostacyclin (vasodilator, antithrombotic) more than thromboxane (platelet activator). The net effect is a small but real increase in arterial thrombotic events — myocardial infarction, stroke — comparable in size to coxibs. In patients who already have vascular disease, that small absolute risk lands on top of a baseline event rate that makes the increase clinically meaningful.

## How this applies in practice

- Default to ibuprofen rather than diclofenac when an NSAID is needed.
- Reserve diclofenac for moderate dental pain in patients with no cardiovascular risk factors who have failed paracetamol + ibuprofen alternation.
- Adult dose: 50 mg TDS for 5 days; not for dental use in children.
- Tablets are enteric-coated — swallow whole, not crushed or chewed.
- Same NSAID GI, renal, anticoagulant, and pregnancy precautions as ibuprofen.
- When in doubt, refer back to paracetamol ± codeine via the GMP rather than diclofenac in a vasculopath.
