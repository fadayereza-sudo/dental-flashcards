## The recommendation

Identify and manage predisposing factors before reaching for an antifungal — inhaled corticosteroids, broad-spectrum antibiotics, cytotoxics, diabetes, nutritional deficiencies, and serious systemic disease all promote oral candidosis.

## Why

Candida is a normal commensal — its overgrowth signals that the host environment has shifted. Treating with an antifungal alone leaves the underlying driver in place, so recurrence is the rule. Inhaled steroids deposit drug on the oral mucosa, suppressing local immunity; antibiotics wipe out competing bacteria; diabetes raises mucosal glucose; haematinic deficiencies blunt mucosal renewal. Fixing the upstream cause does more than any course of miconazole.

## How this applies in practice

- For an inhaled steroid user with candidosis, the first intervention is a spacer device and rinse-and-spit after each inhalation — that often clears it without antifungal at all.
- Check HbA1c history or random glucose if recurrent infection in an apparently well patient.
- Check ferritin, B12, folate when oral candidosis recurs without obvious cause.
- Recurrent or non-responsive infection in an apparently healthy adult is a recognise-and-refer scenario — could be early haematological disease or undiagnosed immune compromise.
- In children, candidosis usually relates to a local factor like an orthodontic appliance — fix that first, antifungal second.
- For severe infection in immunocompromised patients, refer urgently rather than treat in primary care.
