## The recommendation

Sinusitis is generally self-limiting (average 2½ weeks); use local measures first, and only prescribe phenoxymethylpenicillin or doxycycline if symptoms are severe or persistent (purulent discharge or symptoms lasting at least 7 days).

## Why

Most acute sinusitis is viral. Antibiotics shorten duration only marginally and only in bacterial cases, so a 7-day rule selects out the small minority who benefit while sparing the rest from side-effects and resistance. Steam inhalation thins mucus, restores ostial drainage, and is the actual mechanism of recovery in viral sinusitis. Doxycycline is included because it covers the penicillin-allergic patient, though it cannot be used in pregnancy, breastfeeding, or children under 12.

## How this applies in practice

- First-line: steam inhalation (warm not boiling for children) for at least a week.
- If antibiotics are needed: phenoxymethylpenicillin 500 mg QDS for 5 days, OR doxycycline 200 mg on day 1 then 100 mg daily for 4 days.
- Doxycycline binds calcium in growing bone and developing teeth — never under 12, never in pregnancy.
- Doxycycline + warfarin: don't combine; INR rises.
- Doxycycline causes oesophageal irritation — instruct patient to swallow capsules whole with plenty of water, sitting or standing.
- Ephedrine nasal drops are no longer available to prescribe.
