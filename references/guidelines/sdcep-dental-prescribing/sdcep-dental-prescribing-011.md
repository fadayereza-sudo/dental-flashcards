## The recommendation

Avoid ibuprofen in patients with NSAID/aspirin hypersensitivity, active peptic ulcer disease (without PPI cover), or those taking low-dose aspirin or anticoagulants; use with caution in the elderly, asthmatics, pregnancy, and renal/cardiac/hepatic impairment.

## Why

Ibuprofen blocks COX-1 in the stomach (loss of mucosal prostaglandins → ulceration and bleeding), in platelets (impaired aggregation → bleeding), and in renal afferent arterioles (loss of vasodilatory prostaglandins → reduced GFR, sodium retention, raised BP). On top of that, around 10% of asthmatics develop bronchospasm with NSAIDs because shunting arachidonic acid down the leukotriene pathway triggers airway constriction. Each of these mechanisms maps directly to a contraindication.

## How this applies in practice

- Adult dose: 400 mg QDS for 5 days, after food (max 2.4 g/day).
- Always ask about asthma, peptic ulcer, anticoagulants, and current aspirin before prescribing.
- On low-dose aspirin: do not add ibuprofen — it cancels aspirin's cardioprotection and stacks GI risk. If essential, cap at 1.2 g/day with a PPI and liaise with the GMP.
- On warfarin or DOACs: do not prescribe NSAIDs at all.
- Asthma: only use NSAIDs if the patient has tolerated them previously; warn about bronchospasm.
- Elderly: cap ibuprofen at 1.2 g/day, co-prescribe a PPI, and limit course to 5 days.
- Avoid in pregnancy; never use after 30 weeks (ductus arteriosus closure).
