## The recommendation

Do not prescribe miconazole oromucosal gel to patients taking warfarin or statins — sufficient drug is absorbed through topical oral use to cause clinically significant interactions.

## Why

Miconazole is an azole that inhibits cytochrome P450 isoenzymes (CYP2C9 for warfarin, CYP3A4 for statins). Even a "topical" oral gel is partially swallowed and absorbed, reaching enzyme concentrations high enough to push warfarin INR into the bleeding range, and to raise statin levels into rhabdomyolysis territory. The 7-day course is long enough to do real harm. This is the single most-tested dental prescribing pitfall — a "topical" product with systemic consequences.

## How this applies in practice

- Before prescribing miconazole gel for oral candidosis, denture stomatitis, or angular cheilitis, ALWAYS check the medication list for warfarin and any statin (simvastatin, atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, etc.).
- If the patient is on either, switch to nystatin oral suspension (no significant interaction).
- Same warning applies to fluconazole — same enzyme inhibition, same drugs to avoid.
- Adult dose if safe: pea-sized amount QDS after food for 7 days, continued for 7 days after lesion resolves.
- For denture stomatitis, apply to the fitting surface of the upper denture.
- Document the interaction check — case reports of fatal warfarin bleeding from miconazole gel are well-known.
