## The recommendation

For mucosal ulceration and inflammation, use topical corticosteroids tailored to lesion site: beclometasone inhaler (Clenil Modulite 50 mcg) directed onto tongue/accessible lesions, betamethasone 500 mcg tablets dissolved as a mouthwash for extensive lesions, or hydrocortisone 2.5 mg oromucosal tablets dissolved next to the ulcer.

## Why

Topical steroids suppress the local inflammatory response that drives ulcer pain and delays healing, without the systemic exposure of oral prednisolone. Each formulation matches a lesion pattern: the inhaler puts a metered dose onto a discrete lesion, the soluble tablet as a mouthwash coats widespread lesions, the oromucosal tablet dissolves in contact with one lesion. The key safety rule is to control duration so that cumulative dose stays below systemic thresholds.

## How this applies in practice

- Localised ulcer on the tongue or accessible mucosa: Clenil Modulite 50 mcg inhaler, 1–2 puffs directly onto the lesion twice daily.
- Extensive or multiple ulcers (e.g. major aphthae, lichen planus flares): betamethasone 500 mcg soluble tablet dissolved in 10 ml water as a mouthwash, four times daily — spit out, do not swallow.
- Single lesion in reach: hydrocortisone oromucosal 2.5 mg dissolved next to it, four times daily.
- Control duration — chronic use risks oral candidosis and systemic steroid effects.
- If the lesion doesn't respond in two weeks or keeps recurring, look harder: haematinics, coeliac screen, refer for biopsy if anything atypical.
