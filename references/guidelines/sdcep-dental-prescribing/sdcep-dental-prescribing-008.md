## The recommendation

NICE recommends that antibiotic prophylaxis for infective endocarditis should not be used routinely in patients undergoing dental procedures; for a very small number of high-risk patients it may be considered as non-routine management in consultation with the patient's cardiologist.

## Why

The cumulative bacteraemia from daily activities (toothbrushing, chewing, flossing) vastly outweighs the bacteraemia from a single dental procedure. Routine prophylaxis exposes thousands of patients to antibiotic risk — anaphylaxis, C. difficile, resistance — to prevent a tiny number of theoretical cases of infective endocarditis. There is also no evidence of benefit in patients with prosthetic joints, despite older orthopaedic teaching.

## How this applies in practice

- Default position: no prophylaxis, even in patients with valve disease or previous endocarditis.
- "Non-routine" use is reserved for the highest-risk subgroup (e.g. previous IE episode, prosthetic valve, certain congenital heart disease) and only after a documented joint discussion with the patient's cardiologist.
- Prosthetic joints: no prophylaxis indicated — do not let the orthopaedic team's old letter sway you.
- Reinforce daily oral hygiene relentlessly in at-risk patients — that is the actual protective intervention.
- Document the no-prophylaxis decision and the cardiology liaison if any.
