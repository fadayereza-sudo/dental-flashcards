## The recommendation

Treat necrotising ulcerative gingivitis and pericoronitis first with local measures (scaling for NUG, irrigation and debridement for pericoronitis); add metronidazole 400 mg three times daily for 3 days only if there is systemic involvement or persistent swelling.

## Why

These infections are anaerobic biofilm diseases — the pathology is the bacterial mat sitting on the gingiva or under the operculum, not a deep tissue infection. Removing the biofilm mechanically lets the host clear the rest. Antibiotics on their own, with the biofilm still in place, neither cure the lesion nor relieve the pain. The course is shorter than for an abscess (3 days, not 5) because once mechanical debridement has been done, only a brief antimicrobial knock-down is needed.

## How this applies in practice

- Always do the local treatment first — limited debridement if the patient cannot tolerate full scaling acutely.
- Reserve antibiotics for systemic features (fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy) or persistent swelling despite local measures.
- Metronidazole is first choice; amoxicillin 500 mg TDS for 3 days is the alternative.
- After the acute episode, treat the underlying cause: oral hygiene reinforcement, smoking cessation for NUG; assess the lower 8 for repeat pericoronitis.
- Same metronidazole rules: no alcohol, not with warfarin.
