## The recommendation

Treat herpes simplex (primary or recurrent intra-oral) with symptomatic relief and antimicrobial mouthwash; reserve systemic aciclovir 200 mg five times daily for 5 days for immunocompromised patients or severe infection in non-immunocompromised patients.

## Why

Most herpetic gingivostomatitis is self-limiting in immunocompetent hosts, and aciclovir works only on actively replicating virus, so once vesicles have fully formed the drug shortens duration only slightly. The benefit is bigger when started early or when host immunity is the rate-limiting factor — hence the focus on severe and immunocompromised cases. Aciclovir is selectively activated by viral thymidine kinase, which is why it kills herpes-infected cells while sparing healthy tissue.

## How this applies in practice

- Local measures: chlorhexidine 0.2% mouthwash or hydrogen peroxide 6% mouthwash, soft diet, plenty of fluids, regular paracetamol.
- Antimicrobial mouthwash matters because severe pain prevents brushing and secondary bacterial infection sets in.
- Systemic aciclovir is for: immunocompromised patient with any herpes infection, or non-immunocompromised patient with severe primary gingivostomatitis.
- Adult dose: 200 mg five times daily for 5 days; doubled in immunocompromised patients.
- Refer immunocompromised patients with severe infection to hospital.
- For herpes labialis, aciclovir 5% cream applied at the prodromal stage — once the vesicle is fully formed, the topical does little.
- For herpes zoster (shingles): aciclovir within 72 hours reduces post-herpetic neuralgia; refer all to GMP/specialist.
