## The recommendation

In adult anaphylaxis, lay the patient flat and raise the feet, give 100% oxygen at 15 L/min, and inject adrenaline 0.5 ml of 1:1000 intramuscularly, repeating after 5 minutes if there is no improvement; call an ambulance first and expect to transfer.

## Why

Anaphylaxis kills by airway oedema and circulatory collapse. Adrenaline reverses both: alpha-1 effects vasoconstrict to restore blood pressure and reduce mucosal oedema, beta-1 effects increase cardiac output, beta-2 effects bronchodilate. IM into the mid-anterolateral thigh gives rapid absorption and a peak serum level within minutes — faster than subcutaneous, safer than IV outside a critical care setting. Laying the patient flat with legs raised uses gravity to maintain venous return while the drug takes effect.

## How this applies in practice

- Recognise: upper airway stridor/laryngeal oedema, wheeze, tachycardia >110, hypotension, rash, abdominal pain, sense of impending doom.
- Adult adrenaline dose: 0.5 ml of 1:1000 IM, repeat every 5 minutes if needed.
- Paediatric adrenaline doses: 6 months–5 years 0.15 ml; 6–11 years 0.3 ml; 12–17 years 0.5 ml (use 0.3 ml if small or prepubertal).
- IM into the outer thigh; do not delay for venous access.
- Remove the trigger (suction out the agent if it's in the mouth).
- If cardiac arrest follows: BLS and early defibrillation.
- Transfer to hospital every time — biphasic reactions occur hours later.
