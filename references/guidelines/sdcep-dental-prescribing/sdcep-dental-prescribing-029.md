## The recommendation

Commit to memory the dental prescribing interactions most likely to harm: NSAIDs with warfarin/DOACs, azole antifungals (miconazole, fluconazole) with warfarin or statins, macrolides (clarithromycin, erythromycin) with statins or warfarin, metronidazole with warfarin, and NSAIDs with low-dose aspirin.

## Why

These are the interactions that appear repeatedly in dental case reports of avoidable harm. Each has a clean mechanism: NSAIDs raise bleeding risk additively with any anticoagulant; azoles and macrolides inhibit CYP enzymes that metabolise warfarin and statins, producing bleeding or rhabdomyolysis; metronidazole has a specific warfarin interaction; NSAIDs cancel aspirin's antiplatelet effect by blocking the same COX-1 site. The patient depends on the dentist spotting the combination before the pharmacist does.

## How this applies in practice

- Warfarin — avoid NSAIDs, aspirin, metronidazole, clarithromycin, erythromycin, miconazole, fluconazole, doxycycline. Safer: paracetamol, phenoxymethylpenicillin or amoxicillin (recheck INR 4–7 days in), nystatin, aciclovir.
- Statins — avoid clarithromycin, erythromycin, miconazole, fluconazole. Safer: paracetamol, penicillins, metronidazole, nystatin.
- Low-dose aspirin — avoid ibuprofen and diclofenac. Safer: paracetamol alone.
- Lithium, methotrexate, SSRIs — avoid NSAIDs.
- Clopidogrel — avoid aspirin.
- Systemic corticosteroids on board — NSAIDs only with PPI cover.
- ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, diuretics — NSAIDs only for ≤5 days, monitor BP.
- The single best habit: update the medication list every visit and check it before any prescription.
