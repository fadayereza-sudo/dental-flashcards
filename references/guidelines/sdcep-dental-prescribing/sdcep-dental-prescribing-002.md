## The recommendation

For a dental abscess with spreading infection, prescribe phenoxymethylpenicillin 500 mg four times daily for 5 days as first-line; it is preferred over amoxicillin because of its narrower spectrum.

## Why

Oral infections are caused mainly by streptococci and oral anaerobes — both are exquisitely sensitive to penicillin. A narrower-spectrum penicillin (phenoxymethylpenicillin) kills these targets without disturbing the wider gut and oral flora, so it produces less collateral selection pressure for resistant organisms and less C. difficile risk than broader options. Amoxicillin reaches higher serum levels and is better absorbed but its broader spectrum does the same job for dental infection while doing more ecological harm.

## How this applies in practice

- Adult dose: 500 mg four times daily for 5 days (40 x 250 mg tablets, 2 four times daily).
- Use four-times-daily dosing — short half-life means missed doses leave sub-therapeutic gaps.
- Switch to amoxicillin (1 g three times daily) only when compliance with QDS dosing is unrealistic; the three-times-daily regimen is easier to follow.
- Never prescribe to anyone with a history of anaphylaxis, urticaria, or rash immediately after penicillin — risk of immediate hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis.
- Remember to check for warfarin: penicillins can potentiate the anticoagulant effect; recheck INR 4–7 days into the course.
