## The recommendation

For mild to moderate dental pain, prescribe paracetamol 1 g four times daily for 5 days (maximum 4 g per 24 hours); refer any patient who has taken more than the recommended daily dose AND ≥75 mg/kg in 24 hours to the emergency department.

## Why

Paracetamol is the analgesic of choice for most dental pain because it controls pain without affecting platelets, gastric mucosa, or renal function, and is safe in almost every comorbid group. The downside is a narrow toxic window: a saturated glucuronidation pathway pushes paracetamol into oxidative metabolism, producing NAPQI, which depletes glutathione and causes hepatic necrosis. The damage is silent for the first 4 to 6 days and can be fatal — so any therapeutic excess needs hospital assessment whether the patient feels well or not.

## How this applies in practice

- Adult dose: 1 g (2 x 500 mg) QDS, maximum 4 g/day. Send 40 tablets for a 5-day course.
- Always ask what over-the-counter preparations the patient is already taking — paracetamol is hidden in cold remedies, co-codamol, and combination products.
- Therapeutic excess threshold: more than 4 g/day AND ≥75 mg/kg in 24 hours. That patient goes to A&E for assessment regardless of symptoms.
- In hepatic disease, alcohol dependence, malnutrition, or weight under 50 kg, reduce the dose and use clinical judgement.
- In pregnancy and breastfeeding, paracetamol is the analgesic of choice.
