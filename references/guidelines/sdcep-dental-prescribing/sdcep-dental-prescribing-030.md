## The recommendation

For children, dose by actual body weight where possible; use the BNFC mean-weight-by-age table (6 months 7.6 kg, 1 year 9 kg, 3 years 14 kg, 5 years 18 kg, 7 years 23 kg, 10 years 32 kg, 12 years 39 kg) only when the child cannot be weighed.

## Why

Paediatric pharmacokinetics vary with body size, not age — a slight 7-year-old and a heavy 7-year-old may differ by 50% in weight, and therefore in the right dose of any mg/kg drug. Age-based dosing is a pragmatic fallback when no scales are available, but it assumes an average build. Under-dosing risks therapeutic failure (undertreated infection, unresolved pain); over-dosing risks toxicity (paracetamol-induced hepatic damage, NSAID renal injury). Weighing the child is the small step that prevents both.

## How this applies in practice

- Put scales in the surgery or borrow the GMP weight from the notes if recent.
- Use mg/kg dosing for paracetamol, ibuprofen, amoxicillin, metronidazole, clarithromycin, aciclovir — all the paediatric-relevant drugs.
- Recalculate when the child's actual weight arrives if you used a mean from the table.
- Practical maxima matter: paracetamol max 4 g/day (or 75 mg/kg), ibuprofen max 30 mg/kg/day.
- Sugar-free preparations preferred where available — long courses of sugary suspensions cause caries in the patient you are treating.
- When in doubt about a paediatric dose, use the BNFC (www.medicinescomplete.com), not adult tables.
- Certain drugs have hard age cut-offs: doxycycline/tetracyclines <12, aspirin <16, 2,800 ppm toothpaste <10, 5,000 ppm toothpaste <16, chlorhexidine only where indicated.
