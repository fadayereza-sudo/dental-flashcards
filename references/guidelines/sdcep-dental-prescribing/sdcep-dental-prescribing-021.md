## The recommendation

Chlorhexidine 0.2% mouthwash, 10 ml for 1 minute twice daily; advise at least a 30-minute interval between the mouthwash and toothpaste, and note that it can be diluted 1:1 with water without loss of efficacy.

## Why

Chlorhexidine is a cationic antiseptic that binds negatively charged bacterial membranes and is retained on oral surfaces for hours — "substantivity" — which is why it controls plaque and secondary infection when brushing is not possible. The drawback is that anionic detergents in toothpaste (sodium lauryl sulfate) inactivate chlorhexidine on contact, cancelling the benefit. Separating the two by 30 minutes allows the toothpaste to clear before the chlorhexidine binds.

## How this applies in practice

- Use it when pain or surgery limits mechanical cleaning: post-surgical, acute ulcerative lesions, severe HSV, oral mucositis, fixed orthodontics during flare-ups.
- Not a long-term replacement for brushing — causes reversible brown staining and taste disturbance with prolonged use.
- Alcohol-free versions are preferred where licensed, especially for mucositis patients.
- Tell the patient: rinse with the mouthwash, THEN wait 30 minutes before brushing (or vice versa). Do not rinse immediately after brushing.
- Dilution tip: 1:1 with water still works and helps patients with stinging from the standard strength.
- Use until the underlying lesion resolves and good oral hygiene is possible, then stop — no indefinite courses.
