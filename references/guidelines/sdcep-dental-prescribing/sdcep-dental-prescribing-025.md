## The recommendation

In renal or hepatic impairment, use paracetamol with caution and dose reduction; avoid NSAIDs altogether in severe renal or severe hepatic impairment, and use with caution and monitoring in mild to moderate disease.

## Why

NSAIDs impair renal function by removing the vasodilatory prostaglandins that protect GFR — in a kidney already struggling, that can tip the patient into acute-on-chronic failure. In liver disease, reduced drug clearance pushes serum levels up, while reduced clotting factor synthesis compounds the GI bleeding risk. Paracetamol is metabolised to the hepatotoxic NAPQI, so hepatic impairment narrows its safe window; renal failure slows its conjugate excretion.

## How this applies in practice

- Severe renal impairment: no NSAIDs. Paracetamol with dose reduction — liaise with the GMP.
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: avoid NSAIDs if possible; if unavoidable, monitor U&Es 1–2 weeks after starting.
- Severe hepatic impairment: no NSAIDs; paracetamol with caution, often reduced dose.
- Mild to moderate hepatic impairment: NSAIDs with caution, dose reduction, monitor LFTs.
- Dehydration is the common denominator that makes both kidney and GI problems worse — avoid NSAIDs in the dehydrated patient.
- Alcohol dependence, chronic alcoholism, malnutrition: reduce paracetamol dose and avoid NSAIDs.
- When in doubt, stick to paracetamol and discuss stronger options with the patient's GMP.
