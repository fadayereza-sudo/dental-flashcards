## The recommendation

Paracetamol is the analgesic of choice in pregnancy and breastfeeding; avoid NSAIDs in pregnancy (never use after 30 weeks) and prefer ibuprofen at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time if an NSAID is essential in breastfeeding.

## Why

NSAIDs in the third trimester can cause premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus and oligohydramnios, and are associated with fetal renal impairment — so the absolute cut-off is 30 weeks. Earlier in pregnancy they also carry miscarriage risk. Aspirin is contraindicated in the third trimester and while breastfeeding (Reye's syndrome through breast milk). Paracetamol crosses the placenta but has no known teratogenic effect at therapeutic doses, and enters breast milk at levels far below infant therapeutic doses — which is why it is the default.

## How this applies in practice

- Pregnant patient with dental pain: paracetamol 1 g QDS is first and usually only choice.
- If paracetamol is not enough, liaise with the GMP or obstetrics before adding anything — don't add codeine or ibuprofen on your own.
- No NSAIDs after 30 weeks under any circumstances without specialist advice and fetal monitoring.
- Breastfeeding mother: paracetamol first; if NSAID essential, ibuprofen at lowest effective dose for shortest time.
- Aspirin: avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Metronidazole and clarithromycin: avoid in pregnancy; amoxicillin and phenoxymethylpenicillin are the safest antibiotic choices.
- Always document that pregnancy/breastfeeding status was checked before prescribing.
