## The recommendation

Do not prescribe aspirin to children under 16 (Reye's syndrome risk) and do not prescribe it after dental extraction or other minor surgery; otherwise its NSAID precautions match ibuprofen's.

## Why

Aspirin in viral illness in under-16s is associated with Reye's syndrome — acute hepatic failure with encephalopathy, often fatal — so its dental use in children is off-limits. After extraction, aspirin's irreversible inhibition of platelet COX-1 means platelet aggregation cannot recover until new platelets are made (5–10 days), so the socket continues to bleed long after the drug has cleared. Other NSAIDs are reversible inhibitors and recover function as the drug is eliminated.

## How this applies in practice

- Never prescribe aspirin under 16 — use paracetamol ± ibuprofen instead.
- Never prescribe aspirin for post-extraction pain or post-surgical pain — bleeding socket risk.
- For other dental pain in adults: dispersible 300 mg, 600–900 mg up to four times daily, after food, max 4 g/day.
- Same NSAID contraindications: peptic ulcer, anticoagulants, asthma with prior NSAID-induced bronchospasm.
- On clopidogrel: do not add aspirin. On warfarin: do not prescribe aspirin.
- A patient already taking low-dose aspirin for cardioprotection should not be given an additional NSAID for dental pain.
