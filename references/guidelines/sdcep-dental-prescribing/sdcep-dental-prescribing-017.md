## The recommendation

Use nystatin oral suspension 100,000 units/ml, 1 ml four times daily for 7 days, when miconazole and fluconazole are contraindicated.

## Why

Nystatin is a polyene antifungal that binds ergosterol in the fungal cell membrane, creating pores that lyse the cell. It is essentially not absorbed from the GI tract, so it has no systemic effect and no clinically significant drug interactions — which is what makes it the go-to for any patient on warfarin or statins. The trade-off is that effectiveness depends on contact time with the lesion, so the technique of holding the suspension in the mouth matters more than for the other antifungals.

## How this applies in practice

- Reach for nystatin first whenever the patient is on warfarin, a statin, or any other CYP3A4-sensitive drug — it is the only safe choice.
- Tell the patient: rinse the suspension around the mouth, then hold it near the lesion for 5 minutes before swallowing.
- Continue use for 48 hours after lesions have healed (vs 7 days for miconazole) — different kinetics, shorter required overhang.
- Children: same regimen as adults.
- Sugar-free preparation is available.
- If the lesion does not respond, the issue is usually compliance (swallowed too quickly) or an unaddressed predisposing factor — not nystatin failure.
