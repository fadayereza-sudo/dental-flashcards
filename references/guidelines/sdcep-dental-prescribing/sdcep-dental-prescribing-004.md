## The recommendation

Restrict second-line antibiotics (clindamycin, co-amoxiclav, clarithromycin) to severe infections that have not responded to first-line treatment; check the diagnosis and consider specialist input before prescribing them.

## Why

Broad-spectrum antibiotics are associated with the rise in Clostridium difficile infection in both primary and secondary care. The empirical use of second-line agents offers no advantage over first-line drugs for most dental patients but contributes disproportionately to antimicrobial resistance and to severe colitis. If a patient has not responded, the most likely explanation is incomplete drainage or wrong diagnosis — escalating the spectrum without re-examining the cause masks the real problem.

## How this applies in practice

- "Not responding" means review the abscess and check for retained pus, not write a stronger prescription.
- Clindamycin causes antibiotic-associated colitis more often than other dental antibiotics — warn the patient to stop and seek help if diarrhoea develops.
- Co-amoxiclav: cholestatic jaundice risk, more common in men over 65; same penicillin-allergy contraindications as amoxicillin.
- Clarithromycin: do not co-prescribe with statins (rhabdomyolysis), warfarin (raised INR), or in pregnancy; cautious in QT-prolonging conditions.
- When in doubt, refer rather than escalate empirically.
