## The recommendation

Every dental practice should stock: adrenaline 1:1000 (1 ml ampoules or 0.5 ml prefilled syringes), aspirin 300 mg dispersible, glucagon 1 mg IM, GTN spray 400 mcg, midazolam oromucosal 5 mg/ml, oral glucose, oxygen (two size D, or two CD, or one E), and salbutamol inhaler.

## Why

Every agent on this list maps to an emergency the practice will, sooner or later, face: adrenaline for anaphylaxis, aspirin for suspected MI, glucagon for hypoglycaemia when IV access or oral glucose fails, GTN for angina, midazolam for status epilepticus, glucose for hypoglycaemia in a conscious patient, oxygen for everything hypoxic, salbutamol for acute asthma. The Resuscitation Council guidance is that any missing drug means the team cannot respond to a predictable emergency — and dental emergencies are time-critical.

## How this applies in practice

- EpiPens and other patient auto-injectors contain less adrenaline than the emergency dose — the practice kit needs 1 ml ampoules or 0.5 ml pre-filled syringes specifically.
- Oxygen supply must last long enough for ambulance arrival: 15 L/min flow needs at least 30 minutes (one CD cylinder or two D cylinders; rural practice keep two E cylinders).
- Midazolam oromucosal is a Schedule 3 controlled drug — exempt from safe-custody but must be denatured before disposal; keep invoices 2 years.
- Defibrillator must be available and the team trained to use it.
- Antihistamines (cetirizine, chlorphenamine, loratadine) are optional for mild allergy — not part of the core kit.
- Check expiry dates monthly and replace used items immediately, not at the next audit.
