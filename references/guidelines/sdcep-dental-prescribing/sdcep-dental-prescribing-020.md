## The recommendation

For high-caries-risk adults and children with true salivary deficit, prescribe sodium fluoride toothpaste 2,800 ppm (10 years and over) or 5,000 ppm (16 years and over); 5,000 ppm is brushed for 3 minutes three times daily, 2,800 ppm twice daily.

## Why

Caries is a balance between demineralisation and remineralisation. In the high-risk patient — head and neck radiotherapy, Sjögren's, polypharmacy-induced xerostomia, multiple recent restorations — the demineralisation rate is high enough that standard 1,450 ppm cannot keep up. Doubling or tripling the fluoride concentration drives a higher gradient into the enamel surface, accelerating remineralisation and inhibiting bacterial acid production. Both products are licensed medicines (POM), so they need a written prescription.

## How this applies in practice

- 2,800 ppm: ≥10 years; brush with 1 cm for 1 minute, twice daily, after meals; do not eat, drink, or rinse for 30 minutes.
- 5,000 ppm: ≥16 years (because of the swallowing/poisoning risk in younger children); brush with 2 cm for 3 minutes, three times daily.
- Both: spit, do not rinse — preserves the topical fluoride film.
- Indications: high caries risk, dry mouth (radiotherapy, Sjögren's, polypharmacy), exposed root surfaces, recurrent caries.
- These are medicines, not cosmetics — for the named patient only.
- Pair with diet advice and saliva substitutes; high-fluoride paste alone cannot save a high-sugar diet.
