## The recommendation

In patients with previous or active peptic ulcer disease who need an NSAID for dental pain, co-prescribe a proton pump inhibitor (lansoprazole 15 mg or omeprazole 20 mg once daily) for the duration of the analgesic course.

## Why

NSAIDs cause GI ulceration by suppressing the gastric mucosal prostaglandins that maintain mucus, bicarbonate, and blood flow. PPIs irreversibly block H+/K+ ATPase on parietal cells, dropping acid secretion and removing the corrosive component of ulcer formation. Co-prescription brings the bleeding-risk back close to baseline in a patient already at risk, allowing necessary analgesia without precipitating a haemorrhage.

## How this applies in practice

- Prescribe the PPI for the same number of days as the NSAID, not "as needed."
- Lansoprazole 15 mg OD or omeprazole 20 mg OD for 5 days is the standard add-on.
- High-risk groups who get a PPI alongside any NSAID: previous GI ulcer or bleed, age over 65, on anticoagulant/SSRI/corticosteroid, heavy alcohol use, serious comorbidity.
- On chronic NSAIDs from the GMP: don't add a dental NSAID — liaise with the GMP instead.
- If the patient is not yet on a PPI but should be, contact their GMP to arrange one rather than starting it yourself outside their usual care.
- For the elderly, co-prescribe a PPI and cap ibuprofen at 1.2 g/day.
