## The recommendation

For patients on anticoagulants (warfarin, dabigatran, apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban), use paracetamol rather than aspirin or NSAIDs for dental pain; avoid NSAIDs in bleeding disorders.

## Why

NSAIDs irritate the gastric mucosa and inhibit platelet aggregation, so stacking them on top of anticoagulation multiplies bleeding risk — both GI and post-extraction. Aspirin adds irreversible platelet blockade. Paracetamol is the safe analgesic because it has no effect on platelets and no direct gastric injury. The one caveat is that regular paracetamol can itself raise INR in patients on warfarin by a small amount, so the team needs to know.

## How this applies in practice

- Default: paracetamol 1 g QDS for any anticoagulated patient.
- Do not add ibuprofen, aspirin, or diclofenac to a warfarin/DOAC regimen.
- If the patient is using paracetamol regularly, flag it to their usual clinician so the next INR check is not a surprise.
- For bleeding disorders (haemophilia, von Willebrand, clotting factor deficiency), consult the patient's haematologist before any NSAID.
- If pain control absolutely requires an NSAID and there is no alternative, liaise with the GMP, cover with a PPI, and accept the bleeding risk with eyes open.
- Antibiotic rules that compound with warfarin: no metronidazole, no clarithromycin, no fluconazole, no miconazole, no doxycycline.
