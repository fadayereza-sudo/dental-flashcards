## The recommendation

Where paracetamol or ibuprofen alone is insufficient, alternate them — for example ibuprofen first, then paracetamol two hours later, repeating — using the normal daily doses of each.

## Why

Paracetamol and ibuprofen have different mechanisms (central COX inhibition for paracetamol, peripheral COX-1/2 inhibition for ibuprofen) and different elimination half-lives. Staggering them keeps continuous analgesic cover from one drug while the other sits at trough, which controls breakthrough pain and pyrexia without exceeding the licensed maximum daily dose of either. This is more effective than the temptation to keep dose-stacking a single agent into the toxic range.

## How this applies in practice

- Tell the patient: ibuprofen 400 mg now, paracetamol 1 g in 2 hours, ibuprofen again 2 hours later, and so on.
- Each drug stays within its own daily maximum (paracetamol 4 g, ibuprofen 2.4 g).
- This is the SDCEP "step up" before reaching for opioids — codeine is rarely the right next step in dental pain because it adds nausea, constipation, and dependence without much added analgesia for inflammatory tooth pain.
- If alternation isn't enough, the issue is usually that the cause hasn't been addressed (drain, extirpate, extract) — fix the source rather than escalate the analgesic.
- Review NSAID contraindications before adding ibuprofen to a paracetamol-only regimen.
