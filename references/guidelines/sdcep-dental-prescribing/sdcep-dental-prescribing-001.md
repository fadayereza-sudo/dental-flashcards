## The recommendation

Treat dental abscesses in the first instance using local measures to achieve drainage, with removal of the cause if possible; antibiotics are required only in cases of spreading infection or systemic involvement.

## Why

A dental abscess is a contained collection of pus and necrotic tissue. Antibiotics cannot reach the centre of an abscess in useful concentration because there is no blood supply to the necrotic core, so the only treatment that reliably resolves the infection is to drain the pus and remove the cause (extract the tooth or open the canals). Adding an antibiotic when drainage has been achieved gives no benefit, exposes the patient to side-effects, and drives antimicrobial resistance. The duration is usually 5 days because longer courses encourage resistant organisms without changing outcomes.

## How this applies in practice

- Drainage by extraction or root-canal opening is the treatment, not the adjunct.
- For pus in soft tissue, attempt drainage by incision.
- Reach for antibiotics only when there are signs of spread: cellulitis, lymphadenopathy, fever, malaise.
- Transfer to hospital immediately for trismus, floor-of-mouth swelling, or any difficulty breathing — these signal Ludwig's angina or airway compromise.
- Default course length is 5 days. Do not extend "to be safe."
