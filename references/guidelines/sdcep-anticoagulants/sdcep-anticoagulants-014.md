## The recommendation

Contact the patient's prescribing clinician — usually the haematologist or cardiologist rather than the GP — when there is uncertainty about the medication, the medical condition, or the impact of either on bleeding risk. For advice on the procedure itself, liaise with a senior dental colleague in your own setting first.

## Why

Two different questions have two different owners. "What is this drug and can it be modified?" is a medical question that belongs to the person who prescribed it; the GP often will not have the detail that the specialist has. "Can I do this extraction safely?" is a dental question and the right first port of call is a senior colleague in your practice or local service — faster than the hospital and usually sufficient. Referring dental patients into secondary care should be the exception, not the default; by following the guidance most can be treated in primary care. Making the wrong call — a medical question to a dental specialist or vice versa — wastes the appointment and delays the patient.

## How this applies in practice

- Drug identity or dose unclear, LMWH dose bracket unclear, combination therapy, INR persistently over 4: phone the prescriber first.
- Nervous about an oral-surgery technique rather than the patient's physiology: ask a senior colleague in practice, a dentist with enhanced skills, or a special-care/oral surgery senior dental officer.
- Any referral letter to secondary dental care must name the anticoagulant(s), the dose, the indication, the prescribing service and the last INR where relevant.
- While waiting for advice, use interim non-invasive measures to relieve pain rather than guessing.
- Before sending anyone to a hospital clinic, phone the service first to confirm it's the right destination — this avoids the most common wasted referral.
