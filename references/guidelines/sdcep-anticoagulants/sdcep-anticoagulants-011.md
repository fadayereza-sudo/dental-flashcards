## The recommendation

Advise the anticoagulated dental patient to take paracetamol, unless contraindicated, for post-operative pain relief rather than NSAIDs such as aspirin, ibuprofen, diclofenac or naproxen.

## Why

NSAIDs impair platelet aggregation on top of whatever anticoagulant or antiplatelet drug the patient already takes, and they increase bleeding risk for DOACs, warfarin, LMWHs and every antiplatelet drug on the list. Ibuprofen also blunts the antiplatelet effect of aspirin if taken regularly, which can raise thromboembolic risk. Paracetamol gives adequate analgesia for routine post-extraction pain without any effect on coagulation, so it is the default. Co-codamol is a reasonable step-up if paracetamol alone is insufficient.

## How this applies in practice

- Hand the post-op sheet with paracetamol dosing written on it; say the words out loud: "no ibuprofen, no aspirin, no Voltarol".
- Check what they already have at home — patients often reach for Nurofen automatically.
- If pain is not controlled by paracetamol, add codeine-based analgesia rather than adding an NSAID.
- If an NSAID is genuinely needed (rare in routine dental post-op), keep the course as short as possible and consider gastroprotection — and only after discussion with the prescribing clinician for high-risk patients.
- Vascular-disease patients already have a reason to avoid NSAIDs irrespective of anticoagulation.
