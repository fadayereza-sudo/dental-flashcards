## The recommendation

At every visit, ask the patient about their current and planned use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs, other prescribed and non-prescribed medications, relevant medical conditions, bleeding history, and whether their anticoagulant course is lifelong or time-limited.

## Why

Overall bleeding risk is not set by the anticoagulant alone. Liver disease, chronic renal failure, haematological malignancy, recent chemo or radiotherapy, advanced heart failure, inherited coagulation disorders (haemophilia, vWD), platelet disorders, and connective tissue disorders like EDS all add risk. NSAIDs (including OTC aspirin and ibuprofen), SSRIs/SNRIs, carbamazepine, cytotoxics, biologics, and herbal agents (St John's Wort, Ginkgo, garlic) can all push a single-agent anticoagulation into effective dual therapy or impair coagulation by another route. The patient's own bleeding history — bruising, prolonged bleeding from previous extractions — is often the best predictor of what today will be like. A time-limited course (e.g. post-orthopaedic LMWH for 6 weeks) lets you delay non-urgent invasive work rather than manage it.

## How this applies in practice

- Every MH check: "Are you on any blood-thinning tablets or injections?" Patients often do not know aspirin or clopidogrel counts.
- Follow up with "Do you buy any painkillers yourself?" — OTC NSAID use quietly converts monotherapy to dual therapy.
- Ask "Have you ever bled for a long time after a tooth out or a cut?" Positive history is a red flag regardless of drug.
- If the patient has liver, kidney, marrow or haematological disease, speak to the prescribing clinician before invasive work — the drug risk compounds with the condition.
- If the drug is time-limited, defer non-urgent invasive treatment until they finish; interim pain relief only.
- Confirm details by phone before the appointment when you can — saves wasted chair time if something has changed.
