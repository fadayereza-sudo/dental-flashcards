## The recommendation

For a patient on a DOAC who needs a dental procedure with a higher risk of bleeding complications, advise them to miss (apixaban or dabigatran) or delay (rivaroxaban or edoxaban) their morning dose on the day of the procedure.

## Why

Apixaban and dabigatran are twice-daily drugs: skipping the morning dose leaves the patient at a trough at the time of surgery without losing therapeutic cover for long. Rivaroxaban and edoxaban are once-daily drugs usually taken in the morning: delaying (not missing) the dose until after haemostasis gives the same trough-level advantage while keeping daily exposure intact. The DOACs' short half-lives make this brief dose adjustment effective quickly and let anticoagulation return to therapeutic range soon after restart, minimising thromboembolic risk. Direct evidence is limited, so this is a conditional recommendation — discuss it with the patient when consenting.

## How this applies in practice

- Complex or multiple extractions, any flap surgery, biopsies and gingival recontouring trigger the dose change.
- Apixaban or dabigatran: skip the morning dose only. Take the evening dose as usual, no earlier than 4 hours after haemostasis.
- Rivaroxaban or edoxaban (morning dosing): delay that morning dose until at least 4 hours after haemostasis, then the next dose the following morning as usual.
- Rivaroxaban or edoxaban (evening dosing): no change needed — just take that evening dose no earlier than 4 hours after haemostasis.
- Because it is only ever one missed or delayed dose, you usually do not need to phone the prescriber — but do if you or the patient have any concerns.
- Stage multi-quadrant surgery across separate visits rather than skip multiple doses.
- Tell the patient not to miss subsequent doses unless bleeding forces it, and to call the practice if bleeding occurs after restart.
- If a higher-risk procedure becomes urgent and the patient has already taken the morning dose, delay the procedure later in the day if possible to let the peak pass.
