## The recommendation

Avoid miconazole and metronidazole in patients on warfarin or another vitamin K antagonist where possible; if any antibiotic is prescribed, warn the patient that their INR may rise and tell the anticoagulation service so monitoring can be intensified.

## Why

Metronidazole and the azole antifungals miconazole and fluconazole produce a clinically important rise in INR through CYP inhibition — miconazole even as an oral gel can tip a warfarin patient into serious over-anticoagulation. Penicillins, macrolides, clindamycin and tetracyclines enhance warfarin's effect in a minority of patients, partly through gut-flora changes that reduce vitamin K synthesis. On top of that, the underlying infection that required the antibiotic can itself alter coagulation, so the INR shift can be hard to attribute. The rule is not "never prescribe" but "do not prescribe blindly": the infection still has to be treated, but the patient and their anticoagulation team need to know.

## How this applies in practice

- Warfarin patient with a dental abscess: first choice is drainage and removal of cause; if an antibiotic is needed, avoid metronidazole where another agent covers the bug.
- Oral candidosis in a warfarin patient: use nystatin, not miconazole (Daktarin) gel. Avoid fluconazole.
- Whenever you do prescribe an antibiotic to a warfarin patient, tell the anticoagulation service and warn the patient to watch for bleeding/bruising and to seek earlier INR retesting.
- Clopidogrel patients: avoid fluconazole and omeprazole (both reduce antiplatelet effect); avoid clarithromycin in ticagrelor patients.
- Carbamazepine reduces DOAC plasma levels and should usually be avoided alongside apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban or ticagrelor; use with caution with edoxaban.
- If prescribing erythromycin for an edoxaban patient, liaise with the prescriber for a dose reduction.
- Always cross-check the BNF interactions checker — this list is not exhaustive.
