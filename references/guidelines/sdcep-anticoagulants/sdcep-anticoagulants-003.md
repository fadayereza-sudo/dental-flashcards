## The recommendation

For every patient on an anticoagulant or antiplatelet drug who has a procedure likely to cause bleeding, achieve haemostasis locally before discharge, and strongly consider suturing and packing the socket with a haemostatic material such as oxidised cellulose or collagen sponge.

## Why

The published incidence of post-op bleeding requiring further measures in continued-anticoagulation patients is about 4%, and fewer than 0.1% end up in hospital — because local measures work. Suturing stabilises the clot and wound margins; a resorbable pack fills dead space and gives the clot a scaffold. Leaving a socket open and relying on pressure alone is the usual reason a patient re-attends out of hours. Patients on aspirin alone have a bleeding risk close to non-anticoagulated baseline and may not need sutures, but everyone else does. Tranexamic acid mouthwash is not recommended from dental primary care in the UK: it's not in the BNF Dental Practitioners' Formulary, it's unlicensed as a mouthwash, and the added benefit over standard local measures is unclear.

## How this applies in practice

- Keep the kit ready: absorbent gauze, oxidised cellulose or collagen sponge, needle holders, tissue forceps, resorbable sutures, scissors.
- Aspirin monotherapy: pressure plus good closure, sutures optional.
- Any other anticoagulant or dual antiplatelet: suture and pack every extraction socket.
- Do not discharge until the clot is stable in the chair — sending the patient home bleeding guarantees a callback.
- Offer a non-animal pack where the patient prefers (religious, cultural or ethical reasons) and stock both options.
- If the patient's own doctor has prescribed TXA mouthwash, use it alongside local measures, not instead of them.
- Failure of initial haemostasis means they will need packing and suturing later anyway — do it now.
