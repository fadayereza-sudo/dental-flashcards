## The recommendation

Plan invasive treatment for anticoagulated patients early in the day and week, limit the initial treatment area, stage higher-risk procedures over separate visits, and for patients far from emergency care extend post-operative monitoring before discharge.

## Why

Bleeding complications from anticoagulated patients are uncommon but almost always present within hours. An early-day, early-week slot means the complication (if it comes) lands during surgery hours, with the same clinician available and the same notes open — not at 11pm on a Friday. Limiting the first area of work (one extraction, three teeth of RSD) gives a clinical test of that individual's bleeding response before committing to more. Staging replaces one large wound with several small ones, each of which is easier to close and monitor. For a rural patient, the time cost of a bleed means extended in-chair monitoring after the procedure buys more safety than any drug manipulation could.

## How this applies in practice

- Never book an anticoagulated complex extraction for Friday afternoon.
- Extract one tooth, observe haemostasis, then decide whether to take the next.
- Break a long RSD into quadrants rather than doing a mouthful in one visit.
- For higher-risk work in a remote patient, watch the socket for longer before discharge and re-verify the clot is stable.
- Give written post-op advice with emergency contact details every time — the patient is the one who will notice rebleeding first.
- If the patient has a long drive home, lean harder on the measures that avoid complications (small initial area, suture, pack) rather than assuming they can pop back.
