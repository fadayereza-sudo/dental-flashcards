## The recommendation

For a patient on a combination of an anticoagulant and an antiplatelet drug who requires dental treatment likely to cause bleeding, consult the prescribing clinician before proceeding.

## Why

Combination therapy (DOAC or warfarin plus aspirin or clopidogrel, or in rare cases a DOAC plus dual antiplatelet) is prescribed for the highest-stakes indications — atrial fibrillation with a recent MI or stent, apical thrombus after MI — where both thromboembolism and bleeding carry major consequences. The bleeding risk is additive: local measures may not be enough on their own. Because the medical picture is what determines whether any drug can be briefly modified, the prescribing clinician (cardiologist or haematologist, not the GP) has to make that call. Some combinations run only for 3–4 weeks, in which case the sensible answer is to wait.

## How this applies in practice

- Triple therapy (DOAC plus dual antiplatelet) for 3–4 weeks post-MI is short-lived — delay non-urgent dental work until it finishes.
- Contact the cardiologist or haematologist with a concrete question: the specific drugs, the procedure planned, the patient's bleeding history.
- Do not infer a "continue both" or "stop one" decision from the individual drug sections — the interaction between agents and the underlying condition change the risk calculus.
- Keep pain control non-pharmacological or paracetamol-based while waiting for advice; do not prescribe NSAIDs.
- When you do operate, pull every stop: stage treatment, minimal initial area, suture and pack every time.
