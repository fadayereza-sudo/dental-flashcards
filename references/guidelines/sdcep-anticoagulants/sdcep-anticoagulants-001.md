## The recommendation

Before treating a patient on an anticoagulant or antiplatelet drug, classify the planned procedure as unlikely to cause bleeding, likely to cause bleeding with a low risk of post-operative complications, or likely with a higher risk.

## Why

The drug-by-drug guidance that follows all branches off this call. Procedures that do not break the mucosa (BPE, supragingival scale, supragingival fillings, impressions, orthodontic adjustments, infiltration LA) need no modification of therapy, because the baseline bleeding is trivial. Most invasive work — simple extractions of 1–3 teeth, incision and drainage, six-point perio chart, RSD, subgingival fillings — sits in the low-complications bracket. Complex or multiple extractions, any flap raising (surgical extractions, perio surgery, preprosthetic, periradicular, crown lengthening, implants), gingival recontouring and biopsies sit in the higher bracket. "Higher risk" here is relative — in the wider surgical world these are still minor bleeds, so the label is about nudging up the care taken, not about deferring routine dentistry.

## How this applies in practice

- Treat the classification as the first decision before opening the BNF: the answer ("continue the DOAC / check INR / miss a morning dose / liaise") depends entirely on which of the three columns the procedure sits in.
- A single extraction is low-risk; extracting several adjacent teeth in one visit tips it to higher risk because the wound is bigger.
- Flap raising is the usual trigger for higher-risk status — if you raise a flap, plan for suturing and packing before starting.
- IDBs and other regional nerve blocks are classed as unlikely to cause bleeding, but prefer infiltration or intraligamentary LA where possible to avoid haematoma in the pterygomandibular space; if IDB is needed in an unstable warfarin patient, check the INR first.
- The line between "low" and "higher" isn't rigid: stage a long RSD or a multi-tooth plan to keep each visit in the low-complications lane.
