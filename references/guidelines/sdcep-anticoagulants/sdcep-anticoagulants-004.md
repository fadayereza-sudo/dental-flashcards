## The recommendation

Do not interrupt anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy — except under direct written instruction from the patient's cardiologist — for patients with prosthetic metal heart valves or coronary stents, patients who have had a pulmonary embolism or DVT in the last three months, or patients on anticoagulation for planned cardioversion.

## Why

In these four groups the thromboembolic risk of even brief interruption outweighs any plausible bleeding benefit. A mechanical valve clots within hours of losing anticoagulation. A fresh coronary stent can occlude — stent thrombosis is often fatal. Recent VTE has an extremely high rebound-clot rate in the first three months. Cardioversion requires at least three weeks of uninterrupted anticoagulation to clear atrial thrombus before the procedure; pausing resets that clock. The dental bleeding risk of staying on the drug is always controllable with local measures; the thrombotic risk of stopping is not.

## How this applies in practice

- Look specifically for "mechanical heart valve", "coronary stent", "PE or DVT in last 3 months", "awaiting cardioversion" in the MH.
- If the drug must come off, the cardiologist (not the GP, not you) writes the instruction in writing.
- Manage bleeding with local measures and staging, not by stopping the drug.
- Patients after recent MI may be on triple therapy (DOAC plus dual antiplatelet) — these also fall under "don't touch without cardiology".
- Patients waiting for cardioversion are rare but unmistakable: keep the dental plan conservative and do not advise any dose changes.
