## The recommendation

For a patient on warfarin or another vitamin K antagonist, with an INR below 4, treat without interrupting their anticoagulant. Check the INR no more than 24 hours before the procedure, or no more than 72 hours before if the patient is stable.

## Why

Warfarin has a narrow therapeutic range and a long half-life — interrupting therapy leaves the patient at sub-therapeutic anticoagulation for days, long enough to risk a thromboembolic event. The evidence suggests no meaningful difference in bleeding complications between continuing and stopping warfarin for dental work when the INR is under 4; when bleeding does happen it is controlled locally. An INR of 4 or above tips the balance: bleeding risk becomes clinically important without meaningfully reducing thrombotic risk, so the procedure is deferred and the anticoagulation service notified. "Stable" (per the BNF definition) means no weekly monitoring and no INR above 4 in the last two months — those patients are consistent enough that 72-hour-old readings still reflect today.

## How this applies in practice

- Ask for the INR booklet at the appointment — patients should present it routinely.
- Unstable INR, or any doubt about stability: test within 24 hours before the procedure.
- Stable INR (no weekly testing, no readings ≥4 in 2 months): up to 72 hours before is acceptable.
- INR ≥4: do not proceed with invasive treatment. Tell the anticoagulation service or GP; urgent treatment goes to secondary dental care.
- INR <4: treat as normal, continuing warfarin. Consider limiting the initial treatment area, stage higher-risk procedures, suture and pack.
- If you prescribe an antibiotic (especially metronidazole, or any antibiotic for an unwell patient), warn the patient that the INR may rise and notify the anticoagulation service to consider earlier re-testing.
- An IDB on an unstable warfarin patient is the one "low-bleeding" procedure that still warrants an INR check — bleeding into the pterygomandibular space is not a trivial complication.
