## The recommendation

For a patient on a DOAC (apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban or edoxaban) who needs a dental procedure with a low risk of bleeding complications, treat without interrupting their anticoagulant medication.

## Why

DOACs have predictable short half-lives (apixaban ~12 h, dabigatran ~13–18 h, rivaroxaban 5–13 h, edoxaban 10–14 h) and rapid onset of action. Evidence suggests no significant difference in post-op bleeding between continuing and interrupting the DOAC for routine dental procedures, and all reported events were controlled with local measures. The balance tips firmly towards continuing: a brief interruption may be trivial bleeding-wise but exposes the patient to unnecessary thromboembolic risk from an anticoagulant that is prescribed for a significant clinical indication.

## How this applies in practice

- Simple extractions (1–3 teeth, restricted wound), RSD, six-point perio chart, incision and drainage, and subgingival restorations all count as low risk — continue the DOAC.
- Book the patient early in the day so any bleeding problem happens during surgery hours.
- Limit the initial treatment area: do one extraction or three teeth of RSD, assess bleeding, then decide whether to continue.
- Morning appointments coincide with the peak drug level — accept this, because being able to deal with a bleed in-hours outweighs the theoretical peak-level disadvantage.
- Strongly consider suturing and packing even on a "low" bleed — it's what keeps the incidence of complications low.
