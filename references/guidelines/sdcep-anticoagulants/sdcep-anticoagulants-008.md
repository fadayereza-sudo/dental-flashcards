## The recommendation

For a patient on a prophylactic (low) dose of a low molecular weight heparin — dalteparin, enoxaparin or tinzaparin — treat without interrupting their anticoagulant. For a treatment (higher) dose, or if the dose is unclear, consult the prescribing clinician before invasive work.

## Why

LMWH bleeding risk is dose-dependent. Low prophylactic doses (e.g. dalteparin 2,500–5,000 units OD, enoxaparin 2,000–4,000 units OD, tinzaparin 3,500–4,500 units OD) produce a bleeding risk comparable to or below a VKA within therapeutic range, and dental procedures can go ahead. Treatment doses (weight-based, e.g. enoxaparin 1.5 mg/kg OD or 1 mg/kg BD; tinzaparin 175 units/kg OD) produce anticoagulation equivalent to full warfarin or DOAC therapy and need a medical conversation first. The doses can overlap in patients who are light, heavy or renally impaired, which is why uncertainty is itself a trigger to phone the prescriber. LMWHs have short half-lives, so dental work can safely proceed 24 hours after a stopped course.

## How this applies in practice

- Ask why they are on the injection: post-orthopaedic prophylaxis and pregnancy prophylaxis are usually low dose; DVT/PE treatment, cancer-associated thrombosis and bridging are usually treatment dose.
- Unsure about dose? Call the specialist or GP — the milligrams per day tell you which bracket they sit in.
- Prophylactic dose: continue, treat as for any anticoagulated patient — limit the initial area, stage higher-risk work, suture and pack.
- Treatment dose: do not proceed without liaison; bleeding risk is in the warfarin range and may need modification agreed with the prescriber.
- Elective post-ortho LMWH courses run up to 6 weeks — where possible, delay non-urgent dental work until they finish.
- For patients given heparin during dialysis, delay invasive dental treatment to the following day.
