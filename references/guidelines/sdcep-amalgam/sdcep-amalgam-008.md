## The recommendation

When the use of dental amalgam is justified for a restricted-group patient, communicate the reason to the patient, take valid consent, and document the justification in the clinical record.

## Why

The Article 10(2) exception puts the legal weight onto the clinician's individual decision. The regulator's expectation is that you can produce, at any later point, evidence that the alternative materials were genuinely not feasible for this patient at this time — not just preferred-against. The communication and consent step is also what protects the therapeutic relationship: a parent who later finds out their child received amalgam without explanation has a legitimate complaint, even if the clinical decision was sound. SDCEP frames this as the standard "valid consent" obligation that already governs every dental decision, applied with extra care because the default has shifted.

## How this applies in practice

- Three things to record: (1) the specific clinical reason amalgam was needed (e.g. moisture control unachievable, cooperation insufficient for adhesive protocol, diagnosed allergy to alternative materials), (2) the alternatives considered and why each was unsuitable, (3) the consent discussion with the patient or parent.
- Spell it out for parents: "Composite would normally be first choice, but we couldn't keep this tooth dry enough for it to bond properly, and amalgam will give a more reliable filling here."
- Significant departures from SDCEP advice should be documented per SDCEP's general statement of intent — the same principle applies to any guidance.
- Treat documentation as protection, not paperwork — it is what stands between you and a regulatory case if the decision is later questioned.
