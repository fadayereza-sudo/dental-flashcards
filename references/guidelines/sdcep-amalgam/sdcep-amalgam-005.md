## The recommendation

When treating a pregnant patient, avoid using dental amalgam; postpone non-urgent restorative treatment and the removal of existing amalgam restorations until after delivery, and reserve treatment during pregnancy for urgent indications such as relief of pain or infection.

## Why

There is no evidence that placing or removing amalgam during pregnancy harms the developing foetus, but both COT and SCENIHR apply the precautionary principle: any clinical intervention that can be deferred should be. Mercury crosses the placenta more readily than it crosses into breast milk, so pregnancy is the highest-exposure window for the developing infant. The data on alternative restorative materials in pregnancy is also limited, which is why SCENIHR discourages those too where possible — the safer course is usually to wait, not to swap one material for another.

## How this applies in practice

- Routine restorative treatment in a pregnant patient should be deferred until postnatal review unless there is pain, infection or another urgent indication.
- If urgent treatment cannot wait, choose the restorative material based on the individual risk for the foetus, the stage of pregnancy, and the patient's values and preferences — there is no automatic "best" alternative.
- Do not remove existing amalgam restorations during pregnancy "to be safe" — placement and removal are when mercury vapour release peaks, so removal carries more theoretical exposure than leaving the restoration alone.
- Record the reason for any treatment given during pregnancy and the consent discussion.
