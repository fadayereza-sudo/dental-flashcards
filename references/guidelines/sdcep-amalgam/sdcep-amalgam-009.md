## The recommendation

From 1 January 2019, dental amalgam may only be used in pre-dosed encapsulated form (Article 10(1)), and every dental facility that places or removes amalgam must be equipped with an amalgam separator (Article 10(4)).

## Why

Both rules cut the amount of mercury that escapes the surgery. Pre-dosed capsules end the use of bulk mercury and alloy with hand-mixing — they remove the operator's exposure to free liquid mercury and eliminate the spillage and disposal problem that came with it. Amalgam separators capture the mercury-laden particulates from suction waste before they enter the practice drainage. Without a separator, every excess from a placement and every fragment from a removal flushes straight into the wastewater system, where it joins the environmental mercury load that drove Minamata in the first place.

## How this applies in practice

- Stock encapsulated amalgam only — bulk-mixed amalgam is non-compliant.
- Every UK dental practice that places or removes amalgam needs an amalgam separator fitted on the suction system; verify yours is installed and serviced.
- Used capsules and removed amalgam fragments are clinical waste with mercury content — segregate per the practice's hazardous-waste contract, do not put them in domestic waste or sharps bins.
- These are facility-level legal duties enforced under the Control of Mercury (Enforcement) Regulations 2017 — non-compliance is a regulatory offence, not a guidance lapse.
