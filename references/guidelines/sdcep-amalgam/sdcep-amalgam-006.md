## The recommendation

When treating a breastfeeding patient, avoid using dental amalgam; otherwise base management decisions on individual circumstances and the patient's values and preferences.

## Why

Mercury transfer through breast milk is lower than mercury transfer across the placenta, so the theoretical risk to the infant is smaller during breastfeeding than during pregnancy. The FDA has concluded that infants are not at risk of adverse effects from breast milk in women exposed to mercury vapour from dental amalgam. Despite that, breastfeeding patients sit inside the same Article 10(2) restriction, so the legal default is still to avoid amalgam unless an exception is justified — but unlike pregnancy, there is no blanket "postpone" rule, because the urgency calculus is different.

## How this applies in practice

- Routine restorative treatment is not automatically deferred for a breastfeeding patient — discuss timing and options with her.
- If amalgam is the only feasible option (e.g. moisture control or cooperation considerations), the same justification, consent and documentation rules apply as for any Article 10(2) exception.
- Be ready to reassure: there is no evidence that breast milk from a mother with amalgam fillings poses a risk to the infant, and removal of existing restorations is not indicated.
- Tailor the conversation — values around breastfeeding duration, infant age and the urgency of the dental problem all feed into the shared decision.
