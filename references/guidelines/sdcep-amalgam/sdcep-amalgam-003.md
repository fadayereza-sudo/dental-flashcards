## The recommendation

When treating primary teeth, avoid using dental amalgam — the working group concluded there are no indications for amalgam in primary teeth.

## Why

Primary teeth have a limited remaining lifespan, and the alternative pathways (preformed metal crowns via the Hall Technique, selective caries removal with glass-ionomer or composite, sealants, fluoride varnish, and preventive-only management) cover every scenario where amalgam might once have been the default. Preformed metal crowns in particular need no or minimal preparation, are well tolerated by children, and reduce the risk of pain or infection compared with direct restorations. Because each of these alternatives works in primary teeth and the tooth itself is going to exfoliate, the moisture-control or cooperation arguments that occasionally justify amalgam in permanent teeth do not carry over.

## How this applies in practice

- The Hall Technique (sealing carious primary molars under a preformed metal crown without preparation) is now taught in every UK dental school — if it isn't part of your toolkit, this is the gap to close.
- For occlusal lesions, selective caries removal plus a glass-ionomer or composite restoration is the standard alternative.
- Preventive-only management (fluoride varnish, sealants, oral hygiene and dietary advice) is a legitimate option for early lesions, especially when cooperation is limited.
- "No indications" is a firm line — if you reach for amalgam in a primary tooth, you need to be able to explain why none of the alternatives worked.
