## The recommendation

From 1 July 2018, do not use dental amalgam for the treatment of deciduous teeth, of children under 15 years, or of pregnant or breastfeeding women, except when deemed strictly necessary by the dental practitioner based on the specific medical needs of the patient.

## Why

The targeted groups reflect the precautionary principle: developing foetuses, infants and children are more susceptible to the toxic effects of other forms of mercury, even though there is no direct evidence of harm from amalgam itself. Restricting amalgam in children also seeds a future cohort of amalgam-free patients, supporting long-term phase-down. The "specific medical needs" exemption is read by SDCEP as including specific dental needs, so a clinician can still use amalgam in the patient's best interests, but the decision must be justified, documented and consented to.

## How this applies in practice

- The rule applies to three patient groups: any patient when treating deciduous teeth, all under-15s (primary or permanent teeth), and any pregnant or breastfeeding patient.
- "Strictly necessary" is the legal test for an exception — convenience or speed are not enough; you have to be able to defend the decision to a regulator.
- Document three things every time you use amalgam in one of these groups: the clinical justification, the discussion with the patient, and their valid consent.
- The rule restricts placement only — it does not require removal or replacement of existing amalgam restorations.
