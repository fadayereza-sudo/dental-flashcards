## The recommendation

Manage caries as a dynamic balance — promote remineralisation through fluoride and mineral ions, reduce demineralisation by cutting sugar exposure — rather than as a one-way decay process.

## Why

Caries is dietary sugars fermented by plaque bacteria, producing acid that demineralises enamel. The de- and re-mineralisation cycle runs all day, and early lesions can be reversed if remineralisation outpaces demineralisation. Caries only progresses into dentine and cavitates when the balance tips persistently the wrong way. The clinical implication is that "drilling and filling" treats the symptom; controlling the balance treats the disease. Two things tip remineralisation in the patient's favour: more fluoride at the tooth surface, and less acid challenge. Both are modifiable.

## How this applies in practice

- An incipient white-spot lesion is not a hole to fill — it is a sign the balance is wrong, and high-ppm fluoride plus dietary change can arrest or reverse it.
- The same paste, varnish and dietary advice apply across the life course; only the doses and recall intervals change with age and risk.
- Frame the discussion to patients in balance terms: every sugar exposure tips it the wrong way for about an hour, every fluoride exposure tips it back.
- A child or adult who keeps cavitating despite restorations does not need more restorations — they need the imbalance fixed at source.
- Use a contemporary detection and management framework (the toolkit names ICCMS) so you record the lesion stage at every recall and can track whether the balance is improving.
