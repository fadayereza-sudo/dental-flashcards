## The recommendation

Perform an updated medical, social and dental history with an extraoral and intraoral visual and tactile soft-tissue examination on all patients at every dental assessment visit.

## Why

Dentists and the wider dental team are the only health professionals routinely examining the inside of the mouth in well populations. Most symptomless mouth cancers can be detected by visual examination, and earlier detection meaningfully improves five-year survival. A "look only" exam misses indurated submucosal lesions; tactile examination — palpating lips, neck, nodes, floor of mouth, tongue with the patient's tongue protruded — adds the consistency information that distinguishes a soft inflammatory lump from a firm, fixed one. The recommendation is for every visit because cancers can develop between recalls and a quick look at the start of every appointment is the cheapest possible screen.

## How this applies in practice

- Follow a routine: extraoral first (face, lips, neck, lymph node chains), then intraoral (lips inner, buccal mucosa, gingivae, tongue dorsum/lateral/ventral, floor of mouth, palate, oropharynx).
- Ask the patient to protrude the tongue and move it side to side; lift the tongue with gauze to examine the lateral and ventral surfaces — the highest-risk sites for oral cavity cancer.
- Palpate firmly enough to detect induration; a firm submucosal mass with overlying normal mucosa can be the only sign of early disease.
- Document negative findings — "soft tissues NAD intra-oral and extra-oral" — at every visit; this protects the patient and the clinician.
- The GDC explicitly requires this routine; failure to perform a soft-tissue examination is a recognised regulatory concern in fitness-to-practise cases.
