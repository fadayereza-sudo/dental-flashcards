## The recommendation

Use fluoride toothpaste of at least 1,000 ppm fluoride; for children at higher caries risk and for everyone aged 7 years and over, use 1,350 to 1,500 ppm fluoride.

## Why

Moderate to high-certainty Cochrane evidence shows fluoride toothpaste at 1,000 ppm or above prevents caries in both primary and permanent dentitions. Below 1,000 ppm there is no consistent evidence of a caries-preventive effect. Higher concentrations (1,350–1,500 ppm) give greater protection in those at higher risk, which is why they are the default once permanent teeth are erupting (around age 7). The chemical formulation (sodium fluoride, sodium monofluorophosphate, stannous fluoride) does not change the recommendation — only ppm matters.

## How this applies in practice

- Read the ppm on the tube — about a third of UK toothpastes on the market contain no fluoride at all, and "natural", whitening, herbal, and imported brands are common offenders.
- Default for under-7s with average risk: 1,000 ppm minimum.
- Default for everyone from age 7 upwards, and for any higher-risk under-7: 1,350–1,500 ppm.
- Maximum over-the-counter strength in the UK is 1,500 ppm; anything above that is prescription only.
- When a patient brings their own toothpaste choice, ask why and check the ppm before recommending change — empty endorsements of "any fluoride toothpaste" miss the point.
