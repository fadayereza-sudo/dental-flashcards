## The recommendation

Minimise both the amount and frequency of sugar-containing food and drink consumption. All sugar-containing food and drink should be consumed as part of a meal, not as a between-meal snack. Avoid sugar before bedtime, when saliva flow and buffering are reduced.

## Why

The Stephan curve shows that plaque pH plummets within one minute of any sugar exposure and takes 20–40 minutes to recover via saliva buffering and clearance. Each sugar event is one demineralisation episode. Frequent intake means pH stays below the demineralisation threshold for most of the day with no time for remineralisation. Tying sugar to mealtimes confines it to periods of high salivary flow, which limits the damage. Higher consumption frequency of free-sugar foods is independently associated with greater caries risk in both deciduous and permanent dentitions.

## How this applies in practice

- The classic clinical example: two patients eat the same total sugar daily; the one who eats it in single doses at meals has dramatically less caries than the one who grazes.
- "Three meals, max two snacks" is a workable rule for most patients.
- Snack swaps: fresh fruit, vegetable sticks, plain yoghurt, cheese, plain popcorn — these have a place in a snacking pattern; sweets, dried fruit, biscuits do not.
- For high-risk caries patients, a diet diary picks up the hidden sippers and grazers — ask patients to record number of intakes, what was eaten, and timing.
- Bedtime: brush, then nothing but water until morning. Anything sugary at bedtime sits on the teeth all night with minimal saliva to clear it.
