## The recommendation

Use chlorhexidine gluconate mouthrinse (0.1% or 0.2%) only for short-term plaque and gingivitis control — for example after periodontal or implant surgery — and rinse the mouth with water between toothpaste and chlorhexidine because they are chemically incompatible.

## Why

Chlorhexidine has high-certainty evidence for reducing dental plaque and gingivitis, with both 0.1% and 0.2% effective in the early healing period after periodontal and implant surgery. Long-term use accumulates predictable side effects: extrinsic brown staining of teeth and tongue, taste disturbance or alteration, mucosal soreness and desquamation, and a burning sensation. Most toothpaste detergents (sodium lauryl sulphate) inactivate chlorhexidine, so using them within 30 minutes of each other wastes the chlorhexidine. The clinical message is precise: short course, separated from toothpaste, with a defined endpoint.

## How this applies in practice

- Typical regimen post-surgery: 0.2% chlorhexidine twice daily for 1–2 weeks, decoupled from brushing by at least 30 minutes.
- Counsel patients on staining before they start — staining is reversible with subsequent professional cleaning but distresses patients who weren't warned.
- Avoid prescribing for long-term plaque control in the absence of a defined indication — the side effects accumulate and there is no chronic-use evidence base.
- Useful as adjunct in patients with medication-induced gingival enlargement while medication review is in progress.
- Advise patients with a history of chlorhexidine reaction to avoid all CHX products (mouthwash, varnish, gel, surgical scrub) — anaphylaxis is rare but documented.
