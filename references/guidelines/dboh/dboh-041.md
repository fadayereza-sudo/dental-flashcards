## The recommendation

Use the urgent suspected cancer pathway (2-week wait) for any patient with: unexplained ulceration in the oral cavity lasting more than 3 weeks; a persistent, unexplained head or neck lump; a lump on the lip or in the oral cavity; a red patch (erythroplakia); a red and white patch (erythroleukoplakia / speckled leukoplakia); persistent unexplained hoarseness; or persistent throat pain or pain on swallowing for more than 3 weeks.

## Why

The 3-week threshold is what separates routine inflammatory or traumatic lesions (which should resolve once the irritant is removed) from possibly malignant ones that need biopsy. Erythroplakia carries the highest malignant transformation rate of any oral mucosal lesion (around 50%); speckled leukoplakia is next. A persistent neck lump in an adult is a head and neck cancer until proven otherwise. The 2-week pathway exists because earlier diagnosis is the single biggest modifiable factor in oral cancer survival, and getting the patient seen fast is the GDP's most consequential single action.

## How this applies in practice

- The triggers are absolute — if any one is present and unexplained, refer urgently; do not wait to see if it improves at the next recall.
- Take a baseline photograph if equipment is available and document the lesion in detail before referral.
- Use the local 2-week wait pro forma; copy the GP into the referral.
- Tell the patient explicitly: "I'm referring you on the 2-week suspected cancer pathway. This is to rule out something serious, not to confirm it. You should hear within a week."
- Less common but important signs that should also trigger referral: unexplained tooth mobility not from periodontal disease, delayed healing of an extraction socket, fixation of a lesion, regional lymphadenopathy, dysphagia, weight loss, cranial neuropathies.
- The dental team is responsible for facilitating the referral, not just suggesting it — chase up if the appointment is not confirmed.
