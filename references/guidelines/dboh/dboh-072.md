## The recommendation

Rank the four quit methods clearly when advising patients: (1) local stop-smoking services with stop-smoking aids — best chance, 3 times more likely to quit than willpower alone; (2) prescribed stop-smoking medicine — doubles the chance; (3) over-the-counter NRT — 1.5 times more likely; (4) willpower alone — least effective.

## Why

Patients overestimate willpower because it costs nothing. The numbers above let you compete with that intuition: 3x is a much bigger swing than most people realise. Behavioural support targets the psychological side of nicotine addiction (cravings, triggers, identity); pharmacotherapy targets the physiological side (nicotine receptor downregulation, withdrawal). Combining the two beats either alone, which is why "specialist support + medication" is the headline message of VBA.

## How this applies in practice

- Quote the multipliers in the conversation: "On willpower alone, very few people manage. With expert support and a quit medication, you triple your chance."
- Free NHS stop-smoking services are the gold standard — find your local provider and have the referral pathway ready.
- A trial in Glasgow showed pregnant women were 2.63 times more likely to be non-smoking by end of pregnancy when offered financial incentives plus support — flag this if local schemes exist.
- Stoptober (October) gives a national hook — patients who stop for 28 days are 5 times more likely to quit for good; use this as a calendar prompt at autumn appointments.
- Even after stopping, support during withdrawal is helpful — symptoms include ulcers, which fall in the dental remit to recognise and reassure about.
