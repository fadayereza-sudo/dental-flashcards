## The recommendation

For people with, or treated for, periodontitis, use interdental brushes sized to fit each space snugly — they are more effective than floss or tape. Where teeth are too close together for a brush, use floss or tape. Cleaning at the gumline is particularly important for these patients, and interdental cleaning is best done before toothbrushing as a habit-forming approach.

## Why

Tissue loss from periodontitis opens larger interdental spaces that floss simply cannot fill mechanically — interdental brushes physically disrupt the biofilm where it matters. The brush must fit snugly: too small and it doesn't reach the walls of the embrasure, too big and it traumatises the papilla. Doing interdental cleaning *before* toothbrushing links the new behaviour to an existing habit, which is the single best lever for adherence in oral hygiene change.

## How this applies in practice

- For each periodontitis patient, prescribe specific interdental brush sizes for specific spaces — most need more than one size in their kit.
- Demonstrate in the chair, with disclosing solution, so the patient sees what's missed.
- Floss is appropriate only for tight contacts; if a brush fits, use a brush.
- Frame the interdental-first sequence as habit-stacking: "Do the small thing right before the big thing you already do every day."
- Re-evaluate technique at every recall — periodontitis maintenance fails most often on interdental adherence.
- For population in general, daily interproximal cleaning should begin by age 18 (earlier if gingivitis is present).
