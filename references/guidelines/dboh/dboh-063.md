## The recommendation

"Free sugars" includes all monosaccharides and disaccharides added by the manufacturer, cook or consumer, plus sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, smoothies, fruit juices, fruit/vegetable concentrates and purées, and all sugars in alcoholic and dairy-alternative drinks. It does NOT include sugars in whole fresh fruit and vegetables, or those naturally present in milk and milk products.

## Why

The "free sugars" concept exists because sugar in its bound form (inside whole fruit, locked into a vegetable cell wall, or in milk) behaves differently in the mouth than sugar that has been freed by processing, juicing, or extraction. Free sugars are immediately available to plaque bacteria and drive caries. Whole fruit, by contrast, requires chewing, contains fibre, and is consumed in smaller doses — so an apple is not nutritionally equivalent to a glass of apple juice. Patients (and many parents) think "natural" or "no added sugar" labels mean safe; they often mean the opposite.

## How this applies in practice

- Honey is a free sugar — patients add it to drinks thinking it's healthier than table sugar; cariogenically it is not.
- Smoothies and fresh fruit juices are free sugars even though they look and feel "natural".
- Dried fruit (raisins, apricots) is concentrated free sugar with high stickiness — limit to mealtimes only.
- Plant-based milks (oat, soya, rice, nut) often contain added free sugars — tell patients to choose unsweetened, calcium-fortified versions.
- "No added sugar" on a label does not mean low free sugar — fruit juice concentrate counts as free sugar and is often the sweetener.
