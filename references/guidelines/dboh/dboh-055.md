## The recommendation

For high-risk patients with accelerated tooth wear, prioritise removal of intrinsic and extrinsic acid sources over toothpaste choice. Specialised toothpastes containing fluoride or fluoride-plus-stannous play a supplementary role only.

## Why

Professional consensus (because clinical-trial evidence on tooth-wear prevention is sparse and low-certainty) is clear: changing diet, treating reflux, or stopping a damaging habit will do far more for tooth wear than switching toothpaste. Patients tend to gravitate towards the easy fix — a "special toothpaste" — and feel they have done the work. They have not, and the wear continues. The professional message must be that pastes are an adjunct, not the intervention.

## How this applies in practice

- Explain the priority order to the patient explicitly: "First we identify and reduce the acid; second we consider the toothpaste."
- Specialised pastes (fluoride or fluoride plus stannous) are an add-on for patients who have already addressed their main risk factor — not a stand-alone solution.
- Avoid high-abrasive pastes (e.g. some smoker's or whitening pastes) in any wear patient.
- There is no strong evidence that delaying brushing after every meal protects against wear in the general population — saving that advice for the post-vomiting context where it matters.
- A patient who returns at recall with no dietary change and a "new toothpaste" needs the conversation reopened, not a stronger paste.
