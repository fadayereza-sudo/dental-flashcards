## The recommendation

Free sugars should provide no more than 5% of total dietary energy from age 2 upwards. Maximum daily added sugar: 19 g (5 cubes) for 4–6-year-olds, 24 g (6 cubes) for 7–10-year-olds, and 30 g (7 cubes) for those aged 11 and over. One sugar cube is approximately 4 g.

## Why

The 5% threshold reflects the SACN review showing that beyond this level, caries risk rises measurably and so does the risk of obesity and related disease. Cube counts make the abstract "5%" tangible for parents and children — it's much easier to picture 5 sugar cubes than 19 g. National survey data show that UK children and adults exceed this target consistently, with girls aged 11–18 and boys aged 4–10 the highest consumers (around 12.5% of energy from free sugars).

## How this applies in practice

- Translate to a familiar product: a 330 ml can of cola is roughly 35 g of sugar — already over the daily limit for an 11-year-old in one go.
- Use cube counts in patient and parent conversations — they are more memorable than grams.
- Soft drinks, fruit drinks, cereals and confectionery are the main contributors for children — focus dietary advice there.
- Trends are slowly improving for children but adults have shown little change — don't assume parents have absorbed the message themselves.
- "Sugar swap" advice (water, lower-fat milk, sugar-free drinks instead of sugary drinks) gets the biggest single gain because soft drinks dominate the intake.
