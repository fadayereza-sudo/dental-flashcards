## The recommendation

Address mechanical wear factors (aggressive brushing, high-abrasive pastes, filing teeth, opening objects with teeth, oral piercings, bruxism) alongside chemical risks. Do not advise the general population to reduce fruit intake — fruit should only be reduced (or not grazed on) when identified as a specific risk factor in a high-risk individual.

## Why

Tooth wear is multifactorial and acid usually combines with mechanical insult. Aggressive brushing on softened enamel does more damage than either factor alone. Fruit, however, is a borderline case: it is healthy at population level (UK adults and children eat too little of it) but has been linked to severe wear in adults referred to specialist hospitals where eating fruit over an extended period was a contributing factor. Generic advice to "cut down on fruit" risks worsening overall diet for the majority of patients to address a problem only a small subset has.

## How this applies in practice

- For mechanical wear: switch to soft-medium small-headed brush, modify technique, switch from a high-abrasive paste, advise removal of piercings, raise awareness of bruxism (consider stress, splint).
- "Filing teeth" and "opening packets with teeth" are habits to ask about — patients won't volunteer them.
- Workplace dust/particulate exposure (e.g. construction) needs PPE.
- Snacking on fruit should NOT be discouraged in the general population — only individual high-risk patients with documented fruit-related erosion need to swap acidic fruit (lemons, oranges, grapefruit) for less acidic options like banana, avocado, or vegetables between meals.
- For those individuals: address habits like holding fruit against teeth or biting citrus directly.
