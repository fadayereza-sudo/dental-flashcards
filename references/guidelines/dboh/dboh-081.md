## The recommendation

Use the AUDIT-C, a 3-question screening tool, to identify drinkers at risk: (1) how often do you have a drink containing alcohol? (2) how many units on a typical drinking day? (3) how often have you had 6+ units (women) or 8+ units (men) on a single occasion in the last year? Score each question 0–4. Score 0–4 = low risk; 5–10 = increasing/higher risk (give brief advice); 11–12 = possible problem (refer).

## Why

AUDIT-C is the validated short form of the WHO AUDIT screening tool, designed for primary care settings — it has been shown to work in NHS general dental practice. Three questions take under a minute, identify the bulk of at-risk drinkers, and produce a numeric score that frames the conversation: "Your score is 7, which suggests your drinking might be putting your health at risk." Identification with feedback is the active ingredient — patients often genuinely don't know they are above the safe threshold.

## How this applies in practice

- Use the scratch-card format if available — it makes the score patient-led and reduces clinician burden.
- For score 0–4, give positive feedback: "Your drinking is at low risk for harm. Keep it up."
- For score 5–10, give brief advice on cutting down — highlight the 14-unit weekly cap, suggest spreading drinks over 3+ days, and several drink-free days a week. Hand out the patient information leaflet.
- For score 11–12, refer to GP or specialist alcohol service. Do NOT simply advise them to stop — withdrawal in dependent drinkers can be dangerous.
- Record the score and the advice given in the patient's notes.
- Adding alcohol screening alongside tobacco-status checking at every recall is realistic — they belong in the same conversation.
