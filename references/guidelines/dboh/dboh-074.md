## The recommendation

Acknowledge to smokers interested in vaping that e-cigarettes are not risk-free but are substantially less harmful than smoking, and that there is growing evidence they help people quit, especially when combined with behavioural support. People using e-cigarettes should stop smoking tobacco completely. People who have completely switched to vaping should be recorded as non-smokers in dental records.

## Why

Almost all the harm from smoking comes from tobacco-combustion products, not from nicotine itself. E-cigarettes deliver nicotine without combustion, so they sidestep the tar, carbon monoxide, and the bulk of the carcinogens. A major UK trial showed that, with face-to-face support, e-cigarette users were twice as likely to quit as those using NRT patches/gum. Dual use (smoking + vaping) loses the benefit because the patient is still inhaling smoke; complete switching is the goal.

## How this applies in practice

- For smokers asking about e-cigarettes: "They are not risk-free, but they are far less harmful than smoking. They can help you stop, especially with expert support."
- Dual use is harm reduction only if it is a stepping stone — encourage complete switch as soon as possible.
- E-cigarettes are the most popular stop-smoking aid in England; many patients will already be using them.
- No e-cigarettes are currently licensed as medicines in the UK, but products are regulated under the Tobacco and Related Products Regulations 2016.
- Less than 1% of young people who have never smoked report vaping — vaping uptake by non-smokers, including youth, remains low.
- Long-term safety data is still developing; be honest with patients that we don't yet know everything.
