## The recommendation

It is appropriate to discuss both smoking and alcohol with a patient in one session. Around one-quarter of smokers drink above low-risk guidelines. Patients trying to quit smoking should think about their alcohol consumption, because once they have had a few drinks their willpower to maintain cessation may drop.

## Why

Smoking and drinking are biologically and behaviourally linked. Alcohol increases the "feel-good" chemicals nicotine produces in the brain, and nicotine alters how the brain responds to alcohol so smokers need more alcohol than non-smokers to get the same effect. They are also socially linked — smoking on a night out with drinks is the classic relapse scenario. Tackling both together makes sense, as long as the conversation stays patient-centred and the patient chooses which to address first.

## How this applies in practice

- Offer the conversation: "Would you like to talk about smoking, alcohol, or both?" — don't impose order.
- For smokers attempting to quit, prepare them for high-risk situations: "You'll probably feel like a cigarette after a few drinks. What's your plan?"
- Stop-smoking attempts have higher success when alcohol is also reduced — flag the link rather than treating each in isolation.
- Some patients will resist talking about both at once — respect that and book a follow-up for the second topic.
- Person-centred approach: the patient leads on which behaviour is theirs to change first.
