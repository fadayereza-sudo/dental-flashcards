## The recommendation

Brushing should start as soon as the first primary tooth erupts using fluoride toothpaste of at least 1,000 ppm. Parents or carers must brush their young children's teeth and supervise brushing until at least 7 years of age. From 3 years children should be encouraged to spit, not rinse.

## Why

Children under 7 lack the manual dexterity and attention to brush effectively without help — the supervised-brushing evidence base (school programmes show significant caries reduction) is what underpins the age-7 cut-off, in the absence of home-setting trials. Adult involvement also controls the toothpaste dose (preventing fluorosis from eating paste), prevents licking from the tube, and guarantees systematic coverage. From 3 years children can usually spit reliably — train this habit early so the no-rinse fluoride benefit is preserved.

## How this applies in practice

- "From birth to 3 years" is the key parent message: any erupted tooth needs brushing, with a smear of 1,000+ ppm paste.
- 3–6 years: pea-sized blob, child + adult both brushing (transitional phase).
- From age 7: many children can brush alone but still need prompting, supervision, and motivation. Some still need help, depending on dexterity and risk.
- Higher-risk children should use 1,350–1,500 ppm family paste (provided they will not eat it from the tube).
- Tell parents not to wake a sleeping breastfed baby to brush — brush as close to bedtime as possible instead.
- Children who eat the paste straight from the tube are over-dosing fluoride — that is the time to drop to a lower-ppm child paste, not the rule for the rest.
