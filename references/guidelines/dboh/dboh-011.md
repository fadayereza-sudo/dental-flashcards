## The recommendation

Use the COM-B model to diagnose why a patient is not changing a behaviour: for change to occur the patient must have the Capability, the Opportunity, and the Motivation to do it.

## Why

Provision of information alone increases knowledge but rarely produces sustained behaviour change. COM-B forces the clinician to look past "they didn't listen" and identify which of the three building blocks is missing. Capability covers the physical and psychological ability — knowledge of what to do, the manual skill, the self-control to maintain it. Opportunity covers the environment — income, social context, available facilities. Motivation covers the desire and intention to start and to keep going. Most behaviour-change failures are not motivation problems alone; identifying the right limiting factor is what makes the next intervention land.

## How this applies in practice

- A teenager who cannot brush effectively because they have never been shown the method has a Capability gap — demonstrate, do not lecture.
- A father who knows what to do but lives in a hostel without a sink has an Opportunity gap — signpost local services and adjust the advice (chewable fluoride options, gum, mid-day rinse).
- A patient who can brush and has the time but does not see why it matters has a Motivation gap — explore consequences and rapport, do not repeat instructions.
- Many patients have gaps in all three areas at once — pick the most fixable and start there.
- Asking "what stops you doing this?" before suggesting how often opens the right diagnostic door.
