## The recommendation

Adults should not regularly drink more than 14 units of alcohol per week, spread evenly over 3 or more days. Drink-free days each week help reduce intake. There is no safe lower limit — no level of regular alcohol consumption improves health. An alcohol-free childhood is the safest option for young people.

## Why

The 14-unit-per-week limit is the threshold below which long-term health risk is "low" — explicitly not "zero". Alcohol is the leading cause of ill-health, disability and death in England among 15–49 year-olds. Spreading the 14 units over 3+ days protects the liver from acute toxicity peaks; concentrating the same total into one or two heavy sessions is more harmful. The "no safe lower limit" message matters because patients (and clinicians) often look for permission to drink "a small amount safely" — that permission can't be given honestly.

## How this applies in practice

- One unit = 10 ml or 8 g of pure alcohol — about half a pint of regular beer, half a small glass of wine, or one single measure of spirits.
- A single 250 ml glass of 12% wine is 3 units; a 750 ml bottle is 9 units — easy to overshoot 14 units in a few days.
- A pint of "regular" beer is 2 units; a pint of "premium" is 3 units; a 440 ml can of super-strength lager is 4 units.
- Phrase the recommendation as a positive: "Several drink-free days a week helps keep risk low" — easier to act on than "drink less".
- Dry months (Dry January) reliably reduce drinking afterwards — a useful nudge for patients who want a reset.
