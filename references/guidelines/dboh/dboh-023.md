## The recommendation

Diagnose periodontitis when interdental clinical attachment loss is detectable at two or more non-adjacent teeth, or when buccal/oral attachment loss of 3 mm or more with pocketing of 3 mm or more is detectable at two or more teeth and cannot be ascribed to another cause.

## Why

The 2017 World Workshop case definition was designed to distinguish true periodontitis (microbial-host inflammation that destroys attachment) from localised attachment loss caused by trauma, recession from brushing, restorative encroachment or a fractured root. Requiring the loss to be interdental and at non-adjacent teeth filters out localised mechanical causes. Requiring attachment loss rather than just pocket depth catches the patient with recession and a 3 mm pocket whose true loss is much greater than the probing depth alone suggests. Without a precise case definition, "periodontitis" gets used loosely and treatment plans drift; with it, the diagnosis is reproducible.

## How this applies in practice

- Probe interdentally on adjacent teeth — attachment loss measured from the CEJ is the key, not pocket depth from the gingival margin.
- A patient with a single 7 mm pocket on one tooth from a fractured cusp does not have periodontitis; they have a localised periodontal lesion.
- Recession with a shallow pocket can still mean significant attachment loss — measure from the CEJ.
- A previously treated stable patient with historical bone loss but BPE 0–2 still has the diagnosis "treated periodontitis"; the disease is in remission, not abolished.
- Use the case definition before staging and grading — you cannot stage a disease the patient does not have.
