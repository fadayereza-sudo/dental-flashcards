## The recommendation

Treat partially erupted, mesioangular impacted lower third molars as a specific caries risk both for the third molar itself and for the distal surface of the adjacent second molar.

## Why

A mesioangular impacted lower 8 traps plaque in a stagnation pocket at the cemento-enamel junction of the distal of the 7. Toothbrushing cannot reach this surface, the soft tissue operculum holds biofilm in place, and acid attack on a curved root surface where enamel is thin produces caries that is silent until it is deep. The lesion is often missed on standard occlusal-only inspection and only shows on a bitewing or panoramic. By the time the second molar is symptomatic, the lesion is frequently subgingival and unrestorable, which is the basis on which removal of the impacted third molar is sometimes justified prophylactically.

## How this applies in practice

- Any patient with a partially erupted lower 8 should have the distal of the 7 examined radiographically — bitewing if it captures the area, otherwise panoramic.
- Mesioangular impactions are the highest-risk angulation for distal-7 caries.
- Even where a third molar is asymptomatic, distal-7 caries on imaging is a recognised indication to consider third-molar removal under FDS criteria.
- Counsel the patient explicitly: "Your wisdom tooth is trapping plaque against the back of the molar in front; we need to either keep it impeccably clean or consider removing it."
- Sealing the distal surface of a 7 once exposed (e.g. after the deciduous E exfoliates or after operculectomy) is reasonable opportunistic prevention.
