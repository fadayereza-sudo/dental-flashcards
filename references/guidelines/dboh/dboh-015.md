## The recommendation

Use every patient contact — including urgent visits — as an opportunity for brief behaviour-change advice, and time deeper interventions for life-course transitions when patients are most receptive.

## Why

Habit formation takes anywhere from 2 to 9 months depending on the patient and the behaviour, so single bursts of advice rarely stick. Repeated brief reinforcement at every contact compounds, and the principle of "Making Every Contact Count" treats even an emergency appointment as a chance to plant a seed rather than a wasted preventive opportunity. People are more open to change at certain points — pregnancy, becoming a parent, leaving school, starting a new relationship, retiring — so timing the intervention to land in those windows raises the chance of success. Patients who do not attend regularly may only present in pain, which is precisely when their attention is captured.

## How this applies in practice

- An emergency extraction appointment is the right time to mention smoking, not the wrong time — the patient is paying attention.
- New mum at the 6-week postnatal stage is unusually receptive to dietary and hygiene advice both for herself and the infant.
- Pregnancy is a teachable moment for sugar reduction and a free-NHS-care window to bank routine prevention.
- Recall is the routine vehicle for behaviour-change reinforcement — do not save it all for one appointment.
- Expect 2–9 months for a new habit to bed in; plan the recall interval to allow you to coach across that whole window.
