## The recommendation

For tooth wear suspected to involve intrinsic acid (gastric reflux, laryngopharyngeal reflux, chronic pregnancy vomiting, eating disorders such as bulimia), do not brush immediately after vomiting; rinse the mouth with water and apply fluoride toothpaste with a finger or use a fluoride mouthrinse. Refer for medical management of the underlying condition.

## Why

Stomach acid (pH ~1.5–3.5) softens enamel within seconds. Brushing softened enamel mechanically removes the demineralised layer, accelerating tissue loss. Rinsing with water dilutes and clears the acid; fluoride applied topically (without abrasion) supports remineralisation while the surface is most vulnerable. Eating disorders and reflux disease are systemic conditions — addressing the dental damage without addressing the cause means the wear continues and the patient does not get the help they need.

## How this applies in practice

- After any vomiting episode: rinse with water first, wait at least an hour before brushing, then use fluoride toothpaste; in the meantime, fluoride paste on a finger or fluoride mouthwash gives the topical benefit without the abrasion.
- Pattern recognition: palatal erosion of upper anteriors with relative sparing of lowers is the classic intrinsic-acid pattern (the tongue protects lowers).
- Pregnant patients with hyperemesis: brief them on the post-vomiting routine at the booking visit; offer fluoride mouthwash.
- If you suspect an eating disorder, raise it sensitively and offer to coordinate with the GP. The dental finding may be the first medical contact for these patients.
- For GORD, refer to GP for assessment — uncontrolled reflux will undo any local dental measures.
