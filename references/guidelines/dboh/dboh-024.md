## The recommendation

Distinguish gingivitis (reversible inflammation contained within the gingiva) from periodontitis (host-mediated inflammation with irreversible loss of periodontal attachment), and recognise gingivitis as a risk factor for periodontitis but not an inevitability.

## Why

Both conditions are initiated by plaque, but in gingivitis the inflammation stays above the attachment and reverses when plaque is removed. Periodontitis happens when a dysbiotic biofilm — a shift in the balance between bacteria and host immune response — drives inflammation that crosses into the periodontal ligament and bone, and that destruction does not undo when plaque is later removed. Not every patient with gingivitis develops periodontitis: susceptibility is host-determined and influenced by smoking, diabetes, genetics and stress. The prevention story is asymmetric — preventing gingivitis prevents periodontitis in susceptible people, but treating established periodontitis cannot rebuild lost bone.

## How this applies in practice

- Bleeding on probing without attachment loss is gingivitis, fully reversible with plaque control alone.
- "Dysbiosis" is the right word for the biofilm shift in periodontitis — small amounts of plaque cause minor inflammation, but the imbalance between the biofilm and the immune response is what drives destruction.
- Counselling: "Bleeding gums are reversible. Once the bone goes, it's gone — that's why we treat the bleeding now."
- Susceptibility varies, so two patients with similar plaque levels can have very different periodontal outcomes; this is what makes individual risk assessment essential.
- Primary prevention (plaque control + tobacco avoidance) protects against both diseases simultaneously.
