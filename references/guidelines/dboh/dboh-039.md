## The recommendation

Counsel patients with high UV exposure — outdoor workers, sunbed users, fair-skinned individuals — on lip protection, and examine the vermilion border carefully at every visit.

## Why

The outer lip is exposed UV-vulnerable skin sitting on a mucosal interface. Cumulative UV damage causes actinic cheilitis, which is the malignant precursor to lip squamous cell carcinoma. Sunbeds compound the risk. The lower lip is the most affected site because it juts forward into the sun. Awareness of UV as a separate risk pathway from tobacco/alcohol is what catches lip cancer early in patients who are not in the classic oral-cancer risk group — for example, fair-skinned men with chronic outdoor occupations who do not smoke.

## How this applies in practice

- Advise sun-protective clothing (broad-brimmed hat) and SPF 30+ lip balm for outdoor workers, fair-skinned patients and sunbed users.
- Avoid sunbeds — Class 1 carcinogen.
- Examine the vermilion border, focusing on the lower lip, as part of every routine extraoral exam.
- Recognise actinic cheilitis: blurring of the vermilion border, persistent dryness or scaling, pale atrophic patches. Refer for biopsy.
- A persistent ulcer or non-healing crust on the lower lip in a fair-skinned outdoor worker is lip cancer until proven otherwise.
- NICE NG34 covers sun exposure risks and benefits and is worth knowing for the dental team's broader counselling role.
