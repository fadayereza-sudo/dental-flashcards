## The recommendation

Provide intensive long-term preventive care for patients who have had head and neck radiotherapy, chemotherapy or surgery, recognising they are at increased risk of dental caries, oral mucositis, periodontitis, peri-implantitis and osteoradionecrosis.

## Why

Head and neck radiotherapy permanently damages salivary glands, producing xerostomia that lasts for life, and irradiated bone has reduced vascularity that limits healing — extractions in irradiated bone risk osteoradionecrosis. The xerostomia removes the patient's natural caries defence, so caries explodes if fluoride is not maximised, and the mucosa is fragile, with episodes of mucositis from minor trauma or treatment cycles. Limited mouth opening from surgical or radiotherapy fibrosis compounds the access problem. The dental team's job changes from routine recall to intensive, lifelong support: stabilise pre-treatment, then prevent rather than restore.

## How this applies in practice

- Stabilise oral disease before cancer treatment starts: extract any tooth of doubtful prognosis pre-radiotherapy, place fluoride mouth trays, achieve perio stability.
- Lifelong: 5,000 ppm fluoride toothpaste, fluoride varnish at 3-monthly recalls, daily fluoride mouth rinse, saliva substitutes (sprays, gels, sugar-free chewing gum).
- For any extraction in previously irradiated bone, refer to a maxillofacial unit — atraumatic technique, antibiotic cover and consideration of hyperbaric oxygen depending on local protocol.
- Counsel the patient that the increased risk is permanent — not just the few years after treatment.
- Reduced mouth opening (trismus) limits chair time and access; book longer appointments and consider supine positioning.
- Patients living with the long-term consequences of cancer treatment carry a high psychological burden; integrate sensitive communication into every visit.
