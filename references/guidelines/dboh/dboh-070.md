## The recommendation

Limit fruit juice and smoothies to a combined total of 150 ml per day, taken with meals. One 150 ml portion counts as one of the 5-a-day, no matter how much is drunk. A portion of fresh, frozen, canned (in juice) fruit or vegetables is 80 g; a portion of dried fruit is 30 g.

## Why

Fruit juice and smoothies contain free sugars released from the fruit cell walls — once liberated, they behave like added sugar in the mouth and contribute to caries and weight gain. Capping them at 150 ml limits the damage and counts the contribution at one 5-a-day portion regardless of total volume so that drinking more does not "earn" more credit. Whole fresh, frozen, or canned-in-juice fruit retains the fibre and slower consumption pattern that protect teeth.

## How this applies in practice

- One small (150 ml) glass of juice with breakfast = one of the 5-a-day. After that, water.
- Smoothies count too — they're often portrayed as "all fruit", which is true but misleading because the cell walls are broken.
- Dried fruit portion is small (30 g, about 3 dried apricots or 1 tbsp raisins); easy to overshoot.
- Patients trying to "be healthy" with a daily 500 ml smoothie are exposing themselves to a substantial free-sugar load — name this.
- Eatwell Guide encourages 6–8 cups/glasses of fluid daily — water, lower-fat milk, sugar-free drinks, tea and coffee all count; juices contribute but are limited.
