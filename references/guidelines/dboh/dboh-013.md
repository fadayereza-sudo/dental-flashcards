## The recommendation

When a patient is ready to act, agree on SMART goals — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Timely — rather than generic exhortations.

## Why

Vague goals like "brush better" cannot be measured, monitored or adjusted, so the patient cannot tell whether they are succeeding. SMART forces every step of a plan to be concrete enough to be assessed at the next visit. Specific provides focus; Measurable lets you and the patient track progress; Achievable means the goal is in reach (an unreachable goal demoralises rather than motivates); Relevant means it matters to the patient personally, not just to the dentist; Timely sets the deadline that keeps motivation alive. Setting the bar slightly above current behaviour but within reach is what builds self-confidence over successive cycles.

## How this applies in practice

- Bad goal: "improve interdental cleaning."
- SMART version: "Use TePe brushes between every lower posterior contact, every night before bed, for the next 6 weeks until our review."
- For a smoker not ready to quit: "Cut down from 20 to 15 a day in the next month, with a quit date set at our next review" beats "stop smoking soon."
- If the goal is impossible (a frail older patient committed to flossing every tooth daily), it will collapse and the patient will feel they failed — pick the next achievable rung instead.
- Write the goal in the notes verbatim — at the next visit you can ask whether the specific commitment was kept.
