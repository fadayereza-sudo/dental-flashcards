## The recommendation

Use the WHO Basic Periodontal Examination (BPE) probe to record one code per sextant — 0 (healthy), 1 (bleeding), 2 (plaque retention factor), 3 (pocket 4–5.5 mm), 4 (pocket ≥6 mm), * (furcation involvement) — at every periodontal screen.

## Why

The BPE is a screening tool, not a diagnosis: it is fast, reproducible across operators, and tells the clinician where to look more carefully. The probe has a 0.5 mm ball end and a black band from 3.5–5.5 mm. If the band is partially obscured, the pocket is 4–5.5 mm (code 3); if fully obscured, the pocket is ≥6 mm (code 4). Codes 0–2 mean primary prevention is enough; codes 3–4 trigger a detailed periodontal assessment with a full pocket chart and radiographs to stage and grade the disease. The asterisk for furcation involvement flags an architectural risk that worsens prognosis regardless of pocket depth.

## How this applies in practice

- Use a WHO BPE probe with light force (around 20–25 g — fingernail blanching).
- Record one code per sextant; the sextant gets the highest code present.
- A BPE of 3 in any sextant requires a 6-point pocket chart in that sextant and initial therapy with self-care advice.
- A BPE of 4 in any sextant requires a 6-point pocket chart of the entire dentition plus radiographs.
- The BPE does not give a diagnosis or assess treatment response — pockets often persist despite successful treatment.
- Furcation (*) in any sextant is treated like code 4 for management.
