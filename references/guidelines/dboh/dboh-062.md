## The recommendation

For vulnerable adults — those lacking manual dexterity or mental capacity — oral hygiene care should be tailored to the individual and their carers. Care-home staff should provide daily mouth care as set out in the resident's personal care plan after their oral health assessment, including denture care, removing dentures overnight, and using any prescribed high-fluoride toothpastes or mouthrinses (NICE NG48).

## Why

Oral hygiene is a daily activity, so the care plan must address the daily reality of who is doing what. Without explicit care-plan instructions, mouth care is the first thing skipped on busy shifts. Removing dentures overnight reduces denture stomatitis and mucosal trauma. Partial denture wearers in particular have higher plaque retention on remaining natural teeth and so benefit from extra attention — they sit between "fully dentate" and "edentulous" in care planning and are often missed.

## How this applies in practice

- Care-home oral health assessment should produce a written, individualised mouth-care plan — not just "brush teeth daily".
- Specify: which toothbrush (manual or powered), which paste (and ppm), denture cleaning routine, overnight denture removal, any prescribed mouthrinse.
- Triple-headed manual brushes have low-certainty evidence; some patients find them helpful, others find them awkward — try before adopting.
- For learning-disabled patients, powered brushes may help but some find them distressing — assess individually.
- Adapted-grip handles help dexterity issues; this is cheap and underused.
- Train carers in the technique — they often haven't been shown how to brush someone else's teeth effectively.
