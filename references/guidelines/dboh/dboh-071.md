## The recommendation

Use the Very Brief Advice (VBA) pathway with every patient: Ask about tobacco use at every visit, Advise that the best way to quit is a combination of specialist support and medication (do not ask if they want to quit), and Act according to their response — refer interested patients to local stop-smoking services, and for those not interested, leave the door open with "It's your choice. Help will always be available."

## Why

VBA takes 30 seconds and roughly doubles a patient's chance of quitting. The crucial design feature is *not* asking "do you want to quit?" — that triggers defensiveness. Instead, you give the same evidence-based advice to all smokers, regardless of stated readiness. Most smokers know they should stop and need professional offers, not warnings. People are 3 times more likely to quit successfully with combined specialist support and pharmacotherapy than with willpower alone. Many patients need VBA on multiple occasions before they act — keep delivering it.

## How this applies in practice

- Ask at every visit; record current/ex/never status; for ex-smokers ask when they stopped.
- Ask specifically about smokeless products too: "Do you use snuff, paan, chewing tobacco, or shisha?"
- Avoid the warning-and-pressure approach — it costs you the conversation and any future opportunity.
- Use the offer phrasing: "Research shows the best way to stop is with a combination of specialist support and medication. You are up to 4 times as likely to quit with assistance. I can help arrange both."
- For interested patients: actively refer (don't just signpost) to a local stop-smoking service; mention NRT and that e-cigarettes can also help.
- For uninterested patients: "It's your choice. Help will always be available. Let me know if you change your mind." Record and re-ask next visit.
- People who have completely switched to vaping should be recorded as non-smokers in dental records.
