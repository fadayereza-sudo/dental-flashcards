## The recommendation

Treat diabetes and periodontitis as bidirectionally linked: assume any patient with diabetes is at increased perio risk, ask about HbA1c, and explain that periodontal therapy can improve glycaemic control.

## Why

Hyperglycaemia drives advanced glycation end-products and immune dysregulation that worsen periodontal inflammation, and periodontal inflammation drives systemic inflammation that worsens insulin resistance. Well-controlled diabetes is not itself a risk factor, but most patients oscillate between levels of control across months and years, so the safe assumption is increased perio risk for anyone with the diagnosis. International expert consensus supports periodontal therapy as a measure that can reduce the risk of diabetes complications, which is why HbA1c is part of the perio history. Target HbA1c is 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) or below; consistently below 7.0% (8.6 mmol/L) indicates good control.

## How this applies in practice

- Ask "Do you have diabetes? When was your last HbA1c? Do you know the number?" at every recall in known diabetics.
- An HbA1c above 7.0% means impaired healing and a worse response to periodontal therapy — explain this to the patient.
- Consider writing to the GP or diabetes physician for the HbA1c if the patient does not know it (a downloadable template letter accompanies DBOH).
- After periodontal treatment, write back to the diabetes team — the periodontist's letter is part of the patient's diabetes care.
- Type 2 diabetics in supportive periodontal care: moderate-certainty evidence shows perio treatment improves metabolic control and reduces systemic inflammation, though the durability beyond 4 months is uncertain.
