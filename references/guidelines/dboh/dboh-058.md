## The recommendation

Brush all tooth surfaces and the gumline at least twice a day (last thing at night and on at least one other occasion) with fluoride toothpaste, for at least 2 minutes, using a small-headed brush with medium-texture bristles. Spit out excess toothpaste; do not rinse.

## Why

Two minutes is not arbitrary — it is the time needed to reach every surface systematically. Most people who brush "for ages" still miss the same areas because they brush by feel rather than pattern. A small head reaches distal surfaces of last molars; medium bristles strike the balance between plaque removal and gingival safety (low-certainty evidence shows hard bristles cause more gingival lesions). Spitting without rinsing leaves a residual fluoride film in the plaque biofilm that continues to remineralise enamel for hours. The night brush matters most because saliva flow falls during sleep.

## How this applies in practice

- Anchor advice on the night brush ("last thing at night, or before bed for shift workers") — saliva flow drops in sleep.
- Filament type doesn't matter clinically — pattern and pressure do. Address the patient's existing technique rather than prescribe a new one.
- A timer (egg timer, brush timer, app) helps with the 2-minute target — most patients underestimate brush time by half.
- Disclosing tablets are the cheapest, most powerful teaching tool for adults who keep getting plaque scores back; they show exactly which surfaces are missed.
- Daily interproximal cleaning should start by age 18 — earlier if there is gingival inflammation.
- "Spit, don't rinse" — repeat at every recall; patients revert.
