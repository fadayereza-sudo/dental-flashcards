## The recommendation

Use resin-based pit and fissure sealants in preference to glass-ionomer for caries prevention on permanent teeth, applied on eruption in higher-risk children, and check at every visit for wear, integrity and leakage.

## Why

Moderate-certainty Cochrane evidence shows resin-based sealants prevent and arrest caries in permanent teeth for up to 48 months. The evidence for glass-ionomer based sealants is inconclusive — they bond chemically and release fluoride, but trial outcomes are inconsistent enough that resin remains the default. A sealant is not a one-off treatment; placement is highly technique-sensitive, with poor isolation or contamination causing micro-leakage and silent caries underneath. Once retention is lost, the protection is lost — so the value of the sealant depends entirely on whether the team checks and re-seals it.

## How this applies in practice

- Default product: bisphenol-A-glycidyl methacrylate resin sealant; glass-ionomer is a fallback when isolation is impossible (e.g. partially erupted molar that cannot be kept dry).
- Apply on eruption to first and second permanent molars in children at higher caries risk — do not wait until decay appears.
- Isolation is everything: rubber dam if achievable, otherwise cotton rolls and high-volume aspiration with a cooperative child.
- Examine sealants at every recall — wear, marginal staining, marginal leakage. Re-seal if the integrity has gone.
- Consider opportunistic sealing of newly exposed proximal surfaces, e.g. the mesial of a 6 once the deciduous E exfoliates, or a surface laid bare during an adjacent restoration.
