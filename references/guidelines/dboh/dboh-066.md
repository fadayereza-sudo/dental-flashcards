## The recommendation

Free sugars should be as low as possible in the first year of life. Between meals, give young children only plain milk or water; baby juices and sugary drinks should be avoided, particularly at bedtime. The recommendation to avoid food and drinks containing free sugars before bedtime in children is based on physiology and applies overnight as well.

## Why

Salivary flow drops markedly during sleep, so the buffering and clearance that protect teeth during the day are not available. Sugars in the mouth at bedtime sit on the teeth, fermenting in plaque, for the whole sleep period. A consistent positive association between caries risk and free-sugars intake around bedtime has been shown across age groups (3–5, 6–11, 12–16 years). Although the evidence is low-certainty (research design is hard), the physiology is clear and the cost of the recommendation is nil.

## How this applies in practice

- The bedtime drink is a high-yield conversation: ask parents what is in the cup or bottle that goes to bed with the child.
- Acceptable: water, plain milk before brushing.
- Unacceptable: fruit juice, squash, "baby juice", flavoured milk, sweetened plant milks.
- After tooth brushing, nothing but water — this rule covers adults too if they snack overnight.
- Bedtime is also when fluoride toothpaste should be doing its protective work (because the mouth is not being rinsed by saliva), so don't undo it with a sugary nightcap.
