## The recommendation

Take a comprehensive medication history at every perio assessment — most importantly, identify drugs that cause dry mouth (notably antidepressants) and drugs that cause gingival enlargement (most commonly calcium channel blockers, also phenytoin and ciclosporin).

## Why

Dry mouth removes the saliva that buffers acid and clears bacteria, accelerating both caries and periodontal inflammation. Gingival enlargement creates pseudo-pockets that the patient cannot clean, locks plaque against the tissue and amplifies inflammation, and progresses faster the higher the plaque score. Calcium channel blockers (especially nifedipine and amlodipine), phenytoin and ciclosporin are the classic offenders, but the list is wider. Recognising the medication is what differentiates "this patient cannot remove plaque properly" from "this medication is making plaque control biologically impossible at the current threshold," and it changes the management plan.

## How this applies in practice

- Ask "Has your medication changed since I saw you?" at every recall and check the BNF if you do not recognise a drug.
- Flag dry mouth subjectively and objectively — ask, look, lift the tongue.
- For a patient on amlodipine with overgrown anterior gingiva, write to the GP asking whether an alternative antihypertensive is appropriate; document the request even if declined.
- Consider short-term chlorhexidine mouthrinse alongside intensified plaque control for symptomatic enlargement.
- Increase recall frequency and professional debridement for patients on chronic offending drugs whose medication cannot be changed.
- Template letters for both gingival enlargement and diabetes liaison are published alongside DBOH.
