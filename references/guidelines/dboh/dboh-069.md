## The recommendation

Be alert to commercial baby foods marketed as "natural" or "organic" — the sugars they contain are still cariogenic. Discourage feeding patterns that promote grazing, especially pouches with nozzles and "pacifier-type" feeders, because they prolong contact between sugary foods and teeth.

## Why

"Natural sugars" on a baby-food label does not mean caries-safe — fruit juice, purée, and concentrate are all free sugars. Pouches with nozzles let toddlers self-feed sweet purée over extended periods, often "on the go", which mimics the worst pattern: frequent low-volume sugar exposure with no mealtime structure. Sweet finger-foods (biscuits, wafers, fruit puffs, fruit shapes) make up two-thirds of the baby finger-food market and are typically marketed as "healthy snacks" because of fruit content. They are not.

## How this applies in practice

- Read labels with parents — point out fruit juice, purée and concentrate as free sugars.
- Pouches: encourage spoon-feeding rather than direct sucking from the nozzle, and use pouches as part of a meal not as a between-meal snack.
- Recommend savoury and less-sweet first solids (broccoli, parsnip, sweet potato, carrot) to broaden taste preferences.
- Dried-fruit "snacks" are concentrated free sugars and should not be a default toddler snack.
- Whole fresh fruit and vegetables are the recommended first foods — start with single, less-sweet vegetables and only later introduce fruits.
- When new feeding products appear, ask the same two questions: how much free sugar, and how prolonged the contact?
