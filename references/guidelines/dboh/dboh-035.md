## The recommendation

Diagnose peri-implant mucositis when there is bleeding on gentle probing (with or without increased probing depth) but no bone loss beyond initial remodelling; diagnose peri-implantitis when there is bleeding on gentle probing plus increased probing depth plus bone loss beyond initial remodelling.

## Why

Peri-implant tissues respond to plaque just like teeth: superficial inflammation (mucositis, ~43% of implants) is the analogue of gingivitis and is reversible with plaque control; true breakdown (peri-implantitis, ~22%) is the analogue of periodontitis with progressive loss of supporting bone. The trick is distinguishing initial bone remodelling (1–2 mm of crestal loss in the first year after loading is normal) from disease-driven loss. Without a previous baseline, the diagnostic shortcut is bleeding/suppuration on gentle probing, probing depths ≥6 mm, and bone levels ≥3 mm apical of the most coronal intra-osseous portion of the implant.

## How this applies in practice

- Probe all implants gently — light force, walking the probe around the crown — and record bleeding and depth at every recall.
- Compare current radiographs to the prosthesis-loading baseline; without that baseline you have to use the threshold rule (≥6 mm pocket and ≥3 mm bone loss).
- Mucositis: reverse with reinforced plaque control around the implant, including interdental aids and consideration of short-term chlorhexidine.
- Peri-implantitis: refer early. Unresponsive deep pockets with bleeding, pus and progressive bone loss need specialist input.
- Risk factors for peri-implant disease: poor oral hygiene, history of periodontitis, smoking, diabetes, lack of supportive care.
