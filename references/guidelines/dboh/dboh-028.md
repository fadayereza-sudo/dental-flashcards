## The recommendation

Do not provide "routine scale and polish" to adults with good periodontal health; no clinical benefit has been demonstrated.

## Why

The Cochrane review on routine scale and polish (Lamont et al., 2018) found no clinical benefit in healthy adults — neither for plaque scores, gingival inflammation nor caries outcomes. The intervention persists in dental culture because it feels productive and patients expect it, but it consumes appointment time, generates iatrogenic discomfort, and substitutes a passive experience for the active behaviour change (toothbrushing, interdental cleaning) that actually drives perio outcomes. Where there is no plaque retention factor and no inflammation, there is nothing for the scale to improve. The corollary: in patients with established periodontitis, professional mechanical plaque removal of supra and subgingival deposits is part of supportive periodontal care and is justified.

## How this applies in practice

- A healthy adult with BPE 0 attending for recall does not need a scale just because they have come; they need the medical/dental history check, the soft-tissue exam, the BPE and risk-based prevention.
- Frame this for patients: "Your gums are healthy — what keeps them that way is what you do at home, not what I do here."
- Removing visible calculus or stain that the patient finds objectionable is reasonable cosmetic care, but bill and document it as such, not as "perio treatment."
- For patients with periodontitis, scaling and root surface debridement of pockets ≥5 mm with bleeding on probing is part of supportive perio care, not "routine scale and polish."
- This is the most counter-cultural recommendation in DBOH; expect resistance from long-standing patients and explain the evidence.
