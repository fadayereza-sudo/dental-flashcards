## The recommendation

Ask all patients about smokeless tobacco using local product names — paan/zarda (tobacco added to betel quid), gutkha, naswar/nas/niswar, khaini, snuff, snus, shisha (waterpipe), and heat-not-burn products. All forms of legal UK tobacco carry oral cancer risk; the same VBA approach applies, with referral to specialist support for those wanting to quit.

## Why

Smokeless tobacco use is concentrated in specific cultural communities and is consistently under-asked-about in primary dental care. Paan with areca nut, common in South Asian communities, is strongly associated with oral cancer in the Indian subcontinent and increasingly in the UK. Shisha is widely (and wrongly) believed to be safer than cigarettes because the smoke is "filtered" through water; in fact it produces tar, carbon monoxide and carcinogens at levels comparable to cigarettes. Heat-not-burn devices have minimal long-term safety data and are largely industry-promoted.

## How this applies in practice

- Add smokeless tobacco to the medical-history script — "Do you use any tobacco product, like snuff, paan, chewing tobacco, or shisha?"
- If a patient does not recognise the English term, use a visual aid — figure 11.1 in DBOH or the smokeless-tobacco products briefing.
- Examine for typical patterns: leukoplakia or erythroplakia in the buccal sulcus where chew is held, oral submucous fibrosis (limited mouth opening, blanching) from areca nut.
- Refer suspicious lesions urgently per oral cancer pathway (chapter 6).
- Deliver VBA as for cigarette smoking; cessation services accept smokeless tobacco users.
- Record outcome in clinical notes regardless of patient response.
