## The recommendation

For patients unwilling or unable to quit completely, advise that reducing smoking now makes future quitting more likely, particularly when combined with licensed nicotine-containing products. People who reduce smoking without NRT typically compensate by drawing smoke deeper, exhaling later, and taking more puffs.

## Why

The instinctive response when smoking less is to extract more nicotine per cigarette by inhaling more deeply — so the patient gets the same nicotine but more tar and carbon monoxide per cigarette than before. This is "compensatory smoking" and it negates the harm-reduction benefit. Adding licensed NRT supplies the nicotine the body is craving so the patient does not need to drag harder on each cigarette. The strong safety profile of NRT means the benefits of recommending it for harm reduction outweigh the disadvantages, especially when the alternative is no action.

## How this applies in practice

- Frame reduction-with-NRT as a stepping stone, not the destination — "cutting down with patches makes you more likely to quit fully later."
- Patients who reduce without NRT often feel they're doing well but are not actually reducing harm — explain compensatory smoking.
- All forms of NRT are appropriate for harm reduction, not just for total cessation.
- Re-ask about quitting at each visit — many smokers move from "not interested" to "ready" over months.
- For patients in treatment for substance-use disorder, smoking-cessation interventions during treatment increase tobacco abstinence — don't assume "now is not the time".
