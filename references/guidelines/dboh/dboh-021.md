## The recommendation

Set the recall interval for each patient on the basis of individually assessed risk, not a default 6-month booking — the range is 3 to 12 months for children and 3 to 24 months for adults.

## Why

There is no evidence that any specific recall interval per se influences caries development or progression. What matters is matching the visit frequency to the patient's risk and to the preventive interventions they need to receive (fluoride varnish for children, sealants on eruption, supportive perio care, etc.). A blanket 6-month policy under-treats the highest-risk patients (who need 3-month intervals to keep ahead of disease) and over-treats stable, low-risk adults (who can safely go up to 24 months without losing control). Recall must be reassessed at every visit because risk changes across the life course.

## How this applies in practice

- For a low-risk caries-free adult with stable perio, 12–24 months is defensible if you document the basis.
- For a higher-risk child needing 6-monthly fluoride varnish, the recall is anchored to the varnish schedule, not the other way round.
- For a frail older adult entering a care home with new xerostomia and polypharmacy, default to a short recall (3–6 months) until you know the trajectory.
- Document the risk assessment and the resulting interval at every visit — "recalled in 6 months" without rationale is an audit weakness.
- For care-home residents, NICE NG48 supports oral assessment on entry and shortened recall — these patients are almost always higher risk.
