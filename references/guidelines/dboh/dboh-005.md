## The recommendation

Use only a smear of fluoride toothpaste for children under 3 years, and a pea-sized blob for children aged 3 to 6 years.

## Why

Aesthetic fluorosis is caused by excess fluoride ingested during enamel formation, not after eruption. The crowns of permanent incisors finish calcifying by 30 months, and the crowns of premolars by 6 years — these two windows are when ingested fluoride can produce visible white-or-brown mottling on the front teeth and premolars. A smear of 1,000 ppm paste contains ~0.1 mg of fluoride and a pea-sized blob ~0.25 mg; even if a 15 kg child swallowed the lot, the dose works out at 0.007–0.025 mg/kg, which is well below the threshold for fluorosis. Reducing the *amount* of paste is what prevents fluorosis — not reducing the ppm — because dropping below 1,000 ppm sacrifices the caries-preventive effect.

## How this applies in practice

- Teach parents the visual cues: smear = a thin film flat on the brush head; pea-sized = no bigger than a small pea.
- Don't use children's "milk teeth" toothpastes that contain less than 1,000 ppm — the smaller dose, not the lower concentration, is what protects against fluorosis.
- Once a child is old enough to spit reliably (around age 7), they move on to 1,350–1,500 ppm and a normal brush-length of paste.
- Fruit-flavoured pastes encourage swallowing — start children on mild-mint family paste from the beginning if tolerated.
