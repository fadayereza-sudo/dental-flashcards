## The recommendation

For patients with active caries who are not controlled by 1,500 ppm paste, consider prescribing 2,800 ppm fluoride toothpaste from age 10, and either 2,800 or 5,000 ppm from age 16 and in adults, until caries risk is reduced.

## Why

Higher-ppm pastes deliver more topical fluoride per brush, which is what arrests early lesions and remineralises white spot lesions in patients whose risk profile cannot be controlled by over-the-counter strengths. The age cut-offs (10 for 2,800 ppm; 16 for 5,000 ppm) reflect both the maturity of swallowing reflex and the residual fluorosis risk from continuing enamel calcification — second molars and premolars are still vulnerable in younger children. Above 1,500 ppm the product becomes a prescription medicine in the UK because of the dose ingested if misused.

## How this applies in practice

- The full ladder for a child or young person: 1,000 ppm (under 7, average risk) → 1,350–1,500 ppm OTC (age 7+) → 2,800 ppm prescription (age 10+ with active caries) → 5,000 ppm prescription (age 16+ with active caries).
- For adults with obvious active coronal or root caries, jump straight to 2,800 or 5,000 ppm prescription paste while you stabilise the disease.
- These pastes are stop-gap until the risk is reduced, not a permanent change — review and step the patient down as the active disease resolves.
- Pair the higher-ppm paste with the same "spit don't rinse" rule, and be explicit that the small extra fluoride benefit only happens if the paste stays on the teeth.
- Frequent prescribing without a documented active-caries indication is a prescribing audit flag.
