## The recommendation

Counter the common misconception that nicotine causes the harm of smoking: nicotine is the addictive substance but is relatively harmless. Almost all the harm comes from the thousands of other chemicals in tobacco smoke. Recommend NRT or e-cigarettes confidently to manage cravings during a quit attempt.

## Why

UK research shows only 6 in 10 smokers and ex-smokers know NRT is less harmful than cigarettes, and 4 in 10 incorrectly believe nicotine causes most smoking-related cancer. This belief makes patients reluctant to use NRT and hesitant about e-cigarettes — they think they're substituting one cancer-causing substance for another. They aren't. Nicotine is not carcinogenic at the doses delivered by either NRT or e-cigarettes; the carcinogens in tobacco smoke are the combustion products. Correcting this misconception is part of supporting cessation.

## How this applies in practice

- Use a one-line correction when offered: "Nicotine is what makes cigarettes addictive, but it's the smoke — the tar and other chemicals — that does the harm. NRT and vapes give you the nicotine without the smoke."
- Reassure long-term NRT users that ongoing nicotine replacement is far safer than continued smoking.
- The same logic applies to pregnancy — NRT is licensed in pregnancy; the alternative (continued smoking) is much more harmful to the baby.
- For patients who have switched to vaping, do not undermine the switch by warning them about nicotine — focus on stopping smoking completely.
- This conversation goes hand-in-hand with VBA — building confidence in cessation aids increases uptake.
