## The recommendation

Consider topical remineralising agents such as casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate (CPP-ACP) as an adjunct — not a substitute — for high-risk patients already using high-concentration fluoride paste.

## Why

Remineralisation needs both fluoride and a supply of calcium and phosphate ions to lay back down on the demineralised lattice. In healthy mouths saliva supplies the ions; in xerostomic or very high-risk patients it does not. CPP-ACP binds calcium and phosphate to the biofilm and modulates pH and bacterial colonisation, supplying the ions the saliva is failing to provide. The evidence base supports its use in early enamel lesions in high-risk patients, but only as an add-on to high-fluoride therapy — it does not outperform fluoride alone in average-risk mouths.

## How this applies in practice

- Indicated for high-risk patients already on prescription fluoride paste (2,800 or 5,000 ppm) who continue to develop new white-spot lesions.
- Common products are CPP-ACP creams (e.g. Tooth Mousse) applied at night after brushing.
- Avoid in patients with a true milk-protein allergy — CPP-ACP is derived from casein.
- Do not present it to patients as a fluoride alternative; that misses the mechanism and risks them dropping the more important intervention.
- For root caries in older adults with xerostomia, CPP-ACP after the high-ppm paste is a reasonable add-on, but the foundation remains fluoride and dietary change.
