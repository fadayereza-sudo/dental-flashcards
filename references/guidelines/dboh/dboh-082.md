## The recommendation

Classify drinking levels: low risk = up to 14 units per week; increasing risk = above 14 and up to 35 (women) or 50 (men) units per week; higher risk = above 35 (women) or 50 (men) units per week; binge = 6+ units (women) or 8+ units (men) in a single session. For patients with AUDIT-C 11–12, refer for specialist assessment — do not advise them simply to stop, because unsupervised withdrawal can be dangerous for dependent drinkers.

## Why

Alcohol withdrawal in physically dependent drinkers can cause seizures and delirium tremens, which carry significant mortality. A well-meaning instruction to "just stop" from a clinician without supervision can trigger these events. Specialist services manage withdrawal with benzodiazepines, thiamine and ongoing support. The risk categories define the appropriate intervention: brief advice is appropriate for increasing/higher risk drinking; specialist referral is appropriate when dependence is suspected.

## How this applies in practice

- Higher risk (>35 women / >50 men units per week) — these patients are likely already experiencing health damage even if not yet symptomatic.
- For AUDIT-C 11–12: the script is "Your score suggests it would be worth speaking to your GP. They can advise on the safest way to cut down."
- Alcohol dependence (ICD-11): strong internal drive to drink, impaired control, increasing priority of drinking, persistence despite harm, often with tolerance and withdrawal — usually evident over 12 months but diagnosable after 1 month of continuous use.
- Behavioural counselling (motivational interviewing or CBT) is the NICE recommendation for dependent drinkers; brief advice alone is not enough.
- Vulnerable groups (homeless, those with mental-health comorbidity) often have higher alcohol use and more complex needs — coordinate referrals.
