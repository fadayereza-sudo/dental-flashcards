## The recommendation

For higher-risk patients aged 8 and above with active caries, recommend or prescribe a daily 0.05% sodium fluoride (230 ppm) mouth rinse, used at a different time of day from toothbrushing, until caries risk is reduced.

## Why

A daily fluoride rinse adds a third low-dose topical exposure on top of twice-daily brushing — the smaller the gap between fluoride exposures, the less time the plaque biofilm spends without active fluoride. The "different time from brushing" instruction is the load-bearing part of the rule: if the rinse follows the toothpaste, it washes the higher-ppm paste fluoride straight off the teeth, sacrificing the bigger source of fluoride for the smaller. The 8-year cut-off reflects the age children can reliably rinse and spit without swallowing.

## How this applies in practice

- The standard product is sodium fluoride 0.05% (230 ppm fluoride), available over the counter or on FP10.
- Anchor it on a fixed time the patient already does — after lunch at school, after a mid-afternoon drink at work — never within an hour of brushing.
- If the patient cannot remember to use the rinse twice removed from brushing, do not prescribe it; you will lose the toothpaste benefit for no gain.
- For active root caries in adults, the rule is the same — daily 0.05% NaF rinse decoupled from brushing, alongside 2,800 or 5,000 ppm paste.
- Counsel patients that mouthwash is not a substitute for brushing; it is an addition, and only earns its place if it is timed correctly.
