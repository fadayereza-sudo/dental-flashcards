## The recommendation

Identify "patients giving concern" — those at higher risk of dental disease or for whom disease or its management would pose a significant challenge — and apply additional preventive support beyond the universal advice.

## Why

Universal preventive advice keeps low-risk patients well, but it does not deliver the gain higher-risk patients need. The toolkit assumes a tiered model: everyone gets the baseline (twice-daily fluoride brushing, dietary advice, varnish for children), and a defined higher-risk group gets prescription fluoride, shortened recall, sealant programmes, and liaison with medical teams. Risk changes across the life course, so it must be reassessed at every recall, not set once. Past disease is the strongest single predictor of future disease, so a child or adult with current or previous decay automatically enters the "concern" group.

## How this applies in practice

Higher-risk groups to flag in the notes include:
- Children or adults with current or past dental caries (or whose siblings have caries experience).
- Children who have needed treatment under GA or sedation.
- People with physical or learning disabilities.
- Medically compromised patients — diabetes especially.
- Mentally and physically frail older adults, including those with cognitive decline.
- Patients on cancer treatment, head/neck radiotherapy or any drug therapy causing dry mouth.
- People who are homeless or socially disadvantaged.
- Patients with hypoplasia or retained impacted third molars (specific teeth at risk).
- Patients vulnerable to treatment itself (e.g. on chemotherapy).

If you cannot tick "low risk" without hesitation, treat the patient as "giving concern" and document why.
