## The recommendation

Use the Basic Erosive Wear Examination (BEWE) at all routine examinations: record the highest-scoring surface per sextant on a 4-point scale (0–3), where 1 is initial loss of surface texture, 2 is hard-tissue loss under 50% of the surface, and 3 is loss over 50%. A BEWE of 3 in every sextant, or multiple level-3 scores, indicates severe wear that justifies further investigation.

## Why

Tooth wear is irreversible once tissue is lost, so secondary prevention depends on catching it early. The BEWE works the same way as the BPE: a simple sextant-based screening tool that flags individuals for fuller investigation, validated for use in primary dental care. Without a routine index the early signs (loss of surface lustre, frosted-glass appearance) are easily overlooked at recall, and patients only present once the wear is severe enough to cause aesthetic complaints or sensitivity.

## How this applies in practice

- Score every sextant at every routine exam — same workflow as the BPE, no extra equipment.
- Score 1 = early loss of surface texture (brightness loss, opaque or frosted-glass appearance), discrete area on the buccal surface, minimal incisal-edge loss.
- Score 2 = distinct defect, less than 50% of the surface, dentine often involved.
- Score 3 = more than 50% of the surface, dentine often (not necessarily) involved; for restored teeth, only score if the restoration covers less than half.
- Document the cumulative score so progression between visits is visible — this is what triggers the conversation about risk factors.
- Sensitivity is not a reliable progression marker — patients can have severe wear and no sensitivity, or minimal wear with cervical sensitivity from patent tubules.
