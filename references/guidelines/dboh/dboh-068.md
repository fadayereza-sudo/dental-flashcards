## The recommendation

Only breast milk, infant formula or cooled boiled water should be given in a bottle. Sugar, honey or any other sugar-containing products or drinks must not be added. From 6 months, encourage drinking from a free-flow cup rather than one with a valve that requires sucking. Discourage feeding from a bottle from 1 year of age.

## Why

Sucking from a bottle pools liquid around the teeth, especially the upper anteriors, with prolonged sugar contact. Free-flow cups (open-top or with a free-flow spout) deliver liquid more like ordinary drinking — less time pooled around teeth, less prolonged exposure. Adding any sugar to a bottle, particularly at sleep times, is the classic mechanism of "early childhood caries" with rampant decay of the upper incisors. Honey in a bottle is also a botulism risk in infants under 12 months, on top of the cariogenic risk.

## How this applies in practice

- The "what's in the bottle?" question matters more than "how often?" — sugary drinks in a bottle is the high-risk pattern.
- Free-flow cups from 6 months; off the bottle by 1 year — give parents the timeline at the eruption visit.
- Bottle propping (leaving baby in cot with bottle) is a major risk factor — name it and ask about it.
- Honey is unsafe in infants under 12 months for botulism reasons as well as caries — never recommend.
- For older toddlers still on bottles, a step-by-step transition (water in bottle, milk in cup) often works better than a hard cut-off.
