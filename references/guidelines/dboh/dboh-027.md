## The recommendation

Treat smoking as a major modifiable risk factor for periodontitis that also impairs treatment response; treat vaping as less harmful than smoking but still a perio risk relative to never-smoking.

## Why

Smoking suppresses gingival blood flow and inflammation visibly while accelerating attachment loss invisibly — bleeding on probing is reduced, so the disease is silent until it is severe. It also impairs healing after non-surgical and surgical periodontal therapy, so the same treatment delivers worse outcomes in smokers. Cessation can prevent further deterioration, which is why "ask, advise, act" is the strong recommendation at every visit. E-cigarettes appear less harmful than tobacco but inconsistent evidence suggests vapers still have higher periodontal risk than non-smokers; most vapers are former smokers and carry historic damage. Vaping is therefore a transition tool, not an end-point.

## How this applies in practice

- Record smoking status (smoker, ex-smoker, never smoker) at every recall — strong recommendation.
- A bleeding-on-probing score that looks "improved" in a smoker is not a sign of healing — the bleeding is suppressed by nicotine.
- Refer smokers who want to quit to local stop-smoking support; varenicline plus behavioural support is the most effective combination cited by DBOH.
- For patients who vape, frame it as "better than smoking, not harmless" and continue to encourage eventual cessation.
- Periodontal treatment of an active smoker without addressing smoking is treating one half of the disease.
