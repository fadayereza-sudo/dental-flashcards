## The recommendation

Brush at least twice a day with fluoride toothpaste — last thing at night and on one other occasion — and spit out, do not rinse.

## Why

Moving from once-a-day to twice-a-day brushing reduces an individual's caries risk by 14%. Brushing last thing at night matters because saliva flow drops during sleep, removing the natural buffering and clearance that protects teeth during the day. Not rinsing leaves a residual film of concentrated fluoride in the plaque biofilm, which keeps remineralising the tooth surface for hours afterwards — rinsing washes that protective reservoir straight down the sink. The combination (higher-ppm paste + twice daily + no rinsing) is what produces the population-level reduction in caries increment.

## How this applies in practice

- The "last thing at night" instruction matters more than the morning brush — anchor your advice on it.
- Tell patients to spit, not rinse. If they want a fresh-mouth feel, suggest swilling the toothpaste foam around the teeth before spitting.
- Mouthwash, if used, should be at a different time of day (not straight after brushing) so it doesn't wash off the toothpaste fluoride.
- For children supervised in brushing, the same rule holds — parents tend to rinse them out automatically.
