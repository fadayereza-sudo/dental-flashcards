## The recommendation

For children and young people from 7 years and over who give concern because of caries risk, apply resin pit and fissure sealant to permanent teeth on eruption.

## Why

Sealants physically occlude the deep occlusal grooves where the toothbrush bristle cannot reach, denying the cariogenic biofilm the sheltered habitat it needs. Moderate-certainty Cochrane evidence shows resin-based sealants prevent and arrest caries in permanent teeth for up to 48 months. The benefit is largest on first permanent molars, the highest-caries-risk teeth in childhood, and the strategy stacks with fluoride varnish and higher-ppm paste rather than replacing them. The recommendation is conditional on the child being judged at higher risk, because for low-risk children the gain over good fluoride hygiene alone is small.

## How this applies in practice

- Apply on eruption — do not wait for the tooth to be fully in occlusion if the fissures are accessible and dry.
- Use resin-based sealant as the default; glass-ionomer evidence is inconclusive but is a fallback when isolation cannot be achieved.
- The technique is moisture-sensitive; failure to keep the field dry is the commonest cause of sealant loss.
- Check sealants at every recall for wear, integrity and leakage, and re-seal where necessary — a worn-out unmaintained sealant is no protection.
- Consider sealing exposed proximal surfaces opportunistically, e.g. the mesial of a 6 once the deciduous E exfoliates, or a previously inaccessible surface laid bare during an adjacent restoration.
