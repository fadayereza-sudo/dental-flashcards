**Accessory Cusps**

- Common in the primary and permanent dentitions.
- Most common accessory cusp in the permanent dentition is the mesiopalatal cusp of the first molar (cusp of Carabelli).
- Talon cusps occur in incisors and cause aesthetic and occlusal problems.

**Invaginated Teeth**

- These result from the invagination of the enamel epithelium into the dental papilla during development.
- Correct term is a dens invaginatus or invaginated tooth — NOT dens in dente, gestant composite odontome or dilated composite odontome.
- Clinically it may manifest as a cingulum pit to a tooth which has a grossly distorted crown and root.
- More common in the permanent dentition affecting 1–5% of the population.
- Male : female ratio 2:1.
- It is most common in maxillary lateral incisors.

**Evaginated Teeth**

- This is more common in premolars and manifests as a conical projection arising from the occlusal surface in the central fissure.
- Mainly in mongoloid races with a prevalence of 1–4%.
- Made up of enamel, dentine and pulp.
- Often present with infected pulp after extra 'cusp' is traumatically fractured and pulp exposed.
