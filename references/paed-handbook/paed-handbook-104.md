**Pulpotomy in Primary Molars**

A pulpotomy involves the coronal removal of the pulp tissue that is diagnosed to be inflamed or infected as a result of deep caries. This usually leaves an intact radicular pulp tissue upon which a medicament is applied before placing a coronal restoration.

**Indications for a Pulpotomy:**

- Large proximal carious lesion with the involvement of the marginal ridge (one-third or more) in an otherwise restorable tooth
- A carious or mechanical exposure of vital coronal pulp tissue (Rodd et al., 2006)
- Tooth free from radicular pulpitis diagnosed by:
  - No history of spontaneous pain
  - Haemorrhage from the radicular tissue is easy to control
- Absence of an abscess or a fistula
- Instances where extraction is contraindicated, e.g. haemophilia.

**Contraindications for a Pulpotomy:**

- Patients with certain cardiac conditions or who have a history of heart surgery; pulpotomy is contraindicated due to the risk of precipitating bacterial endocarditis.
- In patients who are immuno-compromised (e.g. leukaemia) or have immune deficient conditions; extraction with the relevant essential precautions is usually preferred.
