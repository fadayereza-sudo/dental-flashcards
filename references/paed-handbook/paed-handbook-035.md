Sealants are materials applied to the pits and fissures of teeth in order to obliterate and hence remove the sheltered environment in which caries may thrive.

Initially developed to prevent caries, their use has been developed further and now has a place in the treatment of caries.

For sealants to be used effectively, their use should be targeted. Below is a summary of patient and tooth selection criteria for fissure sealants:

Patient selection:

- Children with special needs- medically compromised, physically/mentally disabled, learning difficulties, disadvantaged social background.
- Children with extensive caries in their primary teeth should have all permanent molars sealed as soon as possible after eruption.
- Children with caries-free primary dentitions do not need to have first permanent molars sealed routinely. These teeth should be reviewed at regular intervals.

Tooth selection:

- Apart from occlusal surfaces of permanent molars, other surfaces which should not be neglected are: cingulum pits of upper incisors, buccal pits of lower molars and palatal pits of upper molars.
- Sealants should be applied as soon as the selected tooth has erupted sufficiently.
- Any child with occlusal caries in one first permanent molar should have the others sound first permanent molars sealed.
- Any child with occlusal caries in one or more first permanent molars should have the second permanent molars sealed.
