**Posterior Cross-bites with Displacement**

A posterior cross-bite is an abnormal buccolingual relationship of a tooth or teeth when the two dental arches are brought into centric relation. A unilateral cross-bite with a mandibular displacement on closure (initial contact cusp to cusp, achieving full intercuspation when the mandible displaces laterally) can be corrected during the mixed dentition by expansion of the upper arch. Correction with an upper removable appliance is dental rather than skeletal, as the major component of tooth movement is tipping.

Following correction of the posterior cross-bite the posterior capping is removed and the patient advised to wear the appliance as a retainer for a period of six months full-time and six months night-time wear.
