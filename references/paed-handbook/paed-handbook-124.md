**Non-Accidental Injury**

This is a very difficult area and any suspicions should be discussed with a senior member of staff or consultant before discussing your suspicions with the parent and or child. Part of the postgraduate and undergraduate course is dedicated to this area to increase awareness of what is expected of dentists. Our major role is to identify where we are suspicious and refer the child for a second and specialist opinion. For the Leeds Dental Institute this is to the on call specialist registrar in paediatric medicine. If appropriate, concerns may need to be discussed with the Safeguarding Team.

Children can present to the department either where they may have suffered dental trauma or as part of an unrelated appointment e.g. routine check-up. Occasionally dentists will refer children in to see a Paediatric Dental Consultant for a second opinion where they have suspicions.

A thorough history is required of both how the accident happened, your clinical finding and what was said by the different parties involved. Suspicions of non-accidental injuries may arise when:

- Has there been delay in seeking dental advice, for which there is no satisfactory explanation?
- Does the history change over time or not explain the injury or illness?
- When you examine the child, are there any injuries that cannot be explained? (E.g. fractured teeth, discoloured teeth, bruising, healing bone fractures).
- Are you concerned about the child's behaviour and interaction with the parent/carer?

If the answer to any of these questions is YES you should discuss with a senior colleague and follow local child protection procedures. If all the answers are NO then diagnose and treat as normal.
