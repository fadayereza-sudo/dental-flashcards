The parents' behaviour and their attitude towards dentistry can have a key effect on their child's subsequent behaviour in the dental setting. Because of this, effective communication with parents and providing specific information to them about their role during dental treatment can be of critical importance. However, parents who frequently interrupt communication between the child and the dentist, either with non-dental chitchat or with attempts to give their child instructions or encouragement, can cause distraction and confusion, which lead to an increased probability of disruptive behaviour in the child. Such interventions are often very genuine attempts to offer support to the child, but frequently reflect the parents' own anxieties about dental treatment.

Even though parents may feel they are doing their best to disguise such feelings, children are very perceptive to both verbal and non-verbal clues of suppressed anxiety exhibited by their parents. It is therefore important to ensure that the parents clearly understand their role in the dental surgery. This was most clearly explained by Frankel forty years ago: "The presence of a passively-observing mother can be an aid to the child. This can be accomplished if the mother is motivated positively, is instructed explicitly, and co-operates willingly in the role of a 'silent helper'". In addition, having the parent in the surgery helps to avoid any misunderstanding should the child start to become upset during dental treatment. It also allows the dentist to involve the parent in decisions about how best to proceed should problems start to occur.
