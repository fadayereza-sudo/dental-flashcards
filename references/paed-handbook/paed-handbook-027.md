Achieving changes in dietary habits is extremely difficult; therefore advice must be individual, practical and realistic. It should not be all negative, and positive alternatives should be identified. Advising parents to completely stop their children from eating sugary food is not practical. The main dietary message to caries prevention is to reduce the frequency and amount of sugary food and drinks.

Basic advice should be given to every patient. This especially applies to parents of young children who need to be given advice at the appropriate age of the child. Basic advice can include:

- Restrict sugary food and drinks to no more than 4 occasions per day.
  - However, provided patients are brushing twice daily with a fluoride toothpaste, they should safely be able to have 5 meal moments per day (Duggal et al., 2001), for example, 3 main meals and maximum 2 snacks.
  - Confining sugary food and drinks to meal times reduces the time that teeth are exposed to acid attack. Therefore, it is advisable to have any sugary treats as a pudding right after the main meals.
  - 'Grazing' sugary food between meals and sipping soft drinks containing sugar/acid over prolonged periods should be discouraged.
- Snack on sugar-free snacks such as fresh whole fruits (apple, banana, grapes), vegetable sticks (carrot, peppers, cucumber), breadsticks and cheese.
  - Although fruit does contain natural sugars, at normal consumption levels there is no evidence that it increases caries risk.
- Drink only still and non-flavoured water or milk between meals.
  - Cow's milk is non-cariogenic.
  - Sweetened/flavoured milk and soy formula milk contain high level of added sugar.
  - Fruit juices contain concentrated levels of sugars and acids.
- Give only water or milk in feeding bottles.
  - From 6 months onwards infants should be introduced to drinking from a free-flow cup.
  - From 1 year onwards feeding from a bottle should be discouraged.
- Avoid adding sugar to weaning foods or drinks.
- Be aware of hidden sugars in some foods and the acid content of drinks.
  - Many processed foods contain high levels of sugar and acid. For example, a glass of fruit smoothie contains a much higher concentration of sugar and acid than a portion of its whole fruit counterpart.
- Do not eat or drink after brushing at night.
