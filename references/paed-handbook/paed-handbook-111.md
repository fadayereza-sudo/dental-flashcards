**Indirect Pulp Capping (IPC)**

In IPC, the non-remineralisable infected carious tissue is removed and a layer of affected but remineralisable carious tissue is left at the deepest sites of the cavity preparation to prevent pulp exposure. The infected carious tissue is the superficial layer of the carious cavity that needs to be removed to allow the healing of dental pulp. It contains the majority of the microorganisms and their toxic products that presents a source of continuous insult to the pulp. Whilst the affected dentine is the deep layer that contains only few microorganisms. The affected layer can be left in place without any adverse effect on the dental pulp (Al-Zayer et al., 2003). The most important aspect is the sealing of the cavity. This is best achieved by insuring appropriate removal of carious tissue from the EDJ.

**Indications for an IPC:**

- Tooth with large carious lesion (early and advanced dentinal lesions).
- Tooth free from radicular pulpitis diagnosed by no history of spontaneous pain
- Absence of an abscess or a fistula
- No signs of carious pulp exposure

**Stages of Indirect Pulp Capping**

1. Administer local anaesthetic and apply rubber dam wherever possible.
2. Access the cavity with high speed hand piece if the carious tooth is not cavitated.
3. Remove the obvious soft and yellow carious tissue from the cavity using slow speed hand piece or hand excavator leaving firmer and darker dentine that looks intact behind.
4. Remove all carious tissues from the lateral walls of the cavity completely in order to improve the sealing of the restorative material to the tooth, thus, preventing microleakage.
5. Apply a liner (setting calcium hydroxide or light cure resin modified glass ionomer: Vitrebond) over the carious tissue left behind to stimulate pulp repair and then a base of glass ionomer.
6. Restore the tooth with a resin composite or SSC if the tooth presented with proximal caries.
