**Stages of Placing a Conventional Stainless Steel Crown**

1. **Reduction of Occlusal Crown Height:** using either a diamond stone or a tapered bur and following the occlusal anatomy, the crown is reduced to the point where the tooth is completely out of occlusion and there is room to fit a crown.
2. **Reduction of Mesial and Distal Proximal Surfaces:** using the tapered tungsten carbide bur or tapered diamond stone, the mesial and distal surfaces are reduced to ensure sufficient clearance for the crown. No gingival step should be left otherwise the crown will not seat properly; this should be checked using a probe.
3. **Selection of Crown and Trial Fit:** with experience an estimate can be made of the probable size of crown that will most likely fit. The crown is then gently placed onto the tooth and pressed into place. It should snap onto the tooth with only a reasonable amount of pressure.
4. **Crimping the Crown margins:** using the crimping pliers or Adams Pliers the edge of the crown is carefully adapted to give a tight fit to the tooth. This is important to prevent the build-up of plaque on the crown and hence lessen the prevalence of gingivitis. If properly adapted and crimped, there should be a resistance to seating of the crown followed by a snap as the crown fits into place.
5. **Cementing the Crown:** the crown should be filled with suitable cement (polycarboxylate or glass ionomer). Pressure should be applied until the cement has set and then any can be removed with an instrument such as an excavator. The contacts should be flossed, the occlusion checked and finally the crown should be polished with a prophylaxis paste.
