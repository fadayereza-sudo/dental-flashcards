**Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)**

ADHD is the most common childhood onset behaviour disorder affecting 1 in 200 children in the UK. It is also known as attention deficit disorder (ADD). Boys are more commonly affected than girls. Those affected have a greatly reduced ability to maintain attention without being distracted, to control what they're doing or saying (because of impulsivity) and to control the amount of physical activity appropriate to the situation i.e. they tend to be very fidgety. Children with more severe ADHD may be taking stimulant medication to control their behaviour. The most common stimulant drug used is methylphenidate (trade name Ritalin), which improve a child's ability to focus.

**Dental Management:**

A child with ADHD will have a limited attention span and therefore will have difficulty focusing on the dentist. If a child has severe ADHD, as with some children with ASD it may only be possible to complete dental treatment under general anaesthetic. Therefore, as with all other children with disabilities or a medical condition preventive advice and measures are paramount.

**Key Points:**

- The child should not be kept waiting and appointments need to be kept short.
- Ideally dental appointments should be arranged when the plasma level of any ADHD medication is at its optimum.
- It is important to give a child with ADHD clear instructions, to maintain eye contact and not to overload the child with information.
- Behavior management techniques such as tell-show-do, distraction and positive reinforcement should be employed to reward good behaviour.
