**Disturbances of Eruption**

For primary teeth the eruption does not tend to vary with racial group; however the eruption pattern of the primary teeth can be very variable in comparison to the permanent dentition.

Permanent tooth eruption is the subject of considerable racial variation. To be very general, for the permanent dentition: Black people tend to be ahead of Chinese / Japanese who are in advance of Caucasians. In the permanent dentition the teeth of females erupt several months before those of males.

**Premature Eruption**

This may be a familial feature and babies with high birth weights tend to erupt their primary teeth early.

**Natal and neonatal teeth:**

- Those teeth that are present at birth are natal teeth and those that erupt in the first 30 days are neonatal teeth.
- Incidence of 1 in 2000–3000 live births.
- Most commonly the lower central incisor.
- May be associated with Ellis-Van Creveld and Hallermann-Streiff syndromes.
- Can affect feeding.

**Retarded Eruption**

Delayed eruption of primary teeth can occur in children born preterm or very low birthweight.

Delayed eruption may occur in both the primary and permanent dentition in conditions like Down syndrome and Turner syndrome or in gross nutritional deficiency, hypothyroidism or hypopituitarism.

Localized causes of delayed eruption include impaction (e.g. of the first permanent molar upon the distal of the second primary molar), presence of supernumeraries or odontomes.

Finally the very early extraction of primary teeth may be associated with delayed eruption of the permanent successors.
