The dental surgery is a highly specialised environment and contains many sights, smells and sounds which are outside a child's previous experience, and as a result, may contribute to anxiety. The dental surgery environment can be made less threatening by:

- Light, warm coloured décor
- Child orientated pictures
- A few strategically placed toys
- Cabinetry design which allows the majority of equipment to be hidden from view
- The use of good quality motors and hand pieces which minimise sound and vibration

The appearance of the dentist and the dental staff may also influence anxiety. Interestingly, the specific attire worn by the dentist, e.g. the colour and design of the coat, whether the dentist wears a collar and tie or an open neck etc., does not seem to be as important as general neatness and apparent cleanliness. Attention to personal hygiene is important; bad breath can be an inadvertent cause of failure of co-operation! Protective equipment, such as goggles and face masks, may raise anxiety if worn at initial contact, but studies suggest that if these are put on after the child has initially met the dentist they have less influence on subsequent behaviour than one might expect.

The manner with which the dentist and other dental staff speak to and communicate with the child and parents is critical. Dentists with a calm, caring and empathic approach are more likely to be able to successfully manage a potentially anxious child.
