**Cerebral Palsy**

Cerebral palsy is caused by brain damage pre, peri or postnatally resulting in a disorder of posture and movement. It describes a group of non-progressive neuromuscular disorders and is classified according to the type of motor defect:

- Spastic (hemiplegia, paraplegia and quadriplegia)
- Dyskinetic or Athetoid
- Ataxic
- Mixed

**Dental Management:**

Children with cerebral palsy may present with poor oral hygiene and increased periodontal disease due to limited manual dexterity. An increased gag reflex may also make it more difficult for parents/carers to maintain an adequate standard of oral hygiene for the child. Prevention including oral hygiene instruction and support to parents/carers in caring for their child's dentition is therefore crucial. Other common clinical presentations of children with cerebral palsy are drooling, mouth breathing, bruxism, tongue thrust habit and peri-oral sensitivity. Children with cerebral palsy, particularly those with accompanying intellectual impairment may self-mutilate by biting oral soft tissues. They may also have erosion due to gastro-oesophageal reflux and have build-up of calculus and poor oral hygiene, especially if they are PEG fed.

Many children with cerebral palsy do not have any cognitive impairment but may use communication aids such as Makaton and Pictorial Exchange Communication System (PECS) which are programs designed to provide a means of communication to individuals who cannot communicate efficiently by speaking.

**Key Points:**

- Treat the child in a more upright position if they have an exaggerated gag reflex.
- Devise a rigorous preventive programme particularly if the child has had surgery to reposition submandibular salivary gland ducts to a more posterior position to reduce drooling, as this will have left them at high risk of dental caries to the lower anterior teeth in particular.
- Give support and advice to parents and carers as to the optimal ways to care for their child's teeth.
