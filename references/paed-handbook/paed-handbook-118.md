**First Permanent Molars of Poor Prognosis**

- First permanent molars are rarely the teeth of choice for extraction for orthodontic reasons, but the planned removal of poor quality, hypomineralised or carious first permanent molar teeth can be a useful treatment option
- A dental panoramic radiograph must be taken to confirm the presence of all permanent teeth
- The ideal time for removal of lower first permanent molars of poor prognosis in a Class I molar relationship is when the bifurcation of the roots of the lower second molars are starting to calcify, usually at about 9 years of age
- Commonly the removal of a lower first permanent molar requires a compensating extraction of the upper first permanent molar to prevent its overeruption
- Timing of the extraction of a first permanent molar in the upper arch is not as critical as there is a greater tendency for mesial drift in the maxilla
- Extraction of an upper molar does not necessitate a compensating extraction in the lower arch, because in a class I relationship the upper second primary molar partially occludes with the lower first molar
