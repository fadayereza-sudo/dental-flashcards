**Dental Management:**

A good history enquiring if there is any family history of bleeding disorders or if the child bruises or bleeds more easily than expected should form part of the standard medical history taking procedure. If a child has a diagnosed bleeding disorder, careful liaison with the child's paediatrician or haematologist is important to establish not only the diagnosis and severity of the bleeding disorder but also in coordinating and managing the child's dental treatment to ensure delivery of care is safe.

**Key points:**

- Avoid mandibular block injections as haematoma formation in the pterygomandibular region may lead to airway obstruction.
- Avoid dental extractions where possible so pulp treatment of restorable carious primary teeth may be required to avoid extractions.
- If extractions cannot be avoided they are usually best managed in the hospital setting in conjunction with the haematology team.
- Local measures such as socket dressings (e.g. surgicel) and suturing should be employed to control bleeding when teeth have been extracted. Tranexamic acid may also be considered.
