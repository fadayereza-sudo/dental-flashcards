**Missing Teeth: Hypodontia**

Congenitally or developmentally absent teeth:

- Congenital relates to a condition that is present at birth, as a result of either heredity or environmental influences.
- Developmentally absent refers to a structure that normally forms after birth but has failed to do so.

So an absent second premolar is NOT congenitally absent as it does not begin to develop until after birth, it would be more accurately described as developmentally absent. An absent primary central incisor (normally present at birth) would be correctly described as congenitally absent.

**Hypodontia is the term used for absence of teeth:**

- Primary tooth hypodontia:
  - 0.1–0.9% Caucasian population, more common in the maxilla and equal between males and females.
  - For 30–50% of cases of primary hypodontia will be followed by permanent anomalies.
- Permanent dentition hypodontia:
  - Prevalence of 3.5–6.5% (excluding the wisdom teeth) and equal frequency between maxilla and mandible.
  - Female : Male 4:1.
  - Most commonly affects the third molars (prevalence of 9–37%).
  - Type of tooth absent and population prevalence can vary with the racial group studied.

**Severity:**

- Mild hypodontia: <3 teeth absent
- Moderate hypodontia: 3 to six teeth absent
- Severe hypodontia: >six teeth absent
