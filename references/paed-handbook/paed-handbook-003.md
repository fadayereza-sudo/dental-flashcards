In paediatric dentistry, the dental professional should be aware of proper dosage of local anaesthetic (based on weight) to minimize the chance of toxicity and the prolonged duration of anaesthesia, which can lead to accidental lip, tongue, or soft tissue trauma. The table below describes the maximum dosages of the most commonly used local anaesthetic solutions.

**Maximum Dosages of Local Anaesthetic Solutions**

| Solution | Max dose | 1 yr (10 kg) | 3 yrs (15 kg) | 5 yrs (20 kg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2% lignocaine / 1:80,000 adrenaline (20 mg/ml) | 4.4 mg/kg | 2.2 ml | 3.3 ml | 4.4 ml |
| 3% prilocaine / felypressin (30 mg/ml) | 6.6 mg/kg | 2.2 ml | 3.3 ml | 4.4 ml |
| 4% articaine / 1:100,000 adrenaline (40 mg/ml) | 7 mg/kg | 1.75 ml | 2.6 ml | 3.5 ml |

1 cartridge = 2.2 ml.

Therefore, a good rule of thumb is that, in a fit and healthy child, the maximum LA dosage is one tenth of a local anaesthetic cartridge/kg body weight.

It is essential that students learn to give local analgesia with confidence. Control of pain and discomfort are essential for good management of restorative dentistry for children.
