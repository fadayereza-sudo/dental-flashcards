Once you have established what needs to be done, you need to discuss the treatment options with the patient and parent. The modality of care delivery may be influenced by many factors including behavioural factors, medical history, the complexity/amount of care needed and patient/parent choice.

There are five main categories of delivery of care:

- Fissure sealing
- Small restorations: gentle excavation/slow speed removal of caries without local anaesthetic.
- Local anaesthetic: topical analgesia and an injection of local analgesia often rephrased as 'jungle juice/sleepy juice' to avoid the emotive word 'injection'.
- Sedation: for children, inhalation sedation (nitrous oxide/oxygen) is especially useful for patients who are old enough to communicate well with the dentist, have had some previous experience of dental treatment with local analgesia and wish to cooperate but are too anxious to do so without extra help.
- General anaesthesia: GA allows all the dental treatment to be carried out in one visit. All other modalities should be considered first and GA should only be employed when other methods of delivery are deemed inappropriate. This is because all general anaesthetics carry risks, which include (extremely rarely) possible death. In a healthy person, the risk of death under general anaesthesia is estimated to be about three in one million.
