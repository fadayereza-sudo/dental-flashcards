The technique for placement of sealants is relatively simple but is technique sensitive. Salivary contamination of as little as half a second can affect the bond and therefore the retention of the sealant. The stages of placing a fissure sealant are detailed below:

- Clean the tooth
  - Prophylaxis before etching does not enhance retention, but the tooth does need to be free from obvious debris.
  - The tooth can be cleaned by wiping with cotton wool pledget, or brushing with a toothbrush or a bristle brush without prophy paste.
  - Prophy paste should be avoided as these are retained in the depths of the fissures preventing penetration of the resin.
- Isolate the tooth
  - Isolation is critical to successful sealant application.
  - Operator and assistant must act as a team. It is impossible for single operators to apply sealant effectively.
  - Cotton wool rolls and suction are effective means of isolation. Dry guards can be used to aid moisture control.
  - Placement of rubber dam is frequently not possible because of the stage of eruption of the tooth or level of co-operation of the child. It would be inappropriate to delay sealant application to allow further eruption to permit application of rubber dam. The application of sealant is a relatively non-invasive technique frequently used to acclimatise a child. It is difficult to justify the use of local anaesthetics and clamps for the majority of patients, on both clinical and economical grounds.
- Etch the tooth surface
  - Apply etch (37% phosphoric acid) for 20-30 seconds
- Wash and dry the tooth surface, maintaining isolation
- Apply the sealant
  - Ensure that resin flows without air inclusions to cover approximately 1/3 of the incline of the cusp.
- Light cure the sealant
- Check the sealant
  - Wipe the air-inhibited layer from the surface of the sealant- children find the taste distressing
  - Check the retention of the sealant and for flash with a probe

Existing sealants should be checked at every recall visit for wear and leakage. Worn sealants should be 'topped up' if the child is still at increased risk of caries.
