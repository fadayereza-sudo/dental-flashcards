Positive behaviour can be enhanced by:

- Finding out what the child likes to be called and then greeting the child by that name
- Giving clear, direct instructions, and avoiding 'open' questions, e.g. "Okay, John, jump up into the chair, please," is much better than, "Okay, John. Would you like to jump in my chair now, please?"
- Questioning of feelings during procedures, e.g. "How does that feel? Does that feel okay?"
- Giving feedback about the child's behaviour, e.g. "Oh, I like the way you're helping me by opening your mouth. That's the biggest, widest mouth I've seen all day!"
- Physical contact, such as placing a hand on a shoulder or holding a child's hand during or after a particularly difficult procedure
- Maintaining good eye contact with the child when communicating with them
- Giving them a hand mirror so they can see what is happening

The following usually promote negative or inappropriate behaviour and may increase anxiety (i.e. examples of what NOT to do):

- Unsolicited reassurance, for example, "Don't worry, this isn't going to hurt," can inadvertently pass on the message, "I am worried that this next bit might hurt you, so I feel I need to reassure you that it won't."
- Criticism, ridicule and put-downs are all highly ineffective. Calm and clear explanations about why a particular behaviour is inappropriate and the type of behaviour that would be better are far more effective.
- Non-dental chitchat with a third person, such as the dental nurse, or a parent
