Special tests include percussion, sensibility testing and radiographs. Usually, a tapping of the tooth lightly with your gloved finger rather than a metal mirror handle is sufficient to establish whether a tooth is tender to percussion; however, this special test can be unreliable in children. Tapping with a mirror handle is very useful if you suspect ankylosis. Sensibility testing is unreliable in primary teeth but is appropriate for permanent teeth, especially traumatised incisors.

Bitewing radiographs are essential for an accurate caries diagnosis unless all interproximal surfaces can be visualised. They also show occlusal areas and the furcation region in primary molars, and hence can show pathology. OPG radiographs are useful to assess the full developing dentition, including the presence and position of permanent successors, the developmental stage of the lower second permanent molars (when considering the prognosis of the first permanent molars), any anomalies (e.g. supernumerary teeth) and pathology. Periapical radiographs are most useful for primary molars when a pulpectomy is being considered and in trauma cases. The FGDP guidelines provide clear guidance of when to take radiographs in children.

If, for any reason, components of the examination or appropriate special tests cannot be completed, this should be recorded, noting reasons why.
