**White Blood Cell Disorders: Leukaemia**

Leukaemia is a type of cancer of the blood or bone marrow characterized by an abnormal increase of immature white blood cells, known as lymphoblasts. It is the most common form of childhood cancer with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) accounting for 75% of cases. Oral mucosal bleeding may be a presenting factor of leukaemia along with lethargy, pallor, fever and anorexia.

**Dental Management:**

Prevention of dental disease is a high priority for children with leukaemia as it is with other medical conditions. If a child with leukaemia does require dental treatment during chemotherapy, careful liaison with the child's haematologist/oncologist is necessary in order to plan the timing of the dental treatment. It may be necessary for a child to have a platelet infusion and/or antimicrobial prophylaxis prior to invasive dental treatment depending on the child's blood counts. Ideally no treatment should be carried out until the child is in remission or on maintenance therapy.

**Key points:**

- Chemotherapy has several oral side effects, including mucositis, oral ulceration, infection and bleeding. A careful oral preventive care plan is therefore necessary for all children undergoing chemotherapy in order to maintain meticulous oral hygiene, reduce the risk of oral infection and/or mucositis symptoms.
- Pulpal therapy of primary teeth during chemotherapy is contraindicated.
