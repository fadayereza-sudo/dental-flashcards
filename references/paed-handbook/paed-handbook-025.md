Brushing technique:

- 'Spit, don't rinse'
  - Rinsing with water or mouthwash (even fluoride rinses) immediately after toothbrushing will wash away the concentrated fluoride in the remaining toothpaste, thus diluting it and reducing its preventive effects.
- Help and supervision by an adult
  - Help children brush until they are at least 7 years old. Children do not have the manual dexterity to brush their teeth effectively until they can tie their own shoelaces (approximately 7 years old).
  - Continue to supervise children older than 7 years old until confident in their brushing habits.
  - Children must be discouraged from eating or licking toothpaste.
- Emphasise the need to be systematic to clean all tooth surfaces
  - Thorough cleaning should take at least 2 minutes.
- Teach the child to recognise the 'bristle sound' (ask them to listen for a 'shh-shh' sound).
- When 6s and 7s are only partially erupted, show the child and parent/carer how brushing in line with the arch can miss the vulnerable occlusal surfaces of these teeth. Emphasise the need to brush the occlusal surface from the side of the mouth.
- Another area frequently missed is the lower incisor labial surfaces. The labial gingival margin can be a sensitive area to brush especially for patients with a high fraenulum attachment. It may be necessary to retract the patient's lip to highlight plaque retention and gingival inflammation along the gum line.

Types of brushes:

While there is evidence that some powered toothbrushes (with a rotation/oscillation action) can be more effective than manual toothbrushes, more important is that the brush, manual or powered, is used effectively twice daily.
