Dental caries is by far the most common significant oral pathology we face in many child patients. Making an accurate assessment of caries risk not only informs preventive care (see prevention chapter), but also determines the oral health review recall period and the interval between special tests such as bitewing radiographs.

Although caries risk is essentially a continuum from zero risk all the way up to extremely high risk, assessing caries risk for an individual patient is not an exact science. Caries is a multifactorial disease, and overall caries risk is determined by many variables, some of which can be adjusted/controlled (e.g. diet, exposure to fluoride) and some of which cannot (e.g. social class, quantity and quality of saliva, enamel defects). Not all of these variables are easy to assess or measure. The different factors can be subdivided into three groups:

1. Past caries experience (evidence of previous dental disease in the child and in the family). This is the most consistent (and hence most important) predictor of caries risk.
2. Other factors:
   - Caries in parents and siblings
   - Medical history (especially where salivary quality or quantity is affected, e.g. head/neck radiotherapy, antihistamine use, drugs)
   - Social history and socioeconomic background (dental caries is more common in lower socioeconomic groups at a public health level)
   - Dietary habits – especially food frequency, use of sugary drinks
   - Use of fluoride – daily exposure
   - Oral hygiene – toothbrushing with appropriate fluoride toothpaste
   - Salivary flow rate and buffering capacity (not easy to routinely assess in children)
3. Clinical impression: the clinical judgment of the dentist and their ability to combine risk factors, based on their knowledge of the patient and clinical and socio-demographic information is as good as, if not better than, any other single method listed in group 2 above.

In practice, based on an assessment of these factors, it is clinically useful to classify patients into one of three caries-risk groups:

- Low caries risk (unlikely to develop new caries)
- Increased caries risk (likely to develop new caries)
- Very high caries risk (extremely high risk of new/extensive/rapidly progressing caries)

This can be achieved by considering the three groups of factors listed above and summarising your judgement of each in a table like the one below:

| RISK FACTOR | LOW | MOD | HIGH |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past caries experience | | | |
| Other factors | | | |
| Clinical impression | | | |
| TOTAL | | | |

If most of the answers are in the low column, then the patient is probably at low risk of caries. If most of the answers are in the moderate or high column, then the patient is at increased risk of dental caries. 'Very high risk of caries' is a special category for those children deemed to be at extreme risk, such as those receiving head/neck radiotherapy etc.
