**Background: Oro-facial Protection in Sports**

Compared with some types of injuries, trauma to the teeth is generally not serious; yet teeth have the lowest potential to return to a normal healthy state following injury than any other part of the body. Prevention must be the prime emphasis of sports medicine in relation to dental injuries.

The intra-oral mouth guard is an effective device, not only for prevention of sports injuries to the teeth and other oral structures, but also for providing protection against concussion. It is recommended for the following sports: soccer, rugby football, squash, handball, volleyball, racquetball, water polo, wrestling, boxing, horse riding, skiing, ski-racing, figure skating, body building, hockey, judo, karate, weight lifting, skateboarding, sky diving, surfing, moto cross, discus throwing, basketball, gymnastics, parachuting.

Intra-oral mouth protectors may be divided into three types, stock-type, mouth formed and custom made. The custom made is considered to be the most acceptable model.

All mouthguards should be examined periodically for possible defects, and where necessary, replaced. If the athlete has a mixed dentition, space to accommodate the eruption of permanent teeth can be allowed by waxing over these teeth to serve as a spacer. In the early mixed dentition stage and during orthodontic treatment the position of the anterior teeth changes rapidly which can result in the gum shield not fitting after six months or so. There are now special gum shields which are used for children undergoing orthodontics which can be adjusted or made to adapt to the orthodontic appliances.

For a successful mouthguard programme, coach, trainer, parent and player cooperation and support are necessary. Accident prone individuals should have special consideration. For example, individuals with maxillary protrusion are five times more liable to dental injuries than other with a normal overjet. Early orthodontic treatment and extra-oral protection should be considered in these cases.
