**Preparation**

- Obtain valid consent.
- Provide pre and post sedation patient instructions.
- All members of the dental sedation team should have the relevant knowledge and skills for the sedation technique and be able to manage any sedation-related complications or medical emergencies.
- A responsible adult escort, who is capable of looking after the patient unaided, should be present and accompany the patient home after treatment under conscious sedation.

**Procedure (and effects/disadvantages)**

- Use a titrated dose of nitrous oxide with oxygen.
- Monitor patient by trained operator and assistant; monitor as a minimum, the patient's clinical signs and oxygen saturation using pulse oximetry.
- Works best if combined with semi-hypnotic suggestion.
- Time of onset of sedation: titrated to desired effect over approx 5 mins.
- Duration of action: as long as the nitrous oxide is maintained.
- Recovery time: approx 3–5 mins once nitrous-oxide turned off.
- Level of nitrous-oxide should not exceed 50%.
- Minimal impairment of any reflexes.
- Some analgesia is produced.
- N₂O must be administered continuously via a mask close to the operation site and can interfere with treatment on anterior maxillary teeth.
- Level of sedation is dependent on psychological reassurance.
- Generally predictable results.
- Extent of postoperative amnesia variable.
- Must keep nitrous oxide pollution at lower level of 100 ppm over 8 hour TWA.

**Recovery and Discharge**

- The patient is orientated in time, place and person.
- Vital signs are stable and within normal limits for the patients. Respiratory status is not compromised.
- Pain and discomfort have been addressed and haemostasis achieved.
- Responsible escort present.
- Both written and verbal post-operative instructions given.
