There are 3 ways to offer fluoride on a community-wide basis: water, milk and salt.

- Water fluoridation: currently approximately 10% of England's population benefit from water fluoridation, either naturally or artificially. The West Midlands is the most extensively fluoridated area, followed by the North East of England. Information on fluoride levels in water can be obtained from the local water supplier. This is particularly important where additional fluoride is being considered for young children.
- Milk fluoridation: there are a few schemes in England which supply children with fluoridated milk at school managed by The Borrow Milk Foundation, Portsmouth. They are provided in areas which are not fluoridated and where levels of caries are high. Children should not take part if they are receiving additional fluoride.
- Salt fluoridation: Switzerland was the first nation to pioneer salt fluoridation and it is now available in Spain, Hungary, France and parts of Brazil. There is limited research on its absolute effectiveness, especially now that fluoride toothpaste is readily available and in some areas, fluoridated water.
