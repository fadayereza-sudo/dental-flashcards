The treatment plan details exactly how and in what order planned care will be delivered. For treatment involving several visits, the treatment plan should be outline visit by visit. Each visit should have a preventive and restorative component. This is because prevention is aimed at treating the causes of dental disease whilst restoration is merely aimed at repairing the damage. Usually, only one or two preventive messages are delivered at each visit so the family are not overloaded with advice.

The restorative/operative component is usually started in the maxillary arch, which is considered to be more straightforward to anaesthetise than the mandibular arch. At each subsequent visit a different quadrant is anaesthetised and all the operative work including restorations, fissure sealants and extractions are carried out to avoid repeated administration of local analgesia in the same area.
