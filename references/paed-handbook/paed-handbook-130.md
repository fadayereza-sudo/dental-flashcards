**Red Blood Cell Disorders: Anaemia**

The causes of anaemia in children may be due to blood loss, deficiencies in iron, folate and/or vitamin B12, bone marrow failure, or inherited haemaglobinopathies such as sickle cell disease or thalassaemia. Anaemia is generally considered to be present if the haemaglobin level falls below 10.0 g/dL and all anaemic children have a greater tendency to bleed after invasive treatment.

**Key points:**

- Elective dental general anaesthesia should be avoided if a child's haemoglobin is <10.0 g/dL and the child referred to their General Medical Practitioner in the first instance for treatment to raise the Hb level if it is assumed to be due to iron deficiency. In children, iron deficiency is most commonly due to dietary deficiency or malabsorption as opposed to blood loss.
- In children with sickle cell anaemia, minimise stress that may compromise the child's ability to oxygenate the tissues adequately.
- If a child with sickle cell anaemia requires a dental general anaesthetic, careful liaison with the anesthetist is necessary.
