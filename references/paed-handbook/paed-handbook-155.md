**Premature Exfoliation**

There are a number of uncommon conditions that may result in the premature loss of primary teeth. These sometimes may proceed to involve the permanent teeth. Examples are:

- Hypophosphatasia (aplasia or hypoplasia of the cementum leads to the premature loss of teeth)
- Immunological conditions that result in neutropenia may lead to gross periodontal destruction
- Histiocytosis X where invasion of histiocytic tissue destroys supporting alveolar bone

**Delayed Exfoliation: Infraocclusion**

- This is a better term than 'submerged' as the teeth are NOT actively sinking, the jaws and other teeth are actively growing around them.
- Prevalence is between 1–9% with the primary dentition being more frequently affected. The mandibular first primary molar is the most commonly affected tooth. More than one tooth in one individual may be affected and it may occur symmetrically.
- Developmentally absent premolars are more common in children who have infraoccluded primary molars than in the general population.
- Apart from those without permanent successors the infraoccluded teeth are not generally delayed in their exfoliation and the majority will exfoliate naturally within the normal range.
