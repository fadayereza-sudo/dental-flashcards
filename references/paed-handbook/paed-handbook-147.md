**Tooth Size**

- Size and shape are not easily separated and often interdependent.
- Megadont or macrodont (large teeth):
  - Macrodont teeth most often affects the maxillary permanent incisors and mandibular second premolars.
  - Prevalence in UK school children of 1.1% in the permanent dentition.
- Microdont (small teeth):
  - More common in females.
  - Microdont primary teeth are uncommon, prevalence of 0.2–0.5%.
  - Permanent teeth prevalence is 2.5%, generalized microdontia affecting 0.2%.

**Tooth Shape**

Abnormalities in shape can originate during the morphodifferentiation stage of tooth development and are manifested as alterations in crown and root form.

**Double Teeth**

There are a number of other terms: fusion, gemination, dichotomy, synodontia, schizodontia, and connation… however these can allude to possible developmental aetiologies and so the more neutral 'double teeth' is preferred.

- Prevalence in the primary dentition 0.5–1.6% (Caucasians).
- Permanent dentition is less commonly involved with a prevalence of 0.1–0.2%.
- Males and females are equally affected.
- Clinically the presentation may be as a minor notch in the incisal edge of a wider than normal tooth to the appearance of two almost separate crowns.
- Primary dentition: mainly in the labial segments of the arch and more common in the mandible.
- Permanent dentition: occur in any region of the mouth but double incisors are more frequently seen.
- Frequency of permanent anomalies following primary double teeth is 30–50% (Caucasians).
- Physiological root resorption of double primary teeth is occasionally retarded.
