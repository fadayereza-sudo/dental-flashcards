**Dentine Defects**

Those that primarily affect the dentine:

- **Dentinogenesis imperfecta (DI) Type II**
  - All teeth in both dentitions are affected.
  - Teeth are bluish or brownish in colour.
  - Primary teeth are more severely affected and later forming permanent teeth may not be affected at all.
  - Enamel chips off easily and then the dentine rapidly wears away. This is most marked in the primary dentition. The teeth may then become infected.
  - The crowns appear bulbous radiographically and the root maybe short and thin. The pulp chamber obliterates soon after eruption.
  - Prevalence of about 1 in 8000.
- **Dentine dysplasia type I (radicular dentine dysplasia, rootless teeth)**
  - Both dentitions are affected, colour ranging from bluish to brownish tinge.
- **Dentine dysplasia type II (coronal dentine dysplasia)**
  - Disparity of effect upon the primary and secondary dentitions.
  - In the primary dentition it is hard to tell it from DI Type II.
  - Permanent teeth are of normal colour but radiographically show thistle or flame shaped pulp chambers partly occluded by pulp stones.

Those defects that are associated with generalized disorders:

- Osteogenesis imperfecta (Dentinogenesis imperfecta type I), Ehlers Danlos syndrome, Vitamin D-resistant rickets, Vitamin D-dependent rickets.
