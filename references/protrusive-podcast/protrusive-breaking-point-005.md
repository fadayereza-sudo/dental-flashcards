[13:32] But there's another aspect to this as well and I think I'm increasingly aware of the stigma of mental ill health within dentistry. And despite being a welfare, well-being profession, we don't actually look after ourselves and look after our our fellow co-workers very well either. And there remains within dentistry, and it's the same in medicine, the stigma about mental ill health. We can't demonstrate this apparent so-called weakness. And despite the fact we're all biologically chained, we all understand that your ingrown toenail is a biological process, your myocardial infarction is a biological process, mental ill health is a biological process as well. And yet we separate it off to be something with stigma, something different, so we can't talk about it. I I can't go into work and tell my boss that I've been diagnosed with depression. I can't tell my partner that I've started going to Alcoholics Anonymous. I can't tell my partner that I've started antidepressants. These are all challenges that people face and I wonder why that is because we wouldn't hesitate to go in and say to our boss or our partner, "I've been diagnosed with pancreatic cancer and I've got 9 months to live."
