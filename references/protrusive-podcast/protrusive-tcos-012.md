[11:54] [Emma] And it means that you can kind of dip in and out of dental things. So I mix up my questions. I'll start off with, last visits and what you do on a routine. They'll ask them what they do for a living. I'll go, oh, by the way, what do you do for a living? Like, what's yours, what do you do for a job? And then they will have a chat, maybe they're a teacher, and they're quite stressed because they've had the GCSE class just finished, or they're just about to finish for half term, and they're changing schools, and then you go back to them, they go back to another sort of questions to do with budget, and if they want any cosmetic treatment. So I kind of break up the questions from clinical to personal to clinical and then explain what's going to happen in that checkup so they're not going to just get flung in the chair, sat back, and then all these numbers and letters are being read out, rabid x rays, next minute people leave in the room. So it's nice that they get sort of a pre chat to kind of know what to expect, especially because I find a lot of our patients come from NHS and private NHS examinations are worlds apart, especially the time that they spend in the chair.
