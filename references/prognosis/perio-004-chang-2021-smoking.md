# Smoking as a prognostic modifier in non-surgical perio therapy

**Primary citation:** Chang J, Meng HW, Lalla E, Lee CT. The impact of smoking on non-surgical periodontal therapy: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *J Clin Periodontol* 2021;48(1):60-75.

**Supporting (qualitative):** Heasman L, Stacey F, Preshaw PM, McCracken GI, Hepburn S, Heasman PA. The effect of smoking on periodontal treatment response: a review of clinical evidence. *J Clin Periodontol* 2006;33(4):241-253.

## Verbatim (Chang 2021, from abstract)

"Weighted mean difference in PD reduction: -0.33 mm, 95% confidence interval (CI): [-0.49, -0.17]."

"Weighted mean difference in CAL gain: -0.20 mm, CI: [-0.39, -0.02]."

"Baseline PD significantly affected the difference in PD reduction between two groups."

## Verbatim (Heasman 2006, from abstract — qualitative)

"The majority of clinical trials show significantly greater reductions in probing depths and bleeding on probing, and significantly greater gain of clinical attachment following non-surgical and surgical treatments in non-smokers compared with smokers."

"Data from epidemiological, cross-sectional and case-control studies strongly suggest that quitting smoking is beneficial to patients following periodontal treatments."

## Population caveats

Chang 2021 is a meta-analysis of 17 studies from mixed Western populations. Heasman 2006 is a UK-led narrative review pooling roughly 30 trials from similar populations. Effect of smoking cessation was also reported in companion meta-analyses as extra pocket-depth reduction (0.32 mm) and attachment gain (0.2 mm) in quitters over non-quitters over short follow-up.

## Take-home

Abstract-sourced pooled effect size: about 0.33 mm less PD reduction and 0.20 mm less CAL gain per site in smokers. Small per-site numbers that compound across pockets and quadrants. Every perio plan needs a documented cessation conversation.
