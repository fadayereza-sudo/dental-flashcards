# Validity of the clinical prognosis call

**Citation:** McGuire MK, Nunn ME. Prognosis versus actual outcome. III. The effectiveness of clinical parameters in accurately predicting tooth survival. *J Periodontol* 1996;67(7):666-674.

## Verbatim from abstract

"This model suggests that patients are twice as likely to loose their teeth if there is increasing mobility, if they have a parafunctional habit and do not wear a biteguard, or if they smoke."

"Teeth with worse prognosis have a worse survival rate, but the commonly taught clinical parameters used in the traditional method of assignment of prognosis do not adequately explain that relationship. Furthermore, initial prognosis did not adequately explain the condition of the tooth or accurately predict the tooth's survival."

"The effect of these clinical parameters on tooth survival is only partially reflected in the assigned prognosis initially, suggesting that perhaps some of the clinical parameters should be weighed more heavily than others when assigning prognosis."

## Cox-model factors associated with tooth loss (abstract)

Initial probing depth, initial furcation involvement, initial mobility, initial percent bone loss, parafunctional habit without biteguard, and smoking.

Individual factors doubling tooth-loss risk: increasing mobility, parafunction without biteguard, or smoking.

## Population caveats

US private practice, compliant fee-paying maintenance cohort. Ethnicity not reported.

## Take-home

The abstract does not support "X% accuracy" numbers. What it supports is: initial prognosis is an unreliable predictor of tooth survival, and specific modifiers (mobility, parafunction, smoking) each roughly double risk. Weigh those explicitly rather than collapsing into a single prognosis label.
