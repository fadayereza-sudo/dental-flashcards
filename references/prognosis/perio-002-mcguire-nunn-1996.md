# Validity of the clinical prognosis call

**Citation:** McGuire MK, Nunn ME. Prognosis versus actual outcome. III. The effectiveness of clinical parameters in accurately predicting tooth survival. *J Periodontol* 1996;67(7):666-674.

## Verbatim

"The coefficients from the model were able to predict accurately the 5-year and 8-year prognoses 81% of the time... When teeth with 'good' prognoses were excluded, the predictive accuracy dropped approximately 50%."

"Initial prognosis did not adequately explain the condition of the tooth or accurately predict the tooth's survival."

## Headline numbers

- Overall model accuracy: 81% at 5 and 8 years.
- Accuracy when "good" teeth excluded: about 50% (i.e. chance).
- Smoking alone "decreased the likelihood of improvement by 60% and doubled the likelihood of worsening in prognosis at 5 years."
- Tooth loss risk doubled with parafunction without a biteguard, with increasing mobility, or with smoking.

## Population caveats

US private practice, specialist setting, fee-paying compliant patients. Ethnicity not reported. Skews toward middle-class insured patients and may underestimate loss rates in mixed-compliance populations.

## Take-home

Clinicians can spot a good tooth reliably, but the middle categories (fair, poor, questionable) are closer to coin flips. Argues for conservative treatment and reassessment rather than pre-emptive extraction.
