# Direct restorations — evidence sources

All verbatim quotes confirmed in PubMed abstract.

## 1. Opdam et al. 2014 — IPD meta-analysis posterior composite
- *J Dent Res* 2014;93(10):943-949.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0022034514544217 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25048250/
- Design: Individual-participant-data meta-analysis, 12 longitudinal studies, 2,816 restorations (569 failed), Cox regression.
- Verbatim: "The regression analyses showed a significantly higher risk of failure for restorations in high-caries-risk individuals and those with a higher number of restored surfaces... Main reasons for failure were caries and fracture."
- Population: European practice/university cohorts.

## 2. Opdam et al. 2010 — 12-year amalgam vs composite
- *J Dent Res* 2010;89(10):1063-1067.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0022034510376071 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20660797/
- Design: Retrospective practice-based cohort, 1,949 large Class II restorations (1,202 amalgam, 747 composite), single Dutch GDP.
- Verbatim: "After 12 years, 293 amalgam and 114 composite restorations had failed. Large composite restorations showed a higher survival in the combined population and in the low-risk group. For three-surface restorations in high-risk patients, amalgam showed better survival."

## 3. Demarco et al. 2012 — annual failure rate review
- *Dent Mater* 2012;28(1):87-101.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dental.2011.09.003 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22192253/
- Design: Systematic review of 34 clinical studies (1996-2011), ≥5 years follow-up on Class I/II posterior composites.
- Verbatim: "90% of the clinical studies indicated that annual failure rates between 1% and 3% can be achieved with Class I and II posterior composite restorations depending on several factors such as tooth type and location, operator, and socioeconomic, demographic, and behavioral elements."

## 4. Kopperud et al. 2012 — Norwegian practice-based cohort
- *Eur J Oral Sci* 2012;120(6):539-548.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/eos.12004 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23167471/
- Design: Practice-based prospective, 4,030 Class II restorations in 1,873 patients (median 15 yr), mean 4.6 years follow-up.
- Verbatim: "After an average follow-up period of 4.6 yr, 61.6% of the restorations were successful, 11.2% had failed, and 27.2% were not available for evaluation (owing to patient drop-out). The mean annual failure rate was 2.9% for resin-composite restorations and 1.6% for amalgams. For resin-composite restorations, secondary caries was the most common reason for replacement (73.9%), followed by loss (8.0%), fracture (5.3%), and marginal defects (2.4%). Multilevel Cox-regression analyses identified young age of the patient, high previous caries experience, deep cavities, and saucer-shaped preparation technique as predisposing to shorter longevity of resin-composite restorations."

## 5. Schwendicke et al. 2015 — caries excavation criteria network MA
- *J Dent* 2015;43(1):1-15.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2014.10.004 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25456612/
- Design: Network meta-analysis, 28 trials (19 RCTs, 9 NRCTs), 1,782 patients / 2,555 lesions.
- Verbatim: "Risk of complications was highest when excavating until only non-stainable dentine remained, and lowest when not attempting to remove all softened dentine. Risk of pain significantly decreased if self-limiting chemo-mechanical excavation or fluorescence-assisted lasers were used instead of excavating until all dentine was hard. Not attempting to remove all softened or stainable dentine might reduce the risk of complications."

## 6. Veloso et al. 2019 — bulk-fill vs incremental composite
- *Clin Oral Investig* 2019;23(1):221-233.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00784-018-2429-7 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29594349/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 10 trials, 941 restorations, follow-up 12-72 months.
- Verbatim: "No statistically significant differences in the failure rate were observed between conventional and base/flowable bulk-fill resin composites (p = 0.31; RR 1.49; 95% CI 0.69-3.25) or full-body/sculptable bulk-fill resin composites (p = 0.12; RR 1.89; 95% CI 0.84-4.24). The present systematic review and meta-analysis indicate similar clinical performances of bulk-fill and conventional resin composites over a follow-up period of 12 to 72 months. However, clinical trials of longer duration are required."

## 7. Gao et al. 2016 — SDF arresting caries in children
- *JDR Clin Trans Res* 2016;1(3):201-210.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/2380084416661474 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30931743/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 19 prospective trials; 8 pooled using 38% SDF in primary teeth.
- Verbatim: "The overall percentage of active caries that became arrested was 81% (95% confidence interval, 68% to 89%; P < 0.001)... SDF was commonly used at 38%. It was effective in arresting dentine caries in primary teeth among children... Apart from staining the arrested lesion black, no significant complication of SDF use among children was reported."
- Population: Paediatric, predominantly East Asian and Latin American studies.

## 8. Oliveira et al. 2018 — SDF for root caries in older adults
- *J Am Dent Assoc* 2018;149(8):671-679.e1.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adaj.2018.03.028 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29805039/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis of 3 RCTs, 895 older adults, ≥12 months follow-up.
- Verbatim: "SDF applications had a significantly better preventive effect in comparison with placebo... as effective as either chlorhexidine or sodium fluoride varnish in preventing new root carious lesions... Yearly 38% SDF applications to exposed root surfaces of older adults are a simple, inexpensive, and effective way of preventing caries initiation and progression."
- Population: Older adults; study sites Hong Kong and Brazil.
