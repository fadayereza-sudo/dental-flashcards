# Smoking as a prognostic modifier in periodontal therapy

**Citation:** Heasman L, Stacey F, Preshaw PM, McCracken GI, Hepburn S, Heasman PA. The effect of smoking on periodontal treatment response: a review of clinical evidence. *J Clin Periodontol* 2006;33(4):241-253.

## Verbatim

"The majority of clinical trials show significantly greater reductions in probing depths and bleeding on probing, and significantly greater gain of clinical attachment following non-surgical and surgical treatments in non-smokers compared with smokers."

## Headline numbers

- Smokers show roughly 50 to 75% less clinical improvement (probing depth reduction, bleeding reduction, attachment gain) across included trials.
- Effect present in both non-surgical and surgical therapy, and in grade I and II furcations.
- Effect is dose-dependent with pack-years, and partly reversible after cessation (Chambrone 2013 meta-analysis).
- Paired with McGuire & Nunn 1996: smoking alone doubles the likelihood of prognosis worsening over 5 years.

## Population caveats

Narrative review, so no pooled meta-analytic effect size. Included trials are predominantly Western (North American and European) populations.

## Take-home

Smoking is the single biggest modifiable risk factor a GDP controls in perio. Every perio treatment plan needs a documented cessation conversation; the 50-75% blunting of response is the number to quote when motivating the patient.
