# Teeth vs implants in periodontally compromised patients

**Citation:** Roccuzzo M, Imber J-C, Salvi GE, Roccuzzo A. Clinical outcomes of dental implants in patients with and without history of periodontitis: A 20-year prospective study. *J Clin Periodontol* 2022;49(12):1346-1356.

Prior paper in series: Roccuzzo et al. 2012. *Clin Oral Implants Res* 23(4):389-395. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0501.2011.02309.x

## Verbatim

"PCP not compliant with SPC experienced implant loss with odds ratio of 14.59 (1.30-164.29, p = 0.03)."

"The mean number of teeth lost per patient, regardless of the reason for extraction, was 0.6 ± 1.0 for PHP, 1.3 ± 1.3 for moderate PCP and 1.9 ± 1.9 for severe PCP."

## Headline numbers

- 20-year tooth loss: 0.6 (perio-healthy), 1.3 (moderate perio history), 1.9 (severe perio history) teeth per patient.
- 20-year implant survival: 91.8% (moderate PCP), 93.1% (severe PCP).
- Peri-implantitis retreatment required in 27% of moderate-PCP implants and 47.2% of severe-PCP implants.
- Odds ratio for implant loss in non-compliant PCP patients: 14.59 (95% CI 1.30-164.29).

## Population caveats

Italian specialist-practice cohort; generalisable to well-maintained private patients, less so to mixed NHS cohorts where compliance drops out more often. European population.

## Take-home

Strategic extraction to replace a compromised but treated tooth with a "cleaner" implant isn't supported. Treated teeth last well over a decade; implants in the same patient fail more often and need frequent maintenance. Compliance with supportive care is the dominant variable for both.
