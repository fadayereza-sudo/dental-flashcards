# Patient modifiers — evidence sources

All verbatim quotes confirmed in PubMed abstract.

## 1. Moraschini & Barboza 2016 — smoking on implants
- *Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2016;45(2):205-15.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijom.2015.08.996 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26385308/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 15 studies.
- Verbatim: "The effect of smoking on implant failure rate reveals a risk ratio... OR 1.96, 95% CI 1.68-2.30; P<0.00001" and maxillary marginal bone loss "SMD 0.40, 95% CI 0.24-0.55; P<0.00001".

## 2. Monje et al. 2017 — diabetes on peri-implantitis
- *J Clin Periodontol* 2017;44(6):636-648.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpe.12724 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28346753/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 12 studies (7 quantitative).
- Verbatim: "The risk of peri-implantitis was about 50% higher in diabetes than in non-diabetes (RR = 1.46; 95% CI: 1.21-1.77 and OR = 1.89; 95% CI: 1.31-2.46; z = 5.98; p < .001). Importantly, among non-smokers, those with hyperglycaemia had 3.39-fold higher risk for peri-implantitis compared with normoglycaemia (95% CI: 1.06-10.81)."

## 3. Albandar et al. 1997 — ethnicity and early-onset periodontitis
- *J Am Dent Assoc* 1997;128(10):1393-9.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.14219/jada.archive.1997.0058 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9332140/
- Design: Cross-sectional NHANES-linked US adolescent survey.
- Verbatim: "In 1986 and 1987, about 10.0 percent of African-American, 5.0 percent of Hispanic and 1.3 percent of white U.S. adolescents had EOP."

## 4. Susin & Albandar 2014 — aggressive periodontitis epidemiology
- *Periodontol 2000* 2014;65(1):27-45.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/prd.12019 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24738585/
- Design: Narrative/epidemiological review.
- Verbatim: "The prevalence of aggressive periodontitis varies significantly between populations, and differences in race/ethnicities seem to be a key factor. Studies consistently show that aggressive periodontitis is most prevalent in Africa and in populations of African descent and is least prevalent in Caucasians in Europe and North America."

## 5. Sendyk et al. 2017 — age on implants
- *J Oral Rehabil* 2017;44(3):229-236.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/joor.12465 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27917518/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 4 prospective cohorts.
- Verbatim: "The pooled estimates suggest that the risk of implant loss in older patients is not significantly higher (RR = 0·92; 95% CI 0·43-1·96, P = 0·83) when compared to younger subjects. This systematic review suggests that age is not a limiting factor for dental implant therapy."

## 6. Chrcanovic et al. 2015 — bruxism on implants
- *Implant Dent* 2015;24(5):505-16.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1097/ID.0000000000000298 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26218253/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 10 publications, 760 implants in bruxers vs 2,989 in non-bruxers.
- Verbatim: "A risk ratio of 2.93 was found (95% confidence interval, 1.48-5.81; P = 0.002)" for implant failure in bruxers vs non-bruxers. Authors caveat: "the real effect of bruxing habits on the osseointegration and survival of endosteal dental implants is still not well established."

## 7. Ata-Ali et al. 2016 — bisphosphonates on implant survival
- *Clin Oral Implants Res* 2016;27(2):e38-46.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/clr.12526 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25406770/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis of 8 studies, 1,288 patients, 4,562 implants.
- Verbatim: "The summary odds ratio (OR = 1.43, P = 0.156) indicates that there is not enough evidence that bisphosphonates have a negative impact upon implant survival. According to the number need to harm (NNH), over 500 dental implants are required in patients receiving bisphosphonate treatment to produce a single implant failure."

## 8. Schiegnitz et al. 2022 — radiotherapy on implants
- *Clin Oral Implants Res* 2022;33(10):967-999.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/clr.13976 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35841367/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis.
- Verbatim: Overall implant survival 87.8% in head and neck cancer patients. "Radiotherapy proves to be a significant risk factor for implant loss." Implant failure OR 1.97 (95% CI 1.63-2.37, p<0.00001) irradiated vs non-irradiated; grafted irradiated bone vs native irradiated bone OR 2.26 (1.50-3.40).
