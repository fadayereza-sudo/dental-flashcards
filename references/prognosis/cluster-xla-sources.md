# XLA / M3M / MRONJ — evidence sources

All verbatim quotes confirmed in PubMed abstract.

## 1. Kostares et al. 2024 — dry socket after M3M
- *J Stomatol Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2024.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jormas.2024.101821 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38432484/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis of 28 studies, 41,859 impacted mandibular M3M extractions.
- Verbatim: "The overall prevalence of dry socket (DS) following impacted mandibular third molar extractions was estimated at 6.7 % (95 % CI 4.6-9.1 %), indicating considerable heterogeneity among the studies."

## 2. Cheung et al. 2010 — neurosensory deficit
- *Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2010;39(4):320-6.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijom.2009.11.010 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20061121/
- Design: Prospective single-centre, 4,338 lower M3M extractions in 3,595 patients.
- Verbatim: "Of the 4338 lower third molar extractions performed by various grades of operators, 0.35% developed IAN deficit... 0.69% developed LN deficit... By the end of the follow-up period, 67% of IAN deficits and 72% of LN deficits had recovered completely. Depth of impaction was related to the risk of IAN deficit (p<0.001)... Distoangular impaction was found to increase the risk of LN deficit significantly (p<0.001) and Undergraduates caused more LN deficits (p<0.001)."
- Population: Hong Kong teaching hospital; mixed-grade operators.

## 3. Ghaeminia et al. 2020 — Cochrane prophylactic M3M
- *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2020;5(5):CD003879.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003879.pub5 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32368796/
- Verbatim: "Insufficient evidence is available to determine whether asymptomatic disease-free impacted wisdom teeth should be removed or retained... Very low-certainty evidence suggests that the presence of asymptomatic disease-free impacted wisdom teeth may be associated with increased risk of periodontitis affecting the adjacent second molar in the long term."

## 4. Lo et al. 2010 — oral bisphosphonate MRONJ prevalence
- *J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2010;68(2):243-53.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joms.2009.03.050 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19772941/
- Design: Kaiser Permanente PROBE cohort, 8,572 respondents.
- Verbatim: ONJ prevalence "0.10% (95% confidence interval 0.05% to 0.20%)" equivalent to "1 of 952 survey respondents with oral bisphosphonate exposure."

### Supporting — Gaudin et al. 2015 MRONJ after XLA SR
- *J Clin Periodontol* 2015.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpe.12455 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26362756/
- Verbatim: "The risk of MRONJ after dental extraction was significantly higher in patients treated with ARD for oncological reasons (3.2%) than in those treated with per os ARD for OP (0.15%)."

## 5. Khan et al. 2015 — oncology-dose ONJ consensus
- *J Bone Miner Res* 2015;30(1):3-23.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/jbmr.2405 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25414052/
- Verbatim: "The incidence of ONJ is greatest in the oncology patient population (1% to 15%), where high doses of these medications are used at frequent intervals... In the osteoporosis patient population, the incidence of ONJ is estimated at 0.001% to 0.01%."

## 6. Limones et al. 2020 — denosumab vs zoledronate MRONJ
- *Med Oral Patol Oral Cir Bucal* 2020;25(3):e326-e336.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.4317/medoral.23324 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32271321/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 8 RCTs, 13,857 cancer patients.
- Verbatim: DRONJ "0.5 to 2.1% after 1 year, 1.1 to 3.0% after 2 years, and 1.3 to 3.2% after 3 years." BRONJ "0.4 to 1.6% after 1 year of exposure, 0.8 to 2.1% after 2 years, and 1.0 to 2.3% after 3 years."

## 7. Hua et al. 2021 — DOAC vs VKA post-XLA bleeding
- *Front Pharmacol* 2021;12:702057.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2021.702057 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34776943/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 8 studies, 1,113 patients on uninterrupted anticoagulation.
- Verbatim: "A statistically significant lower bleeding risk in patients under DOAC therapy (RR 0.68 95% CI 0.49, 0.95 I2 = 0%)... the results were statistically non-significant after exclusion of any of the included studies... Apixaban: RR 0.85 95% CI 0.45, 1.60; Rivaroxaban: RR 0.95 95% CI 0.36, 2.48; Dabigatran: RR 0.49 95% CI 0.19, 1.28; Edoxaban: RR 0.41 95% CI 0.13, 1.27... Current evidence is of very low-quality and should be interpreted with caution."

## 8. Lodi et al. 2021 — Cochrane antibiotic prophylaxis for XLA
- *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2021;2(2):CD003811.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003811.pub3 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33624847/
- Verbatim: "Antibiotics may reduce the risk of postsurgical infectious complications in patients undergoing third molar extractions by approximately 66% (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.19 to 0.64; 1728 participants; 12 studies; low-certainty evidence)... antibiotics may also reduce the risk of dry socket by 34% (RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.97)."
