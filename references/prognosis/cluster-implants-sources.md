# Implants (survival + peri-implant disease) — evidence sources

All verbatim quotes confirmed in PubMed abstract.

## 1. Moraschini et al. 2015 — 10-year overall survival SR
- *Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2015;44(3):377-88.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijom.2014.10.023 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25467739/
- Design: SR of 23 longitudinal studies, 7,711 implants, mean follow-up 13.4 years.
- Verbatim: "All of the studies reported survival rates and mean marginal bone resorption values, with cumulative mean values of 94.6% and 1.3mm, respectively."

## 2. Jung et al. 2012 — single crowns on implants
- *Clin Oral Implants Res* 2012;23 Suppl 6:2-21.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0501.2012.02547.x | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23062124/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 46 studies.
- Verbatim: "survival of implants supporting SCs at 5 years amounted to 97.2% (95% CI: 96.3-97.9%), and at 10 years amounted to 95.2% (95% CI: 91.8-97.2%). The survival of implant-supported SCs was 96.3% (95% CI: 94.2-97.6%) after 5 years and 89.4% (95% CI: 82.8-93.6%) after 10 years."

## 3. Pjetursson et al. 2007 — implant vs tooth-supported FDPs
- *Clin Oral Implants Res* 2007;18 Suppl 3:97-113.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0501.2007.01439.x | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17594374/
- Design: SR, Poisson meta-analysis.
- Verbatim: "estimated 5-year survival of conventional tooth-supported FDPs of 93.8%, cantilever FDPs of 91.4%, solely implant-supported FDPs of 95.2%, combined tooth-implant-supported FDPs of 95.5% and implant-supported SCs of 94.5%... after 10 years of function the estimated survival decreased to 89.2% for conventional FDPs, to 80.3% for cantilever FDPs, to 86.7% for implant-supported FDPs, to 77.8% for combined tooth-implant-supported FDPs and to 89.4% for implant-supported SCs... 38.7% the patients with implant-supported FDPs had some complications after the 5-year observation period. This is compared with 15.7% for conventional FDPs and 20.6% for cantilever FDPs."

## 4. Derks & Tomasi 2015 — peri-implantitis prevalence
- *J Clin Periodontol* 2015;42 Suppl 16:S158-71.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpe.12334 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25495683/
- Verbatim: "The prevalence of peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis ranged from 19 to 65% and from 1 to 47%, respectively. Meta-analyses estimated weighted mean prevalences of peri-implant mucositis and peri-implantitis of 43% (CI: 32-54%) and 22% (CI: 14-30%), respectively. The meta-regression showed a positive relationship between prevalence of peri-implantitis and function time and a negative relationship between prevalence of peri-implantitis and threshold for bone loss."

## 5. Aghaloo & Moy 2007 — hard tissue augmentation
- *Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants* 2007;22 Suppl:49-70.
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18437791/ (no indexed DOI)
- Design: SR, 90 articles, 5,128 sinus-augmented implants + 2,620 ridge-augmented implants.
- Verbatim (abstract): per-technique implant survival — sinus augmentation 81-95.6%; alveolar ridge augmentation 95.5% GBR, 90.4% OVG, 94.7% DO, 83.8% COG. "alveolar ridge augmentation procedures may be more technique- and operator-experience-sensitive, and implant survival may be a function of residual bone supporting the dental implant rather than grafted bone."

## 6. Mello et al. 2017 — immediate vs delayed placement
- *Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg* 2017;46(9):1162-1177.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijom.2017.03.016 | PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28478869/
- Design: SR + meta-analysis, 30 studies, 3,049 implants.
- Verbatim: "The survival rate of delayed implants (98.38%) was significantly greater than immediate implants (95.21%) (p=.001). For the marginal bone loss (p=.32), implant stability quotients values (p=.44), and pocket probing depth (p=.94) there was no significant difference between the analysed groups... The immediate implants placed in fresh sockets should be performed with caution because of the significantly lower survival rates than delayed implants inserted in healed sockets."
