# Diabetes and periodontitis: bidirectional effect and glycaemic impact

**Citation:** Chapple ILC, Genco R, on behalf of Working Group 2 of the Joint EFP/AAP Workshop. Diabetes and periodontal diseases: consensus report of the Joint EFP/AAP Workshop on Periodontitis and Systemic Diseases. *J Clin Periodontol* 2013;40(Suppl 14):S106-S112.

## Verbatim

"Meta-analyses of intervention studies reveal that mechanical periodontal therapy associates with approximately a 0.4% reduction in HbA1C at 3 months, a clinical impact equivalent to adding a second drug to a pharmacological regime for diabetes."

"There is direct and dose-dependent relationship between periodontitis severity and diabetes complications."

## Headline numbers

- Non-surgical periodontal therapy produces about 0.4 percentage-point reduction in HbA1c at 3 months in type 2 diabetics.
- Severe periodontitis independently worsens glycaemic control in a dose-dependent way.
- Effect size increases with periodontitis severity; baseline HbA1c moderates response.

## Population caveats

The diabetes-periodontitis relative-risk literature is historically anchored on Pima-Indian cohorts, which have an unusually high prevalence of type 2 diabetes. Absolute risk figures don't map one-to-one onto UK populations, though the direction and bidirectionality of the effect is well-replicated across Western cohorts.

## Take-home

Concrete numbers for two conversations: with the patient ("treating your gums can help your diabetes as much as another tablet") and with the GP ("shared-care referral is justified"). Document both.
