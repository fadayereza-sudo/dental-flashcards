Mid-face fractures: The mid-facial skeleton is a complex composite of fine fragile bones. Severe trauma can move the entire mid-face downwards and backwards along the base of the skull, lengthening the face and obstructing the airway. Fractures of the cribriform plate of the ethmoids can lead to dural tears and CSF leak.

Retrobulbar haemorrhage: This is an arterial bleed behind the globe following trauma. It presents as a painful, proptosed eye with decreasing visual acuity, and is a surgical emergency. The clot must be decompressed and evacuated. Involve ophthalmology.

Le Fort I fracture detaches the tooth-bearing portion of the jaw. Le Fort II detaches the true mid-face in a pyramidal shape. Le Fort III detaches the entire facial skeleton from the cranial base.

Diagnosis of Le Fort fractures: Grasp the upper jaw between the thumb and forefinger anteriorly, place thumb and forefinger of the other hand over the supraorbital ridges, and attempt to mobilize the upper jaw. Le Fort II and III produce gross oedema of soft tissues, bilateral black eyes (panda facies), subconjunctival haemorrhage, mobile mid-face, dish-face appearance. Look for a CSF leak and assess visual acuity.

Malar (zygoma) fractures: A common and easily missed injury, usually the result of a blow with a blunt object. Diagnosis: Bruising around eye with subconjunctival haemorrhage (unilateral). Diplopia, a step deformity of the orbital rim, sometimes paraesthesia of the infra-orbital nerve, limitation of lateral excursion of the mandible on mouth opening, and unilateral epistaxis. There is often tenderness on palpation of the zygomatic buttress IO.

Orbital floor fractures: Main signs are enophthalmos and diplopia in upwards gaze (inferior rectus). Late enophthalmos caused by fibrosis of herniated periorbital fat. 'Hanging drop sign' on X-ray.

White eye blowout: Seen in children, often a trapdoor blowout fracture with no subconjunctival haemorrhage but severe pain and intractable vomiting. Repair resolves the condition. Rare but one of the few indications for emergency orbital surgery.

Septal haematoma: A comparatively uncommon complication of nasal trauma which demands immediate evacuation as, if ignored, it can lead to septal necrosis.
