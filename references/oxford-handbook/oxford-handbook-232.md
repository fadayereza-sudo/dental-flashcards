ATLS is a system providing one safe way of resuscitating a trauma victim. The principles of basic life support are included during trauma management and can be applied during emergencies in general dental practice.

Trauma deaths have a trimodal distribution. The first peak is within seconds to minutes of injury. The second is within the first hour, the 'golden hour', and is the area of main concern. The third is days to weeks later.

Primary survey uses the mnemonic ABCDE on the basis of identifying and treating the most lethal injury first.

Airway with cervical spine control. Establish a patent airway and protect the cervical spine from further injury.

Breathing and ventilation. Inspect, palpate, and auscultate the chest. Count respiratory rate. Give 100% O2.

Circulation with haemorrhage control. Assess level of consciousness, skin colour, pulse and BP. Establish two large venous cannulae. Give 2L of pre-warmed Hartmann's solution. Tranexamic acid IV in first 3h.

Disability (neuro-evaluation). Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score. Pupillary response to light, visual acuity. Prevent hypotension and hypoxia.

Exposure. Remove all clothing to allow full assessment of injuries. Prevent hypothermia.

Primary management of maxillofacial trauma: The first consideration is whether the patient has suffered polytrauma or trauma confined to the face. Remember that isolated facial injuries rarely cause sufficient bleeding to induce hypovolaemic shock. Bleeding can cause pressure to the optic nerve and can cause blindness. Visual acuity must be checked.

Airway: Most conscious patients can maintain a patent airway if the oropharynx is cleared. Conscious patients with severe isolated facial trauma maintain their airway by sitting leaning forward—let them.

Patients to admit: Any question of danger to the airway, skull fracture, history of unconsciousness, retrograde amnesia, bleeding, middle-third fracture, mandibular fracture (except when very simple), malar fracture if positive eye signs, children, and those with domestic or social problems. If in doubt admit. If not admitting, give the patient a head-injury card.

If teeth have been lost, ensure they are not in the chest (CXR) or soft tissues.
