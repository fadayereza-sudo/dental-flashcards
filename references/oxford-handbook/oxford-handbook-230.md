Anaemia: The nutritional deficiencies associated with anaemia (iron, vitamin B12, and folate) are all associated with recurrent oral ulceration. Specific deficiencies may be present even in the absence of a frank anaemia. Candidosis may be precipitated or exacerbated by anaemia, particularly iron deficiency. Angular cheilitis is a well-recognized association. The sore, clinically normal tongue (burning tongue) is sometimes a manifestation or even precursor of anaemia.

Leukaemia: Associated with decreased resistance to infection leading to candidosis or herpetic infections. Other oral problems include bleeding and petechial haemorrhage (even with minimal trauma), gingival swelling, ulceration, and mucosal pallor. Spontaneous gingival bleeding may be controlled by using impressions as a made-to-measure pressure dressing.

Myeloma: This malignant neoplasm of plasma cells may occasionally cause macroglossia due to amyloid deposition. Classically, multiple osteolytic punched-out lesions in skull and jaws associated with pain, paraesthesia, and pathological fracture. Bisphosphonates form part of these patients' Rx and 10yr survivors have a 30% risk of BRON.

Acromegaly: Due to excess growth hormone. Oral signs include enlargement of the tongue and lips, spacing of the teeth, and an increased jaw size, particularly the mandible resulting in a Class III malocclusion.

Addison's disease: (Adrenocortical hypofunction.) Classically, causes melanotic hyperpigmentation of the buccal mucosa.

Hyperparathyroidism: Rare. Increased parathormone causes increased plasma Ca2+ liberated from bone. Appears in the jaws as loss of lamina dura; a 'ground-glass' appearance of bone and cystic lesions; 'brown tumour' histologically indistinguishable from a giant cell granuloma. Diagnosis confirmed by raised Ca2+, raised PTH, reduced alkaline phosphatase.

Diabetes: No specific oral changes, although manifestations of decreased resistance to infection can be seen if poorly controlled (e.g. severe periodontal disease). Xerostomia and thrush are prominent features of ketoacidosis. Burning mouth may be a presenting feature. There is a tendency to slower healing following surgery. Lichenoid reactions due to oral hypoglycaemic drugs may be seen.
