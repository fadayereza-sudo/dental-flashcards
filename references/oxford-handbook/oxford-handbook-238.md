True ankylosis: Restriction of movement caused by a pathological joint condition, usually due to trauma (intracapsular fracture in childhood) or infection. Extreme limitation of movement and X-rays will confirm degree of bony union. In fibrous union, exercises are of value. In bony union, interpositional arthroplasty or condylectomy with reconstruction is needed.

False ankylosis: Restriction of movement imposed by extra-articular abnormalities is very rare. Trismus (interincisal opening ~<40mm, is often due to limitation of movement due to spasm of muscles of mastication), this may be confused with ankylosis but does not affect the joint. It is much more common and complicates many oral surgical procedures; may follow trauma or infection, may be a manifestation of occult malignancy, or a response to treatment, e.g. radiotherapy.

Dislocation: Occurs in normal joints due to exceptional circumstances, or in lax joints where dislocation is recurrent. May be unilateral or bilateral. Condyle can be palpated anterior to articular eminence, X-rays confirm position, mouth is gagged open. Rx: immediate reduction; the vast majority can be performed with LA around the dislocated joint &/or sedation. Place thumbs over molar teeth and exert downward and backward pressure. Advise jaw support when yawning. In chronically recurring dislocations, patients can be taught to self-reduce.

Rheumatoid, psoriatic, and gouty arthritides all manifest in the TMJ but in <15% of patients with the systemic disease. Signs and symptoms are joint stiffness, pain, tenderness, and crepitus. Rx: treat systemic condition. Symptomatic Rx of joint with appliances, physiotherapy, exercises, NSAIDs and intra-articular steroids.

Osteoarthrosis: A distinct entity of the TMJ, with a different clinical course from that seen in other joints. Appears to be a degenerative condition. Diagnosis: crepitus, limitation, and pain on movement, tenderness over condyle, often with X-ray evidence of condylar erosions. Most have symptoms for ~1yr which gradually decrease over the next 2yrs. X-rays show condylar remodelling to a flat smooth surface. Rx is aimed at pain relief using standard TMD measures.
