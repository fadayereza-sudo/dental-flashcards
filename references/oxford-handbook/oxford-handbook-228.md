AIDS is the terminal stage of infection with the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). The underlying severe immunodeficiency leads to a number of oral manifestations (75% have orofacial disease) which, although not pathognomonic, should raise the possibility of HIV infection.

Group I—strongly associated with HIV:

Candidosis: Seen in 70% of HIV patients as early manifestation. Erythematous (early), hyperplastic, pseudomembranous (late), and angular cheilitis in young people (most common oral feature of HIV). 50% of patients with HIV-associated thrush develop AIDS in 5yrs.

Hairy leucoplakia: Bilateral, white, non-removable corrugated lesions of the tongue, unaffected by antifungals but usually resolve with aciclovir. Associated with EBV. It is a predictor of a bad prognosis and possible development of lymphoma.

HIV gingivitis: Unusually severe gingivitis for relatively good OH. Often characterized by linear gingival erythema, an intense red band along the gingival margin.

Necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis: Occurs in young, otherwise healthy mouths.

HIV periodontitis: Severe localized destruction out of place with OH.

Kaposi's sarcoma (KS): Commonest malignancy among HIV patients. One or more erythematous/purplish macules or swelling, frequently on the palate. Pathognomonic if seen in a young male who is not receiving immunosuppression.

Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma: Also common. Burkitt's lymphoma is 1000 times more common than in HIV-negative persons.

Dental Rx for patients with AIDS: This group present two risks:

Risk of transmission to personnel (risk from needlestick injury from an infected patient is 0.3–0.45%). Routine cross-infection control measures are necessary in all patients.

As these patients are immunocompromised, any Rx with a known risk of infective complications, e.g. extractions, should be covered with antiseptic and antimicrobial prophylaxis, and any surgery should be as atraumatic as possible.

Prophylaxis after contaminated needlestick injury should be given within 24h. HAART offers the best chance of preventing HIV seroconversion.
