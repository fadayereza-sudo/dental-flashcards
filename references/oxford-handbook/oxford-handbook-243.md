There are three surgical aids to endodontics: apicectomy, root hemisection, and removal of extruded endodontic paste.

Apicectomy: This is by far the commonest Rx. It is a second-line Rx after failure of, or as a supplement to, orthograde endodontics (which has an 86–96% success rate in expert hands).

Indications: Impossible to prepare and fill apical one-third of tooth (e.g. pulpal calcification, curved apex, open apex). Irretrievable broken instrument in canal. Post crown on tooth with apical pathology (only if post crown has sealed the coronal root and crown margin adequately). Root perforation. Fractured and infected apical one-third. Persistent infection due to apical cyst or other lesion requiring biopsy, and repeat root canal therapy has been performed and is adequate. A coronal seal must be achievable.

Assessment: IO X-ray, best possible root filling in situ, free from acute infection, and crown sealed with good quality restoration.

Remember—non-surgical Rx: 72% success; apicectomy and retrograde root filling: 60%, apicectomy no retrograde root filling: 51%.

Special points in apicectomy: Warn patients about post-operative swelling. Lower incisors present an access problem. Think twice about the mental nerve in lower premolar apicectomies. Think hard about alternatives to apicectomy of 6. Don't think at all about 7, 8. Remember the buccal and palatal roots in 4; section the buccal root low to see the palatal. Apicectomy of 6 is fine provided the palatal root can be treated by an orthograde approach, is hemisected, or you are happy to deal with breaching the antrum.

Single-rooted tooth apicectomies should succeed 58–96% of the time. Multirooted teeth, revision apicectomy, and perforation repair have a much lower success rate.
