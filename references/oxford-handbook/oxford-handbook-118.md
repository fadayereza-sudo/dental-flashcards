Removable partial dentures—principles

Definitions

Saddle: That part of a denture which rests on and covers the edentulous areas and carries the artificial teeth and gumwork.
Connector (major and minor): Joins together component parts of a denture.
Support: Resistance to vertical forces directed towards mucosa.
Retainers: Components which resist displacement of denture.
Indirect retention: Resistance to rotation about fulcrum axis by acting on the opposite side to the displacing force.
Fulcrum axis: Axis around which a tooth- and mucosa-borne denture tends to rock when saddles are loaded.
Bracing: Resistance to lateral movement.
Reciprocation: The mechanism by which lateral forces generated by a retentive clasp passing over a height of contour are counterbalanced by a reciprocal clasp passing along a reciprocal guiding plane.
Guide planes: Two or more parallel surfaces on abutment teeth used to limit the path of insertion, and improve retention and stability.
Survey line: This indicates the maximum bulbosity of a tooth in the plane of the path of withdrawal.
Free-end saddle: Edentulous area posterior to the natural teeth.

Kennedy Classification (describes the pattern of tooth loss):
- Class I: Bilateral free-end saddles.
- Class II: Unilateral free-end saddle.
- Class III: Unilateral bounded saddle.
- Class IV: Single, anterior bilateral bounded saddle. Must cross the midline.
Any additional saddles are referred to as modifications (except Class IV).

Craddock Classification (describes the denture type):
- Tooth-borne.
- Mucosa-borne.
- Mucosa- and tooth-borne.

Acrylic versus metal dentures

Approximately 75% of the dentures provided in the UK have an acrylic connector and base. Although metal bases are generally preferred because the greater strength of metal permits a more hygienic design, an acrylic base is indicated for:
- Temporary replacement, e.g. following trauma or in children.
- Where there is inadequate support from the remaining teeth for a tooth-borne denture.
- When additions to the denture are likely in the near future.

Where financial constraints C/I a metal base, attention to the following may avoid the production of a gum-stripper:
- Wide mucosal coverage to provide maximum support.
- Keep base clear of the gingival margins wherever possible.
- No interdental extensions of acrylic.
- Point contact and wide embrasures between natural and artificial teeth.
- Labial flanges for extra retention and bracing.
- Additional support from wrought SS rests.

Clasps provide direct retention by engaging the undercut portion of a tooth. The action of a clasp must be resisted either by a non-retentive clasp arm above the maximum bulbosity of the tooth or by a reciprocal connector. A minimum length of 14mm is generally advocated for cobalt–chrome clasps to prevent rapid distortion.

Depth of undercut for clasp selection:
- 0.25mm—cast cobalt–chrome
- 0.5mm—SS wire
- <0.75mm—wrought gold

Lower connectors:
- Lingual bar should only be used if there is >7mm between floor of mouth and gingival margin to give 3mm clearance from gingivae. C/I if incisors are retroclined.
- Lingual plate is well tolerated and provides good support, bracing, and indirect retention if used in conjunction with rests but covers gingival margins.
- Buccal/labial bar is indicated when the lower incisors are retroclined.
