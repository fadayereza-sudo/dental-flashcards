Root canal treatment—instruments

Files

Stainless steel files:
- K-type files (K-File™, K-Flex™, K-Flexofile™): Usually used in a watch-winding action or with balanced force.
- Hedström file: More aggressive than K-type files. Use with push–pull filing motion. Must never be used with a rotary motion as this can ↑ torsional forces and ↑ chances of fracture.

Nickel titanium (NiTi) files: Much more flexible, even with ↑ diameters. Benefits include ↓ preparation time, ↓ files required, and ↓ operator and patient fatigue. Unlike conventional hand files which have a constant taper of 0.02 (2%), they have a range of tapers (0.05–0.12). NiTi files are vulnerable to fracture due to cyclic fatigue and torsional stress. They should be used with care in very curved canals. They are designed to be used following the production of a negotiable glide path created by small hand files. Initial hand preparation to at least a size no. 10 ISO instrument is always required. Frequent irrigation is important and recapitulation with a small hand file between each rotary file helps maintain a glide path. A light touch and slow speed is essential.

NiTi files for use in a motor with reciprocating motion are now commonplace (e.g. WaveOne™, Reciproc®). The main advantage is that the reciprocating action reduces torsional forces and a single NiTi file is used in the majority of cases.

The single use of endodontic files was advised by the Chief Dental Officer in the United Kingdom due to the theoretical risk of vCJD transmission.

Root canal treatment—materials

Irrigants: Dilute sodium hypochlorite is generally considered to be the best irrigant as it is bactericidal and dissolves organic debris. Normal concentration is 2.5% available chlorine. Chelating agents (e.g. EDTA paste, File-Eze®) are particularly helpful when trying to negotiate sclerosed or blocked canals.

Canal medication: Non-setting calcium hydroxide paste (Hypocal™, Ultracal®) is used as an inter-appointment medicament. It can be very effective in treating an infected canal where there is a persistent inflammatory exudate from the periapical tissues.

Antibiotic/steroid paste (Odontopaste®): Useful if anaesthesia of a hyperaemic pulp is not successful. Dressing with Odontopaste® ↓ inflammation and may allow pulp extirpation under LA during the next visit. It contains calcium hydroxide, clindamycin hydrochloride, and triamcinolone acetonide.

Filling material: GP (gutta-percha), an isomer of latex, comes the nearest to meeting the requirements of an ideal filling material. Supplied in cones: master cones sized to match the master apical file, and accessory cones sized to match the finger spreaders.

Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA): A calcium silicate cement, biocompatible and can set in the presence of moisture. Material of choice in apexification procedures. Can be used as a pulp-capping agent. Creates a physical barrier and releases calcium hydroxide when setting.
