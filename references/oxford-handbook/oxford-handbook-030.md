Palatally displaced maxillary canines

Early detection is essential.

Prevalence

Up to 2%. Occurs bilaterally in 17–25% of cases. F > M.

Aetiology

In normal development, the maxillary canine develops palatal to C and then migrates labially to erupt down the distal aspect of the root of 2. The aetiology of palatal displacement is not fully understood, but some suggest a lack of guidance is the reason behind the association with missing or short-rooted 2 (~6% of palatal 3 associated with small 2). Others argue that it is an inherited polygenic trait and that the link with missing or short-rooted 2 is part of an association with other dental anomalies including microdontia and hypodontia.

Assessment

Clinically by palpation and from inclination of 2; and by radiographs. A DPT and an IO view or two IO views with tube shift can be used to assess the position of the canine by parallax. Consider also position and prognosis of adjacent teeth (including C), the malocclusion, and available space.
