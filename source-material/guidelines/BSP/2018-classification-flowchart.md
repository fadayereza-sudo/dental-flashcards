# BSP — Implementing the 2018 Classification of Periodontal Diseases to Reach a Diagnosis in Clinical Practice (2024)

Source: `BSP_Flowchart_Implementing_the_2018_Classification.pdf` (visual flowchart, 2 pages).
Transcribed here as text for downstream extraction agents that cannot read the PDF visually.

---

## Page 1 — Diagnostic pathway

**Entry**: History, examination and screening for periodontal disease — including BPE and assessment of historic periodontitis (interdental recession).

The pathway then branches by **highest BPE code**:

### Branch A — Code 0 / 1 / 2 with no obvious evidence of interdental recession

Decide by bleeding on probing percentage:
- `<10% BoP` → **Clinical Gingival Health**
- `10–30% BoP` → **Localised Gingivitis**
- `>30% BoP` → **Generalised Gingivitis**

Footnote: Diagnosis should also include a comment on plaque retentive factors where a BPE code 2 is present.

### Branch B — Code 3 with no obvious evidence of interdental recession

1. Appropriate radiographic assessment
2. Initial periodontal therapy and review in 3 months with localised 6-point pocket chart in involved sextant(s)
3. Re-evaluate at 3 months:
   - **No pockets ≥4mm and no radiographic evidence of bone loss due to periodontitis** → continue with code 0/1/2 pathway
   - **Pockets ≥4mm remain and/or radiographic evidence of bone loss due to periodontitis** → continue with code 4 pathway

### Branch C — Code 4 and/or obvious evidence of interdental recession

1. Appropriate radiographic assessment
2. Full periodontal assessment (including detailed 6-point pocket chart)
3. Distribution pattern decides the type:
   - **Molar–incisor pattern** → Periodontitis: Molar–Incisor Pattern
   - **<30% of teeth** → Localised Periodontitis
   - **≥30% of teeth** → Generalised Periodontitis
4. Continue to staging and grading, current disease status, and risk factor assessment (page 2)

## Page 2 — Staging, grading, status, risk factors

### Staging (severity)

Radiographic assessment using periapicals or OPG/DPT (if not clinically justified or only bitewings available, use CAL or bone loss from CEJ).

Use **interproximal bone loss at the worst site** of bone loss due to periodontitis:
- `<15%` (or `<2mm` attachment loss from CEJ) → **Stage I (Early/Mild)**
- Coronal third of root → **Stage II (Moderate)**
- Mid third of root → **Stage III (Severe)**
- Apical third of root → **Stage IV (Very Severe)**

### Grading (rate of progression)

Use **% bone loss ÷ patient age** at the worst site of bone loss due to periodontitis:
- `<0.5` → **Grade A (slow rate of progression)**
- `0.5–1.0` → **Grade B (moderate rate of progression)**
- `>1.0` → **Grade C (rapid rate of progression)**

### Assessment of current periodontitis status

- **Currently Stable** — BoP <10%, PPD ≤4mm, No BoP at 4mm sites
- **Currently in Remission** — BoP ≥10%, PPD ≤4mm, No BoP at 4mm sites
- **Currently Unstable** — PPD ≥5mm or PPD ≥4mm & BoP

### Risk factor assessment

For example:
- Smoking, including cigarettes/day
- Sub-optimally controlled diabetes

### Diagnosis statement

Format: **Extent – Periodontitis – Stage – Grade – Stability – Risk Factors**

Example: *Generalised Periodontitis Stage 3 Grade B – Currently Unstable – Risk(s): Smoker 15/day*
